# Staker: WG21 (ISO/IEC JTC1/SC22/WG21)

**A corporate-funded, member-run C++ standards body whose officers, funders, and founders are largely the same circle.**

WG21 is the accredited ISO/IEC working group that authors and maintains the C++ standard, a corporate-funded volunteer committee whose governance runs through national-body ballots and a by-invitation leadership.

July 2026, by Vinnie Falco


---

## 1. Executive Summary

**Key Judgments**

1. A self-authored, unratified rulebook lets a stable inner circle appoint the committee's chairs and, over time, its own successors. (medium-high)
2. Money, chairs, and a self-electing board sit in the same hands, converting non-voting funding into control the members never granted. (medium-high)
3. Sealed reasoning and absent outcome audits manufacture consensus and let conflicted experts act unseen by the developers they serve. (medium)
4. The firms that fund and staff the committee capture the features that ship and steer what it works on next. (medium)
5. The millions who bear the cost of C++ hold no governance seat, and their proxies cannot be recalled. (medium-high)
6. The committee runs on a platform, a corpus, and volunteers it neither owns nor can replace. (medium-high)
7. Attention flows to employer features while the safety story stays unshipped and the regulatory deadline runs. (medium)
8. The committee requires no proof of implementation, so implementers and vendors set the real C++ standard. (medium-high)
9. A silent installed base has frozen the language in place, and the stakeholders who can leave are leaving while the captive base cannot. (medium)
10. Knowledge concentrates in an aging founding cohort while the safety coalition sits one defection from collapse. (low-medium)

WG21 holds monopoly authority over ISO/IEC 14882, the sole international standard for C++, a language used by roughly 16 million developers. No competing standard exists, and ISO designation confers a procurement legitimacy no informal specification matches. That monopoly makes the committee's governance consequential at industrial scale, while the committee itself has no budget or staff and its principal support entity, the Standard C++ Foundation, reported about 2.1 million dollars in revenue for the year ending June 2025.

The dominant dynamic is a capture spine. A self-electing Foundation board that doubles as the committee's leadership converts the money and chair-holdings of non-voting corporate funders into effective control of the standard's direction. The same parties fund the body, staff and chair the rule-writing groups, and set the technical priorities, closing a loop no member vote authorizes and no external body reviews. The conversion is over-determined, holding whether or not anyone intends it, so the absence of provable intent describes the system rather than clearing it. (medium-high)

The stated beneficiary and the actual beneficiary diverge. WG21 presents as a commonweal project, a vendor-neutral interoperability standard for the developer base and the public exposed to C++ software. By the Blau-Scott prime-beneficiary test it is a mutual-benefit association, corroborated by the Foundation's 501(c)(6) form, organized to improve business conditions for C++ developers. Steering, staffing, and discretionary money accrue to the chair-holding and funding firms and the incumbent leadership, while the diffuse base that is the stated beneficiary bears the diffuse cost and pays ISO to read the result. The commonweal is the front; the mutual benefit is the operating reality.

The trajectory is degrading where the committee meets the outside world. The funder layer is widening as finance firms move toward load-bearing status, high-value contributors are exiting, and two safety efforts have failed to ship as the regulatory clock runs, with CISA roadmap expectations and European Union Cyber Resilience Act obligations arriving from September 2026. Of the findings carrying discoverable trend evidence, 21 are degrading, 51 are stable, and 5 are improving. The stable majority describes structural features, oligarchic control, consensus culture, and funding dependency, that will not move without an external shock, and the degrading minority clusters exactly where external pressure meets internal entrenchment.

## 2. The Organization

WG21 is ISO/IEC JTC1/SC22/WG21, the working group of accredited experts from ISO/IEC member nations formed in 1990-91 to standardize C++ ([The Committee - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/std/the-committee)). The project record shows a United States New Work Item request in 1989, project approval in 1991, a first meeting on 18-19 June 1991 held jointly with the US committee J16, and publication of the first International Standard as ISO/IEC 14882:1998 ([C++ Projects - open-std.org](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/projects)). The group sits under SC22, the subcommittee created in 1985 for programming-language standardization, whose international secretariat ANSI holds in the United States ([ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 22 - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO/IEC_JTC_1/SC_22)). WG21 carries no separate legal personality and revises the standard on authority delegated from above rather than owned. (medium-high)

The 2025 business plan describes three hybrid meetings a year, experts regularly present from 25 national bodies, nearly 30 active domain-specific subgroups, and a three-year revision cadence for ISO/IEC 14882 ([Business Plan and Convener's Report N5019 - open-std.org](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/n5019.pdf)). A volunteer public mirror counts more than 20 nations, 15 active study groups, and 388 papers across releases from C++98 to the C++26 working draft ([wg21.org](https://wg21.org/)). Membership routes through national bodies, or through the US body INCITS which any individual may join, and each body is sovereign over whom it sends ([Meetings and Participation - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/std/meetings-and-participation/)). Members deliberate as individual experts, while national bodies cast the votes ([FGA: The gen on the BSI C++ Committee - jdebp.uk](https://jdebp.uk/FGA/cxxpanel.html)). Deliberation and franchise sit in different hands by design.

WG21's stated scope is "Development and maintenance of ISO/IEC Standards, Technical Specifications, and Technical Reports related to the programming language C++" (Business Plan and Convener's Report N5019). Its parent subcommittee's mission is to improve "portability of applications, productivity and mobility of programmers, and compatibility of applications over time" (ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 22, Wikipedia). Both statements define the work by its artifact and its users in the abstract. Neither names a governing beneficiary, which leaves the question of whom the committee answers to be settled by structure rather than charter. (medium-high)

WG21 runs as a three-stage pipeline, each subgroup led by a chair acting with the convener's authority (The Committee, isocpp.org):

- Study Groups incubate domain-specific proposals until they are mature enough to enter design ([SD-3 Study Group Organizational Information - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-3-study-group-organizational-information)).
- Evolution (EWG) and Library Evolution (LEWG) own feature design and the choice of ship vehicle ([SD-4 WG21 Practices and Procedures - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-4-wg21-practices-and-procedures)).
- Core (CWG) and Library (LWG) own the normative wording (SD-4).
- A closing plenary decides whether consensus exists, as determined by the convener, to adopt each recommended change (SD-4).

Every seat in this pipeline is filled from above, by appointment, not from below, by election.

The convener determines consensus, chairs the group, sets the schedule, appoints Study Groups, and answers upward to SC22, JTC1, and ITTF (The Committee, isocpp.org). The current officers are Convener Guy Davidson, Vice-Conveners Nina Ranns and Jeff Garland, and Secretary Braden Ganetsky (same source). A 2025 transition changed the chair after a long tenure. Herb Sutter served as convener across 2002-2008 and 2009-2025 and as secretary from 1998 to 2002, presiding over every ISO C++ specification except C++98 ([Standard C++ FAQ WG21 - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/wiki/faq/wg21)). His term expired at the end of 2025, and he continues as convener emeritus while remaining chair and chief executive of the Standard C++ Foundation ([A new convenor team and secretary - herbsutter.com](https://herbsutter.com/2025/11/)). Committee minutes confirm Davidson as the new convener leading a three-person team ([WG21 2025-10 Kona Admin telecon minutes N5029 - open-std.org](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/n5029.pdf)). The seat changed hands under an SC22 rule that assigns a vacant convenership to the national body holding the fewest such roles, which favored the UK nominee over a crowded US field ([The Shape of Code, convenor - shape-of-code.com](https://shape-of-code.com/tag/convenor/)). One office determines consensus, sets the agenda, and appoints the subgroups, and the recent handover moved the title while keeping its long-time holder adjacent to it. (medium-high)

An informal but standing body sets priorities. The Direction Group is described as a small, by-invitation group of experienced participants, recorded in SD-4 as Howard Hinnant, Roger Orr, Bjarne Stroustrup, Daveed Vandevoorde, and Michael Wong (SD-4 WG21 Practices and Procedures). The same procedures note ISO guidance the group adopted, which bars AI-generated papers while allowing AI as a copy-editing aid (WG21 2025-10 Kona Admin telecon minutes N5029). Priorities thus begin in a seat filled by invitation, layered above chairs filled by appointment, so both mechanisms that steer the work sit outside any membership vote.

WG21 levies no fees on the international group, and its meetings rest on corporate sponsors rather than the bodies that vote ([SD-5 Meeting Information - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-5-meeting-information)). National-body sponsorship can be pro forma, the financial burden is almost always assumed by corporate sponsors, hosting a meeting costs USD 25,000 to 40,000, and ISO rules bar a mandatory facilities charge on attendees (SD-5). The US body INCITS funds itself through annual participation fees, USD 2,703 per technical committee or study group in 2026, reduced to 1,530 and 510 for small and academic members, with executive-board fees ranging up to 47,840 ([Membership Info - incits.org](https://www.incits.org/participation/membership-info); [Fees for Participation on INCITS Technical Committees - incits.org](https://www.incits.org/dotAsset/2f813690-f5b9-455d-8a94-5afb6435609f.pdf); [Fees for Participation on the INCITS Executive Board 2026 - incits.org](https://www.incits.org/dotAsset/c9027f72-5ff0-4686-a8f3-24c85417e630.pdf)). The UK body by contrast makes its C++ membership voluntary and gratis (FGA, jdebp.uk). The money that runs the committee enters through sponsors, national-body fees, and a support foundation, and none of those channels is the individual expert who argues a proposal on the floor. (medium-high)

Two nonprofits carry the committee's money and much of its leadership. The Standard C++ Foundation is a Washington 501(c)(6) trade association founded in 2012 by fifteen organizations including Microsoft, Google, Intel, IBM, Bloomberg, and DRW Trading Group, and it funds WG21 meeting hosting, travel assistance, and proposal grants while operating isocpp.org and the CppCon conference ([November CTP, isocpp.org, Standard C++ Foundations Announced Today - devblogs.microsoft.com](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/cppblog/november-ctp-isocpp-org-standard-c-foundations-announced-today/); [About - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/about)). Its membership is non-voting and tiered at Gold, Silver, and Bronze levels of $10,000, $5,000, and $1,000 a year ([Membership - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/about/membership)). Its officers are Herb Sutter as chairman and president, Bjarne Stroustrup as treasurer, Nina Ranns as vice-president, Inbal Levi as secretary, and Michael Wong (About, isocpp.org). The overlap with WG21 is direct: Sutter is Foundation president and convener emeritus, Stroustrup is treasurer and a Direction Group member, Ranns is vice-president and a WG21 vice-convener, and Wong is a Foundation director and a Direction Group member. Funding and governance are not arm's length. They run through the same names. (medium-high)

A second entity, the C++ Alliance, is a California 501(c)(3) public charity formed in 2017 with IRS recognition in 2019 and led by founder and executive director Vinnie Falco ([Bylaws - cppalliance.org](https://cppalliance.org/pdf/Bylaws.pdf); [The History of Boost Governance - vinniefalco.com](https://www.vinniefalco.com/p/the-history-of-boost-governance)). Its stated mission includes "supporting the work of the ISO Standard Committee by improving the core language and standard library," and it employs full-time staff engineers and funds the tooling behind the public WG21 mailing mirror (Bylaws, cppalliance.org; wg21.org). The two nonprofits overlap in mission but differ in tax form, a 501(c)(6) trade group beside a 501(c)(3) charity ([The C++ Alliance - cppalliance.org](https://cppalliance.org/)). Two separately governed nonprofits thus feed one committee from two tax categories.

Under the Blau-Scott prime-beneficiary test (Blau and Scott 1962), WG21 is a mutual-benefit association. Its members are national bodies and their accredited corporate and individual delegates, who both shape the standard and consume it, and membership routes through bodies each sovereign over its representation (Meetings and Participation, isocpp.org). The standard that ISO sells to the public carries commonweal characteristics, yet the governing apparatus answers to members, not to the public that buys the result. The legal form of the committee's principal funder points the same way, a 501(c)(6) organized "for the improvement of business conditions for C++ software developers" ([Standard Cpp Foundation - Nonprofit Explorer, ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/461356355)). The beneficiary test splits, the artifact serving a broad public while the governance serves the members, and that split governs every beneficiary question below. (medium-high)

The Foundation's public filings describe a small, donation-funded balance sheet. In fiscal 2025 it reported revenue of $2,102,990, expenses of $1,570,703, and net assets of $936,950, with executive compensation of $40,650 paid to president Herb Sutter (Standard Cpp Foundation, ProPublica). Across every filed year all revenue is classed as contributions, total liabilities stand at zero, and Sutter is the only officer recorded with any compensation (same source). The output itself is priced per unit, the current edition ISO/IEC 14882:2024 running 2,104 pages and selling for CHF 227 as a single-user electronic copy ([ISO/IEC 14882:2024 - webstore.iec.ch](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/102625)). A low seven-figure budget, zero liabilities, and a single compensated officer describe an economic engine that is modest in scale and concentrated at one desk. (medium-high)

### Scope

**The decision.** This assessment serves stakeholders deciding how to engage WG21's governance, by mapping who steers the committee, who benefits from its output, and who bears its cost without a vote.

The diagnosis rests on a few assumptions:

- Public records - ISO and committee documents, national-body fee schedules, IRS filings, and vendor disclosures - can be read at face value.
- The documented overlap between WG21 offices and the Standard C++ Foundation holds through the 2025-26 convenership transition.
- Structural position, not the conduct of any individual, is the unit of analysis.

Out of scope are the technical merit of the C++ standard, the legality or good faith of any actor, individual competence, whether WG21 should exist, and any investment judgment.

## 3. The Landscape

### Market position

WG21 authors and maintains ISO/IEC 14882 and sits under SC22, the JTC1 subcommittee for programming languages, often called the portability subcommittee ([ISO/IEC JTC1/SC22 - open-std.org](https://www.open-std.org/JTC1/sc22/)). Within SC22 it is one of only eight active working groups, alongside the groups for C, Fortran, Ada, COBOL, Prolog, programming-language vulnerabilities, and Linux, while sixteen former groups for languages such as Pascal, ALGOL, and BASIC have been disbanded (same SC22 record). SC22 also carries external liaisons including MISRA, Ada-Europe, and ACM SIGPLAN-HILT ([ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 22 - iso.org](https://www.iso.org/committee/45202.html)). By attendance and output WG21 is by far the largest and most active survivor, which means it also carries the maintenance load that outlived most of its peers. (medium)

By developer population C++ ranks fourth among languages, at roughly 16.3 million developers in early 2025, up from about 9.5 million three years earlier, behind JavaScript, Java, and Python and ahead of C and Rust ([Sutter's BeCPP keynote: C++ added more developers - wro.cpp](https://wrocpp.github.io/posts/cpp-fastest-growing-2025/); [Bjarne Stroustrup on C++ developer numbers - LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bjarnestroustrup_there-are-472-million-developers-in-the-activity-7400914240313917440-TH7T)). Independent indices agree on its standing, with TIOBE ranking C++ third at 9.12 percent in July 2026, Stack Overflow's 2025 survey finding 23.5 percent of respondents using it, and JetBrains' 2024 survey finding 27 percent had used it in the prior year ([TIOBE Index](https://www.tiobe.com/tiobe-index/); [Technology 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology/); [State of Developer Ecosystem 2024 - JetBrains](https://www.jetbrains.com/lp/devecosystem-2024/)). The user base is large and still growing, which raises the stakes of every design decision the committee makes.

WG21 ships a new International Standard on a three-year cadence, from C++98 through C++23 with C++26 in working draft, and runs experimental work in parallel as Technical Specifications (Business Plan and Convener's Report N5019; wg21.org). Participation has climbed steeply, from average meeting attendance near 67 experts across 2004-2016 to 204 attendees from 24 national bodies at the March 2026 Croydon meeting, while the subgroup count grew from 16 in 2012 to nearly 30 by 2025 ([Business Plan and Convener's Report n4960 - open-std.org](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2023/n4960.pdf); [WG21 2026-03 Croydon Hybrid Meeting Minutes - open-std.org](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/n5040.pdf)). In the single Croydon week EWG processed 70 agenda items and LWG ran 38 polls (same minutes). Workload has outrun a body that still meets three times a year and decides by consensus. (medium-high)

### Ecosystem dependencies

WG21's authority runs upward while its value runs outward, and it controls neither end. Procedural authority flows through SC22, JTC1, and ISO/IEC, and the group revises IS 14882 on ISO's ballot cadence (Business Plan and Convener's Report N5019). More than 20 participating national bodies send the experts and cast the ballots, 24 of them at Croydon, each sovereign over its delegates (SD-4 WG21 Practices and Procedures; WG21 2026-03 Croydon Hybrid Meeting Minutes). The standard's practical worth then rests on three independent implementations shipping it, GCC with libstdc++, Clang and LLVM with libc++, and Microsoft's MSVC with its STL, whose conformance to C++20, 23, and 26 remains partial ([cpp compiler support vendors - cppreference.com](https://cppreference.com/cpp/compiler_support/vendors); [Clang C++ Programming Language Status - clang.llvm.org](https://clang.llvm.org/cxx_status); [microsoft/STL - GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/STL)). A committee that writes text it cannot itself compile depends on vendors it cannot direct. (medium-high)

Beyond its parent bodies and its compilers, the committee depends on a private funder for the means to convene. The Standard C++ Foundation underwrites meeting hosting, travel assistance for attendees in need, and grants to advance proposals, under a policy administered with the subgroup chairs ([Financial assistance policy for ISO C++ meetings - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/blog/2015/05/financial-assistance-policy)). A platform, a travel budget, and a conference the working group does not own are dependencies as real as its compilers.

### Domain-specific vulnerabilities

The dominant pressure on the C++ domain is the government-and-industry push toward memory-safe languages. NSA guidance in 2022 and 2023 and CISA's 2023 "The Case for Memory Safe Roadmaps" classify C and C++ as memory-unsafe and urge migration ([CSI Software Memory Safety v1.1 - media.defense.gov](https://media.defense.gov/2023/Apr/27/2003210083/-1/-1/0/CSI_SOFTWARE_MEMORY_SAFETY_V1.1.PDF); [The Case for Memory Safe Roadmaps - ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov/CSA/2023/231206.pdf)). A 2025 NSA and CISA update restates the direction, and CISA lists memory-unsafe languages for new critical-infrastructure code as a bad practice, expecting manufacturers to publish memory-safety roadmaps by the end of 2025 ([CSI Memory Safe Languages Reducing Vulnerabilities - media.defense.gov](https://media.defense.gov/2025/Jun/23/2003742198/-1/-1/0/CSI_MEMORY_SAFE_LANGUAGES_REDUCING_VULNERABILITIES_IN_MODERN_SOFTWARE_DEVELOPMENT.PDF)). A regulator-set deadline is a clock that a three-year release cadence cannot easily match. (medium-high)

The same pressure elevates one competitor above the rest. Rust is positioned as the one production-ready memory-safe systems language that runs without a garbage collector, adopted for performance-critical work by Google, Microsoft, Amazon, and both the Linux and Windows kernels ([Memory-Unsafe Code Is a Liability - corrode.dev](https://corrode.dev/blog/memory-safety/)). Its developer base of about 5.1 million is far smaller than C++'s, but it grew fastest in percentage terms over the same period (Sutter's BeCPP keynote, wro.cpp). C++ competes on an installed base and a performance niche, while its rival competes on a safety property that regulators now favor. The competitive axis has shifted from speed to safety, and C++ does not yet own the new axis. (medium-high)

How C++ is governed shapes how fast it can answer that pressure. Comparable languages run lighter, project-owned governance, Python by a five-person elected Steering Council under the PEP process and Rust by a Leadership Council and subteams under the RFC process ([PEP 13 Python governance - GitHub](https://github.com/python/peps/blob/main/peps/pep-0013.rst); [RFC 1068 Rust governance - GitHub](https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs/blob/HEAD/text/1068-rust-governance.md)). C++ alone among major languages is governed inside a formal international standards body, with national-body voting and ISO balloting rather than a project-owned council. Formal process buys legitimacy and latency in the same purchase. (medium-high)

A quieter vulnerability sits in the committee's own bench. Of 374 attendees recorded across 2004-2016, 47 percent appeared only once, and the estimated half-life of attendance was 9 to 10 years ([Survival rate of WG21 meeting attendance - The Shape of Code](https://shape-of-code.com/2020/12/13/survival-rate-of-wg21-meeting-attendance/)). Rising totals therefore mask heavy turnover at the edges around a durable core. Knowledge concentrates in the few who stay, a strength for continuity and a fragility for succession. (medium)

## 4. The Self-Ratifying Core: a self-authored, unratified rulebook lets a stable inner circle appoint itself and its successors.

A self-authored, unratified rulebook lets a stable inner circle appoint the committee's chairs and, over time, its own successors. (medium-high) The rulebook is SD-4, WG21's practices-and-procedures document, and the office it empowers is the office that wrote it. No plenary vote ever ratified it. Under it, a single convener determines consensus, appoints every subgroup chair and study group, and sets the schedule. Normally the governed adopt the rules before an office acts on them. Here the sequence is reversed.

By the standard measure of decision power (Dahl 1957), the convener is the committee's single decision center, and appointment is the lever that turns that office into a self-renewing core. The power to name every chair also seeds the by-invitation Direction Group, which pre-settles the priorities the plenary then ratifies. Entry at the base is open to any organization that pays, while the decisions sit behind an appointed, invitation-only layer. Open where it is powerless, closed where it counts. (medium-high)

The governance instrument is the anomaly, not the concentration of office. A single consensus-caller is the ISO baseline across sibling committees, where the C convener served 18 years, so long tenure alone diagnoses nothing. What has no peer analog is a practices document that fixes the convener's own powers, [written personally by the long-tenured predecessor](https://lists.isocpp.org/admin/2017/12/0430.php) and [maintained by that office unilaterally since](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2026/06/27122.php), never ratified by the membership. That predecessor held the seat about 23 years by the committee's own record, and chairs [serve at the convener's pleasure](https://lists.isocpp.org/all/2026/03/0477.php). The baseline explains the tenure. It does not explain the unratified rulebook.

Two features keep the core stable, and the first is structural overlap. A small active minority accreting agenda control is the ordinary condition of any volunteer body, so the iron law of oligarchy (Michels 1911) is a weak charge on its own. The sharper fact is that a five-person Foundation board doubles as the committee's senior leadership, so the body positioned to check the leaders is composed of them. Agenda flows from a standing priority list, and succession flows from an appointment power the same circle holds. Control of the reasoning record belongs to the Sealed Room. (medium)

The second feature is founder centrality. The language's creator remains at the center through the Direction Group and continues to author safety proposals, so a founder's syndrome (Block and Rosenberg 2002) reading survives even though a planned handover to a new convener did occur. Stroustrup chaired evolution for 24 years and still shapes the safety agenda from inside the priority-setting body. Founder centrality is expected for the peer class, which is why the finding sits at reduced confidence rather than as an anomaly. The handover moved the office. It did not move the center of gravity. The split between role and influence is the subject of the Hollowing Bench. (low-medium)

The base of participation is porous, and the porousness is ISO's own design. Broad entry is a stated value, and national-body sovereignty over who represents a country is standard practice, with access models ranging from INCITS pay-to-join to the BSI panel's voluntary, gratis route. The clearly-defined-boundaries condition (Ostrom 1990) still fails on the record: the committee was found to have ["essentially zero power to limit participation"](https://patricia.no/2023/01/09/open_letter_guest_post.html) when a convicted individual joined through a sponsoring member, and access has [widened further as one member funds guest attendance](https://cppalliance.org/company/2026/03/27/WG21-Meeting-Support-Statement.html). Open entry at the powerless layer is not the contradiction of a closed core. It is its complement. (medium)

The closed end of the same design is a body that decides before the plenary votes. The by-invitation Direction Group holds a standing plenary slot and [named Profiles a top priority for the next standard](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p5000r1.pdf) ahead of the formal poll, a sequence that reads as an informal-institutions pattern (Helmke and Levitsky 2004) of pre-settlement then ratification. The group is formally advisory and speaks only when unanimous, and that concession is real. The pre-settlement reading rests on analysis rather than on a documented rule, so it is carried at reduced confidence. Seeking a plenary vote to endorse what was already decided informally would turn the practice into a rule. (low-medium)

Dependence runs in both directions, and each direction entrenches the core. Sustained participation requires national-body sponsorship, and losing it leaves a participant with no recourse but to find another, a power-dependence relation (Emerson 1962) that doubles as an expulsion lever. The committee is the dependent party toward the four front-end implementers whose conformance it cannot compel, and that dependency deepens as the supplier base narrows. Altering the status quo requires the convener, each relevant chair in sequence, the national-body bloc at its two-thirds-for and one-quarter-against thresholds, and in practice the implementers. Multiple veto players (Tsebelis 2002) are the deliberate stability design of ISO consensus, and the same design that blocks hasty change also shields the incumbents who prefer none. Whether that dependence hardens into control of the wording itself is the argument of the Capture Spine. (medium-high)

The stated beneficiary is the membership, since a standards body exists to serve those who take part in it. The structure routes the two scarce goods, appointment and agenda, toward the inner circle and the employers whose staff hold the chairs. Those who pay are the members who never voted on the rulebook that binds them, and beyond them the developers who hold no seat, the constituency Governance Without the Governed takes up. Benefit tracks proximity to the appointment power. (medium-high)

The coalition is tight, and its brokerage runs through a few hands. The convener's patronage over the chairs and the Direction Group's hold over those same chairs, who schedule work to its priority list, are two grips on one set of appointees. The vice-convener, Nina Ranns, has also served as INCITS-C++ secretary, placing one person across both committee leadership and the national-body gate that admits or removes participants, a bridge now weakening as that secretary role moves on. The former convener, now chairing the Foundation, aligns more closely over time with the Direction Group's safety line, tying the funding surface to the agenda surface. The one open fault line is the Direction Group's own legitimacy. (medium-high)

**The convener.** Guy Davidson holds the single most decisive internal office and ranks as a definitive stakeholder (Mitchell, Agle and Wood 1997). His authority is both legitimate and reward-based (French and Raven 1959), grounded in the power to call consensus and appoint every chair and study group. He presents as a neutral administrator investing in profiles and seeking reproducible floating point, yet in practice he backs Profiles and carries a numerics agenda, which makes him an aligned actor rather than a check, a stance that matters now because his term is where the C++29 safety settlement runs. His autonomous power sits below the office's face value. His agenda is set by the Direction Group's priority papers, including the standing [P2000 Direction for ISO C++](https://open-std.org/JTC1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2022/p2000r4.pdf), the rulebook defining his powers was written and is still maintained by his predecessor, and he was seated as the UK compromise nominee under a resolution favoring the fewest convenorships. His discretion is widest where the stakes are lowest. (medium)

**The Direction Group.** Convened by Vandevoorde and seating Stroustrup among its members, the Direction Group is a definitive stakeholder whose legitimacy is openly contested. It holds a standing plenary slot and a priority list the chairs treat as authoritative, yet it has no charter, no published selection criteria, and [announced itself in 2017 as "not a decision group"](https://lists.isocpp.org/admin/2017/02/0192.php) with no delegated authority. Its stated role is advisory, a body that recommends priorities much as a board of directors would. Its working role is to pre-settle those priorities and then seek the plenary's endorsement, a move that would formalize influence it already holds. The influence is amplified where employers overlap, since NVIDIA employs both the group's convener and an evolution subgroup chair. A charter-less body that sets the agenda is the sharpest edge of the closed core. (medium)

**INCITS.** The US national body is a definitive stakeholder whose formal role, casting one national vote, understates its real lever. That lever is control of who may participate, exercised through sponsoring members under a published schedule of fees. Withdrawal of sponsorship is de facto expulsion, and the [first-hand account of the removal](https://www.tomazos.com/ub_question_incident.pdf) records a sponsoring delegation cutting off a participant's access to the committee. The delegation was the Standard C++ Foundation's, and the same national body's secretary post has been held by a Foundation officer who also sits in the convener team. A gate operated from inside the leadership it should constrain is not an external check. The open, one-vote-per-organization framing coexists with a coercive lever. (medium-high)

The direction across the core is stable, with one exception. Office concentration survived the change of convener, recorded in the 2025 Kona administrative minutes, the Foundation-and-Direction-Group core persists, and founder centrality is undiminished, while the implementer dependency degrades as the base narrows. If the Foundation board and the Direction Group keep their present membership through the C++29 cycle, then founder-and-leadership centrality outlasts the convener handover and appointments keep routing through the same inner circle. If the board turns over, or a plenary forces the practices document to a ratifying vote, the concentration loosens. Continuity here requires no one to act, only for no one to force a vote. Over the next three years, the first branch is likely. (medium)

No mechanism now corrects the core defect. The practices document has never been put to the body it governs, and no appointment is confirmed by anyone other than the office that makes it. Both gaps close with instruments the committee already runs. The convener post is itself confirmed by national bodies, on the precedent that seated the current convener, and the plenary already conducts formal polls, so a ratifying vote on the practices document and an independent confirmation step for chair appointments would add no new machinery. What is absent is not the capacity to ratify but the decision to require it. (medium-high)

## 5. The Capture Spine: corporate funders convert money, chairs, and a self-electing board into control the members never voted.

Money, chairs, and a self-electing board sit in the same hands, converting non-voting funding into control the members never granted. (medium-high) Each coupling is visible in the Foundation's filings and the committee's own rosters. The unsettled question is motive, not structure, and the couplings produce the outcome whether or not anyone designed them.

**One spine, three joints.** The Standard C++ Foundation holds the money, compiler-vendor implementers hold the wording-group chairs, and a self-perpetuating board holds the Foundation, with the same names recurring at each joint. Non-voting sponsorship was built to keep money out of formal governance, yet routing that money through a board drawn from committee leadership rejoins the two things the design meant to separate. Members have no vote to withhold, because the rulebook examined in the Self-Ratifying Core is self-authored and unratified. A sponsor that cannot vote still funds the room, the travel, and the infrastructure the voters depend on. (medium-high)

The Foundation reported $2.1M in FY2025 revenue, hosts isocpp.org, cppreference, and the committee wiki, and runs its own conference. A support layer that handles logistics and money is expected and scales with size, but the iron law of bureaucracy (Pournelle 1979) marks the deviation: a permanent funding body now sits apart from the volunteer technical mission and controls both money and infrastructure. A volunteer committee can replace a chair in one cycle. It cannot easily replace the entity that owns its public surfaces and its bank balance. (low-medium)

The Foundation's sponsors are formally non-voting, and control rests with a self-electing board of five composed of the people who lead WG21. Self-perpetuating selection is the statutory baseline for the 501(c)(6) trade-association form the Foundation took at its 2012 founding, so board capture (Tillotson and Tropman 2025) turns on a different fact: the overseer and the overseen are one roster, and no bylaw separates them. Oversight that reports to itself is not oversight. (medium-high)

Funders supply essentially all money and most labor and hold the chairs while holding no formal vote, from NVIDIA's stack of subgroup seats to the finance bloc and the Foundation's founding sponsors. The parties the rules regulate, the compiler-vendor implementers, also write them: Wakely of Red Hat runs library wording, and Merrill vice-chairs core wording and the ABI. Influence proportional to contributed money and labor is the expected baseline in a sponsor-dependent body, and practitioner authorship is the competent-oversight norm in a specialized field. Institutional capture (Glaeser 2002) and regulatory capture (Stigler 1971) name the two steps past that baseline: documented chair-holding converts to agenda control, and the process rules themselves, SD-4, were authored by Herb Sutter as convener and remain amendable at will. Non-voting status understates real influence rather than limiting it. (medium)

The officers who control the Foundation's money also set WG21's technical direction - Sutter, Ranns, Stroustrup, Levi, and Wong hold seats on both sides - and financial assistance is gated by the same subgroup chairs who decide technical questions. Although a field this small qualifies few people to chair, so dual-role overlap is expected and collusion unproven, the conflict of interest (Davis 1982) is structural and documented: money and direction answer to one roster. That convergence is where the capture loop closes. (medium-high)

The captured apparatus reaches into the formal ballot as well. The United States holds most convenerships through INCITS, and the Direction Group and the Foundation speak early with one voice, so a pre-coordinated bloc shapes outcomes beyond its formal weight. Every mature body gives organized participants a coordination edge, and the one-voice claim is partly analytical and rests on lighter evidence. Institutional complementarity (Buthe and Mattli 2011) names the pattern at low confidence: domestic aggregation converts into international influence that fragmented national participation cannot match. Organization, not size, is the advantage here. (low-medium)

**Who gains and who pays.** The funders and the dual-role officers gain agenda influence, features aligned to their products, and control of the money that runs the room. The developer base that bears the downstream cost holds no seat, the concern developed in Governance Without the Governed, where those who pay are spoken for by proxies they cannot recall. The same firms that fund and staff the committee also shape what gets worked on, the subject of the Chairholders' Dividend. Cost lands on those the structure cannot hear. (medium)

Ranns is the load-bearing broker, linking WG21 leadership, the US national body, and the Foundation, and her control of minutes and procedure is power no single title records. The Direction Group and the Foundation form the core coalition, with Stroustrup and Wong seated on both. The fault line runs to the C++ Alliance, which funds tooling and community outside the apparatus and presses to split the money from the governance. A broker holds the structure together, and a fault line marks where it could break. (medium)

Herb Sutter's salience is definitive (Mitchell, Agle and Wood 1997). He gave up the neutral convener's chair and [left Microsoft in 2024](https://www.devclass.com/development/2024/11/12/iso-c-chair-herb-sutter-leaves-microsoft-declares-forthcoming-c-26-most-impactful-release-since-c11/1625565), but kept the chair that holds the money, remaining the Foundation's only compensated officer across every filed year. He authored the governing process rules and can amend them, and he authors the active safety settlement, [P3081](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/p3081r2.pdf), so he advocates technically where the convener may not. His move to [Citadel Securities as a Technical Fellow](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/herb-sutter-citadel-securities) aligns him with the rising finance bloc. Formal role emeritus, real power near its peak. (medium-high)

The Standard C++ Foundation's salience is definitive by interlock. On paper it is a neutral, legally separate 501(c)(6) support entity with non-voting sponsors. In practice a single self-electing board holds the money, the infrastructure, and, through its overlap with committee leadership, the standard's governance, the inverse of the Rust Foundation's deliberate split between funding and technical control. Its leverage is the discretionary purse: hosting, travel, and grants that WG21 officers must request. The entity has no instrument to separate its two roles when they conflict. (medium-high)

Nina Ranns holds a definitive salience built on concentration across three centers: the convenership team, the US national body, and the Foundation vice-presidency. She presents as a neutral process officer, while her control of minutes, procedure, and institutional memory is quiet but decisive power. Her paid standing depends on those roles, a positional interest beneath the procedural presentation. Titles understate the broker. (medium)

Microsoft's salience is definitive. It employs the LEWG chair, Levi, and the author of the Profiles framework paper, Dos Reis, ships MSVC and the STL, and helped found the Foundation. Its historical grip on the convenership lapsed when Sutter left in 2024, so formal presence and real influence now roughly track. What stays partly hidden is agenda-shaping through the Profiles framework, a lever wider than a single chair seat. (medium)

The C++ Alliance holds a dangerous salience: real influence, contested legitimacy, no committee office. The Alliance funds the tooling behind the public WG21 mailing mirror, wg21.org, runs the largest C++ Slack, and publishes reform essays that set outside framing. It funded the Safe C++ memory-safety line, [which lost to Profiles](https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/09/16/safe-c-proposal-all-but-abandoned-in-favor-of-profiles/925237), then endorsed Profiles while pressing a governance-reform campaign against the fused money-and-governance model. Its March 2026 vote-independence statement accompanied its move into meeting hosting and funding. Its funding runway is an open gap, so its staying power is unproven. (medium)

The trajectory is degrading. Implementer-staffed wording groups and dual roles persist, and in 2026 the funding layer widened as the C++ Alliance began underwriting attendance and prepared to host the committee at Buzios late in the year, a second funding channel atop persistent NVIDIA and finance-bloc chairs. If the Foundation adds no recusal rule and no separation of money from governance, then the widening funder layer deepens into agenda control before the C++29 cycle closes. If it adopts either, the conversion slows without reversing. Deepening is the likely path over that horizon. (medium)

The existing mechanism is the non-voting sponsorship tier, reinforced by the Alliance's 2026 vote-independence statement. It constrains one thing, the direction of votes, and leaves the self-electing board, the gated grants, and the dual roles untouched. The absent mechanism is specific: a recusal rule binding board members on hosting and grant decisions that touch their own subgroups, plus the separation of Foundation money from WG21 governance that the Rust Foundation already adopted. Both sit within a 501(c)(6)'s power to enact by bylaw. The board could bind itself. It has not. (medium)

## 6. The Sealed Room: sealing the reasoning manufactures consensus and lets conflicted experts act where those they serve cannot see.

Sealed reasoning and absent outcome audits manufacture consensus and let conflicted experts act unseen by the developers they serve. (medium) The seal on subgroup deliberation and the thirty-year absence of any outcome review are the two structural facts of this dossier, and each makes the other harder to see.

The mechanism is information control converted into decision control. Standing document SD-4 bars public quoting of subgroup discussion, wikis, and reflector traffic, so only papers and poll tallies leave the room while the reasoning that produced them stays inside (Akerlof 1970). The same confidentiality reaches the committee's communication channels, reflected in a 2022 [call for a managed chat service](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2022/p2263r1.html). Specialization always concentrates knowledge and confidentiality can protect candor, but that baseline cannot explain why WG21 seals reasoning that its sibling committees under ISO/IEC JTC1/SC22 publish as minutes. Confidentiality meant to protect candor would not need to bar quoting years after a decision ships. The deviation, not the asymmetry, is the finding.

Feedback in the committee is not independent of the actors it evaluates. The convener who runs consensus also determines it, and across more than thirty years the committee has published no retrospective and no outcome audit of any feature it standardized, and no national body has ever filed an ISO appeal (Ashby 1956). A body that never audits an outcome cannot separate a feature that worked from one that merely shipped, so completion stands in for success by default. The benign reading, that volunteer bodies below a resource threshold rarely fund self-audit, does not reach this case, because the Standard C++ Foundation's roughly $2.1 million budget clears that threshold and a three-year ballot checks conformance rather than consequences. The audit function does exist, in external trackers like [Are We Modules Yet](https://arewemodulesyet.org/) and academic feature-adoption studies. The committee's only outcome audit is one it does not run.

With the reasoning sealed, the formal consensus machinery presents a unanimity no observer can test. The Direction Group speaks only when it agrees unanimously, and the reversal of Safe C++ in favor of Profiles carries no comparative evaluation on record (Janis 1972). The formal double-consensus ballot then fronts a direction the Direction Group has already settled informally, and implementation experience works as an unwritten gate rather than a written rule, with Profiles advancing without an implementation (Meyer and Rowan 1977). Convergence among experts who share the same information is the expected baseline, and the non-unanimous Contracts vote of 114 to 12 to 3 shows dissent can reach the record when the seal lifts. The seal is a choice about which disagreements survive. (low-medium)

The fixed three-year release cadence works as a target that has drifted from the quality it once tracked. [P3443](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2024/p3443r0.pdf) documents schedule prioritized over engineering, with 56% of binding-poll papers published under a week before the vote (Goodhart 1984). A cadence is a legitimate coordinating discipline and deadline pressure is ordinary, yet the clock never slips: [C++26 reached feature-complete in June 2025 and shipped its draft international standard on schedule](https://herbsutter.com/2026/03/29/c26-is-done-trip-report-march-2026-iso-c-standards-meeting-london-croydon-uk/), and C++29 opened at once. A metric that is never missed has usually stopped measuring what it was meant to protect. The cost is absorbed as routine, with over 60% of participants reporting they often feel exhausted, 46% calling the pace unsustainable, and incubator study groups skipped in the endgame to hold quorum on the core pipeline (Pauly 1995). Each new cohort inherits the thinned review as normal. (low-medium)

A bounded expert in-group applies validity criteria, good design and taste, that its own members concede they do not share. The Direction Group's direction paper states the committee has no shared taste, and proposals are shut down on stylistic grounds, from ThePhD's [reference-binding optional](https://thephd.dev/to-bind-and-loose-a-reference-optional) to cases Vittorio Romeo [reports from the floor](https://vittorioromeo.com/index/blog/mar18_iso_meeting_report.html) (Haas 1992). A criterion its authors call absent cannot be applied consistently, so what clears the gate tracks the gatekeeper more than the proposal. That gate sits at structural chokepoints, where subgroup chairs decide what is forwarded, INCITS sits between individuals and WG21, and the convener sits between the subgroups and the plenary (Burt 1992). Single points of contact are expected at this scale and chair triage answers a real volume problem, yet the standard enforced at those chokepoints is the one the gatekeepers admit is incoherent, and the proposals it leaves underserved are the safety ones. Safety fails a test the committee cannot define. (medium)

The seal and the audit vacuum together define who can act unwatched. Committee experts and leadership decide, while roughly 16 million developers and the national-body constituencies bear the consequences, and the agent can pursue an employer's interest largely unobserved because the reasoning is sealed and no audit exists (Eisenhardt 1989). Delegation to expert agents is the point of a standards body and the national-body ballot is a genuine monitoring backstop, yet a ballot that sees only papers and tallies cannot monitor the reasoning the seal removed. The conflicted dual roles that supply the divergent interest, funders who set technical direction and implementers who chair the groups specifying what their employers ship, are documented in the Capture Spine, and the resulting drift of attention toward employer features while safety lags is detailed in Safety on the Clock. The seal is the hinge between them: opacity is not a byproduct of delegation here, it is the condition that makes unmonitored delegation durable. The cover protects whoever can use it, and everyone else sees only the tally. (medium)

**The seal has an external cost center.** Because subgroup reasoning never enters the record, the committee's public account of itself is written mostly by people it neither pays nor directs. A recurring reconstruction role, filled by trip reports, community forums, and independent conference coverage, reassembles what happened inside sealed rooms and exists only because the seal creates the gap it fills. Two further positional roles cover the missing audit: academic programming-language and empirical-software researchers supply the outcome evaluation the committee declines to run, and independent long-form critics document consequences like [module and ABI quality in practice](https://mropert.github.io/2026/04/13/modules_in_2026/) that no internal review examines. None of these actors holds a seat, and every one of them is load-bearing. The committee's transparency is volunteer-run and unpaid.

Meeting C++ holds that reconstruction niche as a named actor. Run since 2012 by one person, Jens Weller, through the legal entity Meetingcpp GmbH, the [platform](https://meetingcpp.com/meetingcpp/) aggregates committee and community activity through a weekly blog roll, daily feeds, a YouTube channel, and an annual Berlin conference (French and Raven 1959). Its power base is referent and expert rather than formal, with no seat and no vote, and its salience is dormant (Mitchell, Agle and Wood 1997). What it wants is the audience and revenue the committee's secrecy leaves available, which quietly ties its commercial interest to the transparency gap staying open. The record is consistent across fifteen conferences with no gap between claim and behavior, and the platform is [thriving into 2026](https://isocpp.org/blog/2026/03/announcing-meeting-cpp-2026). A single operator carrying a channel the whole ecosystem reads is a concentration risk the committee never chose and cannot manage.

Adobe STLab is the same pattern at higher salience and thinner evidence. Sean Parent, a senior principal scientist in Adobe's Software Technology Lab, publishes external evaluations including a [memory-safety and C++ successors feasibility audit](https://developer.adobe.com/cpp/whitepapers/cpp-successor-update/), the kind of outcome audit the committee never produces internally, while by his own statement not an active member. The power base is expert and referent, with no legitimate standing inside WG21, and the salience reads as dangerous on a single high-visibility artifact backed by Adobe's weight. The interest is professional and organizational, to keep Adobe's large C++ codebases viable and to steer the language's safety trajectory, a stake the successor audit protects whichever way the question resolves. The most cited external safety audit of C++ arrives from a self-described non-member, which places the committee's most consequential feedback outside its own process. The reading rests on one artifact. (low)

Across these findings the direction is stable to degrading. The seal persists, the audit vacuum persists, the cadence held through C++26, the taste gate again chose design judgment over an implemented alternative, and only the participant strain is clearly worsening. No internal mechanism addresses the seal or the audit vacuum, and none is proposed in the record. Two changes sit within the committee's current budget and form: SD-4 could be amended to publish subgroup rationales on a delay, matching the minutes its SC22 siblings already release, which would keep in-room candor while restoring a reasoning record, and an outcome audit that revisits shipped features against their stated goals could be chartered and funded from the Standard C++ Foundation's existing budget. Absent either, the external reconstructors remain the only audit the committee has.

The forward risk follows the audit vacuum. If the committee charters no internal outcome audit through the C++29 cycle, the audit function stays external and the seal's opacity holds unchallenged. If it charters one, the outside reconstructors lose their monopoly on the committee's reasoning. On a thirty-year base rate with zero retrospectives, an internal audit emerging is very unlikely. Confidence in that projection is high.

## 7. The Chairholders' Dividend: the firms that fund and staff the committee capture the features and decide what gets worked on.

The firms that fund and staff the committee capture the features that ship and steer what it works on next. (medium) Two of the marquee additions to C++26, std::execution and Contracts, trace to firms that hold scarce chair positions and pay for the committee's operation. For those firms, funding the apparatus and steering its output are one act. The chairs are formally neutral and the features carry real technical merit. What the merit does not explain is why capture tracks chair-holding so closely.

The mechanism is a pipeline that runs from chair to feature. A subgroup chair schedules and forwards proposals, chooses the ship vehicle, and owns the resulting standardese, so effort spent acquiring and holding a chair buys the power to steer design toward an employer's products (Tullock 1967). Positioning to capture that share rather than to create value is rent-seeking, and some of it is expected in any resource-limited body. The tell is where the effort concentrates, on the gates that decide what ships rather than the editorial desk that produces it. A chair is a chokepoint before it is an honor. (low-medium)

Prestige is the reward that makes chair-seeking rational. Deference flows to position, the convener, the Direction Group, the chairs, and the marquee conference figures, while the small editorial team that produces the normative text stays comparatively uncelebrated, and members report recognition tracking style over contribution (Bourdieu 1984). Hierarchy that rewards senior position is the peer-class baseline, and senior figures are often major contributors as well. The asymmetry still runs one way. The honor attaches to the seat that steers, not the desk that drafts. (low)

The captured value shows in what shipped. NVIDIA holds the heaviest single-employer chair concentration and landed [std::execution](https://www.open-std.org/JTC1/SC22/WG21/docs/papers/2023/p2300r7.html) in C++26 with its own [bundled reference implementation](https://github.com/NVIDIA/stdexec), the [portable-asynchrony model](https://www.hpcwire.com/2022/12/05/new-c-sender-library-enables-portable-asynchrony/) its GPU business needs. Bloomberg drove [Contracts](https://open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/p2900r14.pdf) through an author list saturated with its own staff. Finance firms standardize the low-latency primitives their trading systems run on. Firms that invest the most expert labor naturally shape the areas they work in, so contribution and capture correlate. What the correlation leaves unexplained is a reference implementation shipping from the same firm that chaired the gate (Coff 1999). (medium)

The firms that pay for the apparatus are the firms that steer it. The committee runs on cross-subsidy: employers cover delegate salaries and travel, corporate hosts absorb the $25,000 to $40,000 cost of a meeting, INCITS fees fund US processing, and [think-cell publicly calls itself the sole funder of the German delegation](https://www.heise.de/news/think-cell-sichert-deutsche-Beteiligung-an-C-Standardisierung-1811068.html) (Faulhaber 1975). Cross-subsidy is the universal and intended funding model for voluntary standards work, so the funding alone indicts nothing. The relevant fact is the overlap, since the firms writing the checks are the firms holding the chairs. Payment and steering resolve to the same set of names. How that money converts into chairs and a self-electing board is the Capture Spine. Here the chairs are the starting point. (medium-high)

What gets worked on follows the same private incentives. The free drafts are a non-excludable good sustained by volunteer labor that collects private, excludable side-benefits, steering an employer's feature and reputation signaling, while work with no private payoff stays chronically unstaffed, review capacity flat and incubator groups skipped to staff the core C++26 pipeline (Olson 1965). Selective incentives sustaining a volunteer body is Olson's own baseline, and here private motive and public good often coincide. The skew appears at the margin the incentives ignore, the unglamorous review and editorial work that no employer sponsors. Attention is a budget, and it is spent where it pays. The crowding-out of safety work that results is the subject of Safety on the Clock. (medium)

Benefit concentrates and cost diffuses. The chair-holding firms and the Standard C++ Foundation board capture the concentrated benefit of steering the standard, while roughly 16 million developers bear the diffuse cost and pay ISO to read the result they helped shape (Wilson 1980). Concentrated benefit against diffuse cost describes nearly every standards body, and the broad base free-rides on unofficial free renderings in turn. The asymmetry that matters is organizational, since the chair-holders act as a bloc and the base cannot. A cost spread thin enough is a cost no one contests. Those unrepresented millions are the subject of Governance Without the Governed. (medium)

The internal relations are a set of overlapping grips with one live fault line. NVIDIA, Microsoft, IBM through Red Hat, Bloomberg, and the finance bloc each hold chairs that gate their own priority areas, a patronage pattern the roster records seat by seat. Personnel also move among the firms that fund, implement, and populate the committee, Microsoft to Citadel, EDG to NVIDIA, IBM to Codeplay, so future employment can shape present positions (Kalmenovitz et al. 2023). A specialized field has few employers, so the moves are expected and the influence mechanism itself stays unobserved. The coalition is not seamless, since Microsoft's Dos Reis called Contracts not ready while Bloomberg carried it to adoption, an [open disagreement that persists after the feature shipped](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/03/31/contracts-are-in-c26-despite-disagreement-over-their-value/5229065). Coalitions of convenience hold only until the features collide. (low-medium)

**The chairs.** The evolution and wording subgroup chairs, EWG's Keane, LEWG's Levi, CWG's Maurer, and LWG's Wakely, are definitive stakeholders and the sequential chokepoints of the process (Mitchell, Agle and Wood 1997). Their authority is legitimate and delegated by the convener, but its working weight is reward and gatekeeping power, since a feature dies if a chair will not schedule or forward it (French and Raven 1959). Three of the four gates sit with a single employer each, EWG with NVIDIA, LEWG with Microsoft, LWG with Red Hat, and Levi holds the LEWG gate while also serving as Foundation Secretary. The neutral-chair framing is real and mostly honored. What it cannot neutralize is a design gate and an adjacent office in the same hand. (medium)

**NVIDIA.** NVIDIA is a definitive stakeholder holding the most complete single-employer stack on the committee. Across nominally independent seats it holds EWG (Keane), two incubator chairs, the 2026 Direction Group chair and the ABI Review chair (Vandevoorde), and INCITS PL22 (Lelbach), and it ships stdexec, the reference implementation of std::execution. Its stated aim is portable heterogeneous computing for the whole ecosystem run through neutral chairs. The divergence is that one stack steers intake, design, direction, ABI review, and US national-body voting at once, in exactly the areas serving a GPU business. The committee's own ABI charter caps representatives at two per company, naming the concentration risk in a group NVIDIA chairs. The rule points straight at the lever NVIDIA already holds. (medium)

**IBM.** IBM is a definitive stakeholder whose influence runs quietly through Red Hat. Through Red Hat it employs the LWG chair and libstdc++ maintainer (Wakely) and a CWG vice-chair who also vice-chairs ABI Review (Merrill), which gives it one of the three implementations and a heavy hand on the standardese. The interest is aligned and structural, since an implementer wants precise, implementable wording and a stable ABI, and it applies that interest as a veto over unimplementable text at the final editorial stage. The roster labels the seats IBM while the engineers are Red Hat and GCC. The decisive lever is the quiet one, applied after the floor vote rather than during it. (medium)

**Bloomberg.** Bloomberg is a dominant stakeholder and definitive within its domain, holding one formal seat (LWG vice-chair, Kuehl) plus EWG voting (Lakos). Its lever is authorship saturation rather than office, since the Contracts author list is heavily its own staff, it argued in [its own paper that P2900 is superior to a Contracts TS](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2024/p3276r0.pdf), and it backs the work with [platinum CppCon sponsorship](https://cppcon.org/sponsors/) and a public [at-scale C++ program](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/stories/bloomberg-advancing-c-scale/). Years of expert labor in one facility is legitimate, and the firm calls Contracts its most powerful correctness tool. The result is de facto ownership of a language feature by a single firm's author list, contested only after it shipped. One seat understates a firm that wrote the feature. (medium)

**The finance firms.** Citadel Securities, DRW, PDT Partners, Jump Trading, and Hudson River Trading form a dominant and rising bloc rather than one accredited actor. Their engineers hold the performance subgroups (SG22 Doumler at Citadel, SG4 Snyder at PDT, SG10 co-chair Revzin at Jump), DRW is a Foundation founder, and Herb Sutter is now a Citadel Technical Fellow while chairing the Foundation. Their aim, better C++ for low-latency and real-time work, is advanced exactly by those chairs, and they brand their [involvement](https://www.citadelsecurities.com/careers/career-perspectives/sea-change-in-c-why-opportunities-abound/) and [early C++26 adoption](https://builtin.com/articles/engineering-future-inside-citadel-securities-early-adoption-c26) openly. As big tech retreats, this bloc becomes the load-bearing constituency for high-performance C++, and Sutter is the broker who links it to the top of governance. The chair count today understates where the weight is heading. (medium)

**think-cell.** think-cell is a definitive stakeholder that surfaced as a dark actor, and on a power-interest reading it rates manage-closely (Mendelow 1991). A Berlin charting-software firm of about 150 to 200 people, it funds a national body's entire committee seat: it has funded the German delegation since 2013, after DIN moved to dissolve the group for lack of contributions, and its engineer [chairs SG9, the ranges study group](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/n5016.pdf), the exact library facility its own product depends on. Its power base is reward coupled with expert standing. The [civic-support framing](https://www.think-cell.com/en/career/events/2025-02-10) is real and modest in scale. What it buys is durable, low-competition control of a national channel and a study-group chair. Paid rescue of a national body is the clearest hidden lever among the dark actors. (medium)

The direction is degrading where it is legible. The captured features shipped in C++26, finalized in March 2026, converting steering into delivered value for the chair-holding firms. The cross-subsidy broadened as the C++ Alliance added an attendance-funding program, and the finance bloc kept absorbing committee talent while the concentrated-benefit structure held steady. Nothing in the record reverses the pattern. The next cycle inherits the same chairs. (medium)

The one anti-concentration instrument on the books is narrow. The ABI Review group caps representatives at two per company, an admission that single-employer over-weighting is a real risk, but it binds one subgroup and governs representation, not the chair appointments that do the steering. No committee-wide rule caps how many chairs one employer may hold, requires chair rotation, or forces recusal when a chair's employer ships the reference implementation of the feature under review. Each is a rule the convener could add under the existing practices document without new machinery. The capacity to limit concentration exists. The rule that would apply it does not. (medium)

If chair concentration persists through the C++29 cycle with no cap on single-employer chairs, the features that advance will keep tracking the chair-holders' employer products and unfunded work will keep lagging. If a committee-wide anti-concentration or chair-rotation rule is adopted, that coupling loosens. On the C++26 base rate, continuation of the pattern is very likely. Confidence in that projection is medium.

## 8. Governance Without the Governed: the millions who bear the cost hold no seat and are spoken for by proxies they cannot recall.

The millions who bear the cost of C++ hold no governance seat, and their proxies cannot be recalled. (medium-high) The gap runs three layers deep. The affected base is absent from the room, the intermediaries who stand in for it answer to no one who chose them, and the decisions taken in its name trace to no single actor. No standards body can seat everyone, and national representation is ordinary ISO practice, but neither fact moves the core: the party that carries the consequences sits outside every channel that produces them.

Two intermediary structures carry the base's voice, and neither can be instructed or recalled. National bodies cast their countries' votes, and the Direction Group, author of Direction for ISO C++, speaks for the direction of C++ with no constituency that selected it (Pitkin 1967). Appointed national-body representation is a legitimate, standard ISO design, yet the Direction Group's mandate rests on accumulated standing rather than on any electorate. A proxy that cannot be recalled is a proxy the represented cannot correct. (medium-high)

Responsibility dissolves at the moment of decision. Consensus governance diffuses responsibility by design, but here it is a convener straw-poll that records no individual vote, and the committee's own practices route direction-setting through a body that speaks only in one unanimous voice (Davies 2024). Tracing a contested discontinuation back to a responsible party yields only a tally, never a name. A decision no name attaches to is a decision no name can be held to. (medium)

The base that carries the cost is large and specific. Roughly 16 million developers write C++, a figure Stroustrup cites defensively, and the language runs for [about 23.5% of developers in the 2025 Stack Overflow survey](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology). SG14's low-latency users get input but no vote, downstream regulated industries inherit the defaults, and the end users whose devices run C++ absorb the memory-safety consequences directly. Though no body can seat every affected party and anyone may join a national body, none of these constituencies holds a governance seat, and each must pay ISO to read the text it helped produce (Young 2000). Paying to read the rules one lives under inverts the ordinary relation between a standard and its public. (medium-high)

The national bodies are the one authority sitting outside the committee's internal consensus, and by salience they are Dominant: high power, high legitimacy, low urgency (Mitchell, Agle and Wood 1997). A bloc of negative votes can force revision or block a Draft International Standard, which carries only on [two-thirds in favour with no more than a quarter against](https://www.iso.org/sites/ConsumersStandards/voting_iso.html). That lever has gone unused for governance for more than thirty years. Formal authority this high paired with near-zero exercise turns the sole external check into ratification by custom. Their alignment is real but dormant, and that dormancy is why internal governance meets no outside test before the ballot stage. (medium-high)

The developer community is a Dependent stakeholder, high in legitimacy and empty of power. The standard exists for these roughly 16 million users, yet they hold no vote and no seat, and their only lever is aggregate adoption, slow and uncoordinated. Their stated wants, gathered through surveys, are safety, stability, and better tooling, while their revealed behavior is staying on old standards or leaving for other languages. Adoption as a vote is a lagging signal the committee reads years after the preference formed. In the meantime the base is spoken for by the Standard C++ Foundation, SG14 chairs, and vendor delegates, proxies whose agendas are their own. The Chairholders' Dividend covers how those agendas take shape. (medium)

Two private actors now measure and teach the base that governance does not seat. JetBrains, a commercial tools vendor, runs an annual [State of Developer Ecosystem survey](https://blog.jetbrains.com/research/2025/10/state-of-developer-ecosystem-2025/) that reached 24,534 developers across 194 countries in 2025. Its power rests on expert credibility (French and Raven 1959), and it ranks Dormant. On the influence grid it sits at Keep Informed (Mendelow 1991). learncpp.com, a free single-author site [running since 2007](https://www.learncpp.com/about/), shapes how newcomers first [learn the language](https://www.learncpp.com/) through referent authority, a Discretionary presence with no formal standing. The governance hook for both is weak, and a reading in which these are routine marketing telemetry and tutorial traffic survives alongside. What deviates is the outsourcing itself: the base's only current voice and its first model of the language rest with parties the committee neither directs nor can replace. The public whose infrastructure runs C++, and the cohorts not yet writing it, are affected most and seated least. (low-medium)

The trajectory is stable and, for the base, adverse. The affected base still holds no seat, and safety regulators in the US and EU are hardening into its involuntary proxy. Representation is arriving from outside the committee because it never formed inside. If the committee opens no formal channel for the affected base before external safety mandates bind, then regulators will keep standing in for the missing seat and the gap will resolve as law written elsewhere. If such a channel opens, the proxy chain shortens and the base gains a voice it can correct. Horizon: three to five years. The base remaining seatless across that window is likely. (medium)

No mechanism lets the affected base instruct or recall the proxies who speak for it. The open-participation route, that anyone may join a national body, is an on-ramp for individuals, not a channel for the base as a class, and it cannot close a collective gap. The committee's procedures name no instrument for binding a proxy to its constituency or removing one that drifts from it. Absent that instrument, the representation gap is structural rather than incidental, and no current process narrows it. (medium)

## 9. Borrowed Ground: the committee runs on a platform, a corpus, and volunteers it neither owns nor can replace.

The committee runs on a platform, a corpus, and volunteers it neither owns nor can replace. (medium-high) Three dependencies stack in one direction: ISO owns the publication platform and sells the result, one academic server carries the entire public paper corpus, and single volunteers hold the toolchain and the most-read renderings of the text. None of the three has a surfaced failover. A dependency that has never failed looks like permanence until the day it lapses, and nothing in the record shows the committee has planned for that day.

The mechanism is the absence of a second copy. Each dependency is a single instance with no mirror, no deputy, and no written procedure to reconstruct it, so a lapse at any point removes a function and starts a clock the committee cannot slow. Because the three layers are independent, they share no common cause of failure, which also means no single fix protects more than one of them. (medium)

Every path to the standard runs through a chokepoint the committee does not own. ISO holds the copyright and bars a free authoritative text, so the final [ISO/IEC 14882:2024](https://www.iso.org/standard/83626.html) is sold while only [drafts stay free](https://isocpp.org/std/the-standard), a gatekeeper's control over an essential facility (Areeda 1990). The public corpus of working drafts and issues lists at [open-std.org](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/standards) sits on a single DTU-hosted server, a single-source dependency (Chopra and Sodhi 2004) with no mirror the committee controls, and national-body accreditation gates who may even enter the room. Relying on established machinery is the efficient choice for a small volunteer body, yet it leaves three unrelated owners each holding a gate the committee cannot buy back. (medium)

The deepest dependency is knowledge that was never written down. Operational knowledge concentrates in very few hands: the LaTeX toolchain that builds the draft was maintained single-handedly by Pete Becker through C++11, the project-editor team is tiny, the reflector runs through a one-person officer, and meeting logistics rest on one organizer, roles that SD-4 documents only lightly. Such concentration is tacit knowledge (Polanyi 1966): the holder can perform the task but has not externalized how, so the capability departs with the person. EDG's 2026 wind-down, with [John Spicer stepping back](https://herbsutter.com/2025/11/10/trip-report-november-2025-iso-c-standards-meeting-kona-usa/) at [Edison Design Group](https://www.edg.com/company/background), is the first of these cliffs to arrive, softened only partly by a planned open-sourcing of the front end. Single-maintainer infrastructure is the ordinary texture of volunteer projects, yet here each departure subtracts a function with no trained understudy. The record shows continuity that has held, not resilience that has been tested. (medium-high)

WG21 does not merely use ISO, it operates inside a platform that can rewrite the rules beneath it. The parent body sets the [Directives, balloting, editing, and participation terms](https://isocpp.org/std/iso-iec-jtc1-procedures) and can change process on its own initiative, as when ISO began encouraging a white-paper route the committee had not chosen. A two-sided platform (Rochet and Tirole 2003) of this kind stands between the producers who write the text and the buyers who purchase it, and it taxes the exchange by selling what the committee gives away in draft. Although operating within ISO is a stable and deliberate choice, it hands the platform a standing authority over process that the committee cannot revoke, and the relationship already shifted at its edge in 2026 when the March meeting was no longer a joint INCITS meeting. (medium)

The gains and the exposures fall on different parties. ISO gains a revenue stream and retained control over process, and the committee gains international legitimacy and infrastructure it does not pay for, while the solo maintainers carry uncompensated load and the broad C++ developer community downstream inherits whatever outage a single lapse produces. An arrangement is cheapest to the body that runs it when the risk is held by parties who have no claim on it. (medium)

The dependencies run one way and create no counter-leverage. WG21 needs ISO, the host, and the volunteers, and none of them needs WG21 in a way that would let the committee compel continuity, so the fault lines are individual rather than systemic. The platform dependency does not stand alone either, since it stacks with the external regulatory lever developed in Safety on the Clock, a second force the committee does not control. Every actor here is cooperative, which is precisely why the exposure stays invisible. Concentration, not hostility, is the risk. (medium)

ISO/IEC JTC1/SC22, the parent subcommittee, is the platform's face inside the structure. It owns work item 14882, runs the ballots, and confirms the convener, which makes its salience Dominant (Mitchell, Agle and Wood 1997), rising to Definitive at the ballot boundaries where it acts. Its power is legitimate and delegated (French and Raven 1959) rather than exercised day to day. The revealing pattern is inverse: formal authority is high while actual influence over substance is low, and the national-body ballot, the one external recourse it hosts, has not been used against a WG21 action in more than thirty years. A gate that has never closed is still a gate, and dormancy is not absence. (medium)

The [open-std.org host](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/), sponsored by DTU with HTML design credited to Keld Simonsen and service historically provided by DKUUG per the [JTC1 service record](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/impit/open/j1n4536/j1n4536.htm), is the single point through which the public reads the committee's output. Its salience is Dependent, a latent structural chokepoint rather than any exercised leverage. Its authority is legitimate only by SC22 delegation, and the real risk is continuity concentrated in one aging, single-volunteer arrangement. The C++ Alliance's wg21.org adds a browsing layer over the corpus but does not rehost the papers, so no real failover has surfaced. A chokepoint that has always just worked is the hardest kind to notice. (medium)

eel.is/c++draft, maintained by Eelis van der Weegen, is the [HTML rendering of the working draft](https://eel.is/c++draft/) that most practitioners actually read, generated by the single-maintainer [cxxdraft-htmlgen](https://github.com/Eelis/cxxdraft-htmlgen) tool. Its power base is expert and referent, with no formal authority. Its salience registers as Dangerous by attribute, yet it functions as a plain single-maintainer dependency rather than leverage. The de facto free front-end to the paywalled normative text runs on one person's tool and one personal domain. The most-used door to the standard is one nobody official is required to keep open. (medium)

Tim Song occupies two roles at once. He maintains the widely cited [timsong-cpp/cppwp](https://github.com/timsong-cpp/cppwp) renderings of the working paper and historical standards, and he is an active LWG member authoring library wording under ANSI. That dual position makes his salience Definitive in domain and places him in the Manage Closely quadrant (Mendelow 1991). He helps write the library text and he hosts how it is read. A near-solo reference host who is also an insider is effectively a register-class actor who arrived through the read-path rather than through a seat. (medium)

cppreference.com, founded and led by Nate Kohl, is the premier free C++ reference and the practical substitute for the paywalled text, run by volunteer wiki editors. Its salience is Definitive and rising, because the Standard C++ Foundation now provides its hosting and administration and led the [2026 MediaWiki migration](https://isocpp.org/blog/2026/04/announcement-cppreference.com-update). A former outside chokepoint is being drawn inside the register, so influence over how C++ is understood is migrating from a solo operator toward the same board that runs governance. Consolidation can raise reliability and narrow the number of hands at the same time. (medium)

The direction is mixed but sharpening at the base. The platform terms, the ISO paywall, the single corpus host, and the read-path renderings all held stable through 2026, while the tacit-knowledge layer degraded as EDG's closure turned a theoretical single-holder risk into a realized one. The first backstop to be tested is arriving by attrition rather than by plan. If another single-holder role turns over before a documented successor exists, then a toolchain or read-path surface lapses for an interval the committee cannot compress, because the knowledge is unwritten. If the planned EDG front-end open-sourcing becomes a template for documenting the toolchain, editorial, and reflector roles, the cliff flattens instead. Over the next standard revision cycle, continued sharpening of the tacit-knowledge cliff absent a documentation program is likely. (medium)

No failover or succession mechanism currently exists for any of these single points. The committee could adopt specific low-cost measures within its present means: mirror the corpus on a second host it controls, publish written runbooks for the toolchain, reflector, and logistics roles, and extend the EDG open-sourcing precedent into a standing rule that critical roles document their operations before a handoff. None of this requires new funding at scale or a change to ISO's terms, and each requires only a decision to treat continuity as a deliverable. The absent mechanism is not money. It is a plan. (medium)

## 10. Safety on the Clock: attention flows to employer features while the safety story goes unshipped and the regulatory deadline runs.

Attention flows to employer features while the safety story stays unshipped and the regulatory deadline runs. (medium) The mechanism is a single chain. Effort concentrates on the features member firms are paid to build, the safety deliverable the diffuse user base ranks first stays unbuilt, and that gap leaves the domain exposed as government pressure hardens into a compliance clock. The committee shipped the capability its own members could author and deferred the one an outside audience must accept. Priority did not set the order. Buildability did.

**Two safety efforts** have now failed to reach the standard. Safe C++ was abandoned in favor of Profiles, and Profiles were then deferred to C++29 rather than made C++26. The C++26 trip report records the split: reflection, one of the base's two stated top wants, shipped, while safety, the other, slipped a full cycle. Review capacity shows the same drawdown, with 56% of polled papers getting under a week of incubation. Spending accumulated standing for present throughput is capital consumption (Mises 1949), and effort tracking what member firms fund - GPU asynchrony for NVIDIA, Contracts for Bloomberg, low-latency for the finance member firms - over what users rank first is an alignment gap (Jensen and Meckling 1976). Employer features are genuinely useful and a cautious safety response can be prudence, yet the deliverable that outranks the rest is the one deferred twice. The work that shipped is the work insiders could finish alone. (low-medium)

The gap would matter less without a clock, and the clock is now explicit. The United States campaign - [the NSA and CISA case for memory-safe roadmaps](https://www.cisa.gov/case-memory-safe-roadmaps), joined by the FBI and the White House ONCD - names C and C++ as the problem class, and [vendors already plan against a January 2026 roadmap expectation](https://www.kdab.com/software-technologies/rust/memory-safety-roadmap-for-secure-programming/). In the European Union the [Cyber Resilience Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act) can bar non-compliant products from the market and impose manufacturer liability, with [reporting obligations from September 2026 and main obligations from December 2027](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/eu-cyber-resilience-act-requirements-products-software.html). Adoption is voluntary, so no license exists to revoke, and the pressure targets the memory-unsafe language class broadly rather than WG21's governance, yet market access and liability are levers the committee cannot control (Vernon 1971). A deadline the committee cannot move meets a deliverable it has twice failed to ship. (medium-high)

The costs do not fall where the features land. Employer firms get the capabilities they staffed for, while the roughly 16 million C++ developers carry the exposure, and memory-unsafe code is increasingly treated as a liability in the procurement and critical-infrastructure settings they cannot leave. That exitless base is the Frozen ABI's subject, and the lever bears hardest on the constituency least able to move. WG21's authority is pragmatic and cognitive (Suchman 1995), authoritative because widely adopted, and while institutional standing outlasts any single performance period, the basis is the performance, and the performance now visibly lags. Regulation may well arrive and the committee may well adapt, but adaptation on the regulator's schedule is not authority. Safety no regulator has credited is safety only in name. (low-medium)

The regulators are the purest external force on the committee's agenda. United States and European Union government safety regulators - the NSA, CISA, FBI, White House ONCD, NIST and DARPA, and the EU Cyber Resilience Act - classify as a dangerous stakeholder (Mitchell, Agle and Wood 1997): high power, high urgency, and no standing inside WG21. Their power is statutory and market-based, resting on market-access control, procurement, and DARPA's [funding of C-to-Rust translation](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/translating-all-c-to-rust). The [Rust-versus-C++ comparison](https://blog.jetbrains.com/rust/2025/12/16/rust-vs-cpp-comparison-for-2026/) is now mainstream industry framing. What they want is stated plainly, the elimination of the memory-unsafe vulnerability class, and their trail from the NSA through CISA, ONCD, the CRA, and DARPA has been consistent and escalating. The committee has registered the threat rather than met it, answering with a Direction Group [safety strategy](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/p3874r0.pdf) and Stroustrup's [note calling the pressure a "credible threat"](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/p3651r0.pdf). Controlling market access is a stronger lever than any committee vote. (medium-high)

Across the chain the direction runs one way. The alignment gap held roughly stable as reflection shipped, but capital consumption, the regulatory lever, and the legitimacy basis degraded together, and the net trajectory is strongly downward. Eroding legitimacy resolves into fragile voluntary authority, the condition the Route-Around develops, where under-delivery invites implementers and vendors to build the de-facto standard themselves. If WG21 reaches the September 2026 CRA reporting threshold with no shipped, regulator-credited safety mechanism, then the safety narrative passes to the bodies that can enforce one, the regulators and the implementers. If a creditable safety increment lands first, the committee keeps the authority it now only asserts. Through the CRA main obligations in December 2027, the first branch is the likely one. (medium)

No dedicated remediation path is identified. The evidence records no specific concern to route to an existing mechanism, and none of WG21's standing processes binds a safety deliverable to an external deadline. The committee can adopt a feature or defer it, but it holds no instrument that ties a ship date to a statutory clock, and a volunteer body under no schedule will not manufacture one on demand. The absent mechanism is a delivery commitment enforceable against the calendar. (low-medium)

## 11. The Route-Around: where the committee under-delivers, implementers and vendors build the de-facto standard themselves.

The committee requires no proof of implementation, so implementers and vendors set the real C++ standard. (medium-high) The published text is a specification, not a ship vehicle, because a feature is inert until a compiler realizes it and a build tool consumes it. Where the committee declines to require implementation, a rival de facto authority forms in the gap and decides what portable C++ actually means. The decision migrates from the plenary room to the shop floor. Real portable behavior is set downstream.

Two absences drive the dynamic. The body adopts features with no requirement that anyone has built them, so conformance timing rests with implementers who answer to their own release calendars. Where it under-delivers a discipline the language needs, outside vendors sell that discipline, and their product becomes the working rule. Running-code legitimacy (Russell 2006), the authority earned by working implementations rather than by adopted text, migrates to whoever ships. The specification describes the language. The toolchain defines it.

The gap is written into committee rule. The body records no requirement on implementation experience to adopt a proposal [P2274R0](https://wg21.link/p2274r0), and the record shows the cost. Profiles advanced without an implementation, unified executors advanced and were then retired, and modules remain partial six years on. An independent tracker counts modules in roughly 108 of 2587 surveyed projects, and a practitioner still asks whether they are usable in 2026. Incompatible binary formats across compilers, the .gcm, .pcm, and .ifc files, mean portability is set by convergence rather than by text. The screening check existed and went unused.

The committee sits atop resources it does not own. It depends on ISO for publication, national bodies for votes, implementers for realization, the Foundation for money, and volunteers for labor, which is the ordinary condition of a vendor-neutral standards body, yet the same dependence means the output can be routed around. Resource dependence (Pfeffer and Salancik 1978), the theory that whoever supplies the scarce input holds the leverage, identifies the scarce input here as a shipping compiler. Carbon, Rust, and compiler extensions each offer a path that never passes through the plenary. Withdrawal would stop the standard, not the language. (low-medium)

Adoption is eroding beneath a body that still speaks for the whole of C++. All voluntary standards rest on buy-in and the ISO-backed base remains large, but the trajectory is what deviates: implementers diverge on modules, regulators press memory-safe languages, and Rust displaces at the margin while Google routes new work to [Carbon](https://github.com/carbon-language/carbon-lang). Fragile voluntary authority (Brunsson and Jacobsson 2000), the condition of an organization whose writ holds only while adopters consent, describes a consent that is thinning at the edges. Rust counts roughly 5.1 million developers against C++'s 16.3 million, a minority growing where safety is mandated. Carbon stays [experimental](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/carbon-2026-latest-news-delays-real-reasons-behind-slow-alheraki-xwf8f), so the displacement has gaps. The erosion is uneven, not a rout. (medium-high)

Sponsors also route among venues to lower the price of passage. Weighing a Technical Specification against the International Standard is routine process design, yet the pattern is a steady tilt toward the lighter vehicle: the ship-vehicle fight over Contracts, de facto compiler behavior standing in for the text, and rival forums such as Boost cited as a better source of facilities. Forum shopping (Lerner and Tirole 2006), moving a proposal to the venue that extracts the fewest concessions, weakens the screening a single hard gate would impose. ISO's promotion of a lighter white-paper process points the same way. The path of least resistance runs outside the standard. (low-medium)

The gap pays whoever fills it. Compiler implementers gain an effective veto over what ships and when. Commercial safety vendors gain a market that exists because the language omits a safe subset. The roughly 16 million developers who write C++ absorb the cost as non-portable behavior and a safety story that never becomes normative. The parties positioned to supply the missing input are the ones who profit, and none of them gains by closing the gap. Scarcity is the product. (medium-high)

The binding leverage is concentrated and tightening. A feature is inert until implementers ship it, and implementer feedback gates ship-vehicle decisions in practice, past any formal seat. Consolidation narrows the field, with EDG winding down and Intel's compiler folded into Clang, as recorded in the November 2025 Kona trip report. The Direction Group overlaps the same layer through Vandevoorde, an implementer figure who also chairs direction, so the party that realizes features helps choose them. The gatekeeper and the roadmap share a chair.

**Major compiler implementers** (GCC/libstdc++, Clang/LLVM/libc++, MSVC/STL, EDG) are the definitive stakeholders of this dynamic (Mitchell, Agle and Wood 1997), holding power, legitimacy, and urgency together. Their power base is control over realization, since a voted feature stays a proposal until they compile it and chronic conformance lag works as a quiet veto. Microsoft withholds the /std:c++23 flag until the mode is complete, a documented case of paced [conformance](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/cppblog/new-release-cadence-and-support-lifecycle-for-msvc-build-tools/). Their leads also hold the wording and direction seats that shape the text they will later ship. They want a manageable implementation burden rather than a maximal standard, and consolidation hands them more of the language each year. The compiler is the standard that runs.

**CMake and Kitware** hold a second definitive position, won through tooling rather than compilation. A C++20 feature reaches users only when the build tool can consume it, and modules depend on CMake's [P1689 scanning](https://www.kitware.com/import-cmake-c20-modules/) to build at scale. At roughly 56 percent ecosystem adoption, the [build tool](https://cmake.org/history/) paces real-world uptake of a shipped feature, so one vendor's roadmap gates the language. Kitware sits in the tooling subgroup and brought P1689 into the process, which turns a de facto position into a seat. One build tool decides when a feature is usable.

**The Rust Project and Rust Foundation** are a dangerous, competitive stakeholder, carrying real power by substitution and no legitimacy inside the committee. Rust draws users and, more pointedly, supplies the governance model against which the committee is judged, its open [Leadership Council](https://rust-lang.github.io/rfcs/3392-leadership-council.html) contrasting with a self-appointed committee. The hidden channel is a funder hedge, because Microsoft, Google, and other Platinum backers of the Rust Foundation also fund the committee's base, so its own sponsors underwrite the substitute. Rust needs only to stay the safer default while interop improves. The benchmark is also a landing site. (medium-high)

**Zig and the Zig Software Foundation** fill the same competitive slot lower down, a systems language with a drop-in C and C++ compiler that takes users at the margin with no committee involvement. It reached the [TIOBE top tier](https://www.birjob.com/blog/zig-hit-tiobe-top-30) and targets a stability-locking [1.0 release](https://ziggit.dev/t/zig-roadmap-2026/10750) for mid-to-late 2026. The drop-in compiler is the capture mechanism, since adoption needs no migration decision, only a linker. Small today, it compounds quietly. (medium)

**The safety-discipline vendors** supply the safe subset the language omits, and each depends on that omission continuing. MISRA owns the de facto safety-critical coding standard, MISRA C++:2023 with 179 rules, and its usage rose about 8 percent to roughly 61 percent of automotive teams, a [compliance regime](https://www.sonarsource.com/blog/misra-c-plus-plus-compliance-early-access/) effectively mandatory in its domain. AUTOSAR's automotive [C++14 baseline](https://gsasindia.com/blog/coding-standards-misra-autosar) fed that standard and survives inside it at around 36 percent adoption, an influence by legacy embedding. Commercial static-analysis vendors monetize the same memory-safety gap, with the top five holding about 52 percent of a growing [SAST market](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/static-application-security-testing-market). Each market shrinks if the gap ever closes. (medium-high)

**The CHERI hardware-safety consortium** (Arm Morello, Cambridge, SRI, lowRISC, Linaro) offers a hardware route to the same end, spatial and temporal memory safety for largely unmodified C and C++, which sidesteps the committee outright. Arm closed its Morello research board with no product line, yet [CHERI-RISC-V](https://ednutting.com/2025/10/05/cheri-vs-oma.html) advanced with new silicon and an active alliance, so the [approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capability_Hardware_Enhanced_RISC_Instructions) persists in another venue. Microsoft and Google both participate, the same overlap that marks the funder hedge. Safety can arrive from the silicon up. (medium)

**The reference layer** measures the language and stands in for its text. Stack Overflow, a dormant but load-bearing actor, is the free front-end most programmers consult instead of the paywalled standard, and its 2025 developer survey put C++ usage at 23.5 percent across some 49,000 respondents. The independent modules [tracker](https://github.com/royjacobson/modules-report) is a demanding actor that fixes the factual baseline for whether a shipped feature actually shipped. When the record of reality lives outside the body, the body no longer narrates its own adoption. (medium)

Behind these named actors sits an unnamed one, the de facto standard formed by divergent compiler extensions and incompatible module binaries. No person owns it and no vote created it. It sets real portable behavior through implementer convergence rather than through the text, exactly where the committee declined to require implementation. An absence has hardened into authority.

The trajectory is uneven erosion at the margin, not displacement at the center. Modules stayed largely unimplemented, Rust kept displacing where safety is mandated, and regulators kept pressing, while ISO-backed adoption held the middle. If the committee ships C++29 with no implementation floor and its safety story still deferred, a deferral examined under Safety on the Clock, then the route-around widens, with Rust holding new safety-critical starts and Google keeping new work in Carbon through the C++29 cycle to about 2029. If instead the body adopts an implementation-experience gate, the route-around narrows toward the safety-vendor layer alone. Continued erosion at the margin is likely over that window. (medium)

The absent mechanism is a rule the body could adopt within its current form, a requirement that a feature ship in at least one, and preferably two, implementations before entering the standard. Such a gate needs no new funding and no change to membership, only an amendment to the working procedures. It would convert the implementers' informal veto into a transparent precondition and close the vacuum the route-around exploits. The check already exists as an option. Whether the body requires it is the open question. (medium-high)

## 12. The Frozen ABI: a silent installed base locks the language in place while the mobile few take the exit the captive cannot.

A silent installed base has frozen the language in place, and the stakeholders who can leave are leaving while the captive base cannot. (medium) The mechanism runs in one direction. The switching costs of a vast body of existing C++ code made a superior but incompatible change declinable, that refusal froze acknowledged-suboptimal library layouts, the freeze denied the base any real exit, and the contributors who did hold alternatives took them. Lock-in at the bottom produces exit at the top.

**The break was declinable because the base is captive.** A vast installed base creates switching costs that keep users on C++ even under memory-safety pressure, and stable binary interfaces lock the standard's own evolution (Klemperer 1987). Some lock-in is intrinsic to any mature language and backward compatibility is a genuine user benefit, so this is not an engineered trap. It is a structural one: [Google's P1863 "ABI - Now or Never"](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2020/p1863r1.pdf) was declined to protect binary compatibility, freezing layouts the committee itself calls suboptimal, because early adopters would bear the transition cost while later users capture the gain (Farrell and Saloner 1986). Caution about breaking a massive base is rational, and a [2025 conference talk](https://cppcon2025.sched.com/event/27bOC/could-we-handle-an-abi-break-today) reopened the question, yet the freeze held and vendors kept compatibility reaching back a decade. (medium-high)

**The base has neither voice nor exit.** The roughly 16 million users have no vote and, through lock-in, no easy way out (Hirschman 1970). Dissatisfied contributors who cannot change the body through voice leave instead, and the capable ones have: Niall Douglas to WG14, JeanHeyd Meneide and Izzy Muerte away from the work, and Safe C++'s author disengaged after Profiles prevailed. Routing user input through national bodies is the deliberate ISO design and some churn is normal, which is the benign frame. The structural finding is that the exit is available only to the mobile, so the departures degrade the body for the captive who remain. (medium-high)

**Alternatives are unevenly held, and the strong-alternative party is the one exiting.** Google holds real alternatives in Carbon and Rust and exercised leverage by walking after "indefinite ABI stability doesn't work for Google" (Fisher and Ury 1981), while finance and accelerator firms have moderate options and the 16 million users have weak ones and are taken for granted. Differing alternatives across an ecosystem is ordinary, and strong firms leaving can be healthy market behavior. What the pattern shows is asymmetry that compounds the freeze: the party with the best exit is precisely the one whose continued investment the committee could least easily replace. (low-medium)

High-value, mobile stakeholders are leaving while captive ones stay (Akerlof 1970). Google ramped down after the lost ABI vote, capable contributors departed publicly, and EDG confirmed its wind-down with John Spicer stepping back. Some attrition is expected of a 35-year volunteer body and total participation still grows, which softens the reading. It does not remove the concern that the departures concentrate among the hardest-to-replace. (medium-high)

**The homed actors are a departing insider and two silent beneficiaries.** **Google** is a definitive but disengaging stakeholder: it owns Clang and LLVM, the front end the wider ecosystem builds on, employed the standard's editors, and can fund a whole competing language, yet its committee footprint is shrinking by choice. Its power now runs through infrastructure ownership and coercion-by-defection rather than votes. **Debian** and **Oracle** never sat on the committee at all. Debian's [GCC-5 libstdc++ transition](https://wiki.debian.org/GCC5) was among its largest package migrations, and Oracle publicly frames an unstable interface as ["a nightmare for library and middleware vendors"](https://www.oracle.com/technical-resources/articles/it-infrastructure/stable-cplusplus-abi.html); both are dormant, coercive-by-consequence actors whose aggregate sunk cost is the weight that made the break unwinnable. They are the dogs that would howl if the interface broke, so it never has to bark. Behind them stands the broad binary-distribution ecosystem, a positional stakeholder whose shipped binaries are the silent constituency the vote never counted.

Who benefits and who pays is inverted from who decides. The installed base gets the stability it depends on, the mobile firms get a clean exit, and the captive developers inherit a language frozen around layouts the committee admits are suboptimal, under safety pressure they cannot vote on. If the exit of high-value contributors continues through the C++29 cycle, with EDG's expertise gone and no counterweight to Google's ramp-down, then the committee keeps the captive base while losing the talent that improves the product for them. If the promised open-sourcing of EDG's front end and a credible safety story retain contributors, the pool stabilizes. Over the next standard cycle the degrading path is likely (medium).

No remediation path is identified. The evidence records no specific concern to route to an existing mechanism, and the committee holds no instrument that gives the captive base a voice or lowers the exit cost that drives its contributors away. The absent mechanism is any direct channel between the governed base and the governance. (low-medium)

## 13. The Hollowing Bench: knowledge concentrates in an aging founding cohort while the safety coalition sits one defection from collapse.

Knowledge concentrates in an aging founding cohort while the safety coalition sits one defection from collapse. (low-medium) The mechanism joins a slow inflow to a brittle alliance. Authority sits with a founding and early cohort that admits newcomers slowly and transfers little, so each high-value departure removes an irreplaceable holder rather than rotating a seat. The same firms whose exit the safety plan cannot survive are the ones with the best alternatives. Concentration at rest, fragility under stress.

**Succession bifurcated rather than transferred.** The convener held the key relationships and authority personally for 23 years, and while a telegraphed handover to Guy Davidson took effect in January 2026, Herb Sutter retained the Foundation chair, the money, the infrastructure, and an author seat (Weber 1978). A real handover did occur and emeritus roles are a normal transition scaffold, which is the benign frame and a genuine prior counter-example. This instance differs in one measurable way: the gavel moved and the control of money and infrastructure did not, so power split in two rather than passing on (announced November 2025). (low-medium)

The inner circle admits newcomers slowly. Attendance grew to around 200 and the March 2026 meeting drew 24 first-timers, yet the Direction Group is by invitation, chairs serve at the convener's pleasure, and the principals are decades-tenured, against a base where 47 percent of past attendees appeared once and the participation half-life is nine to ten years (Lave and Wenger 1991). Long tenure in thankless specialized volunteer roles reflects a scarcity of willing experts, the reasonable reading. The structural point is that a modest inflow of guests does not replenish a long-tenured core, so the concentration hardens as the cohort ages. (low-medium)

That concentration is a cliff, not a slope (Rao and Argote 2006). Leadership clusters in a founding and early cohort, Bjarne Stroustrup from the language's creation and Sutter, Michael Wong, and Daveed Vandevoorde from the 2000s, and EDG's closure with John Spicer stepping back is a concrete near-term loss of embodied wording expertise. Cohort concentration reflecting a formation era is expected, and editor and emeritus transfer is under way. The finding is the timing: the losses are arriving now, softened only by a promise to open-source the EDG front end. (low-medium)

**The safety coalition is broad but brittle.** The C++29 safety plan rests on Direction Group unanimity plus the convener plus chair-holding employers plus national-body consensus, and a single major defection, a large national-body bloc or implementers refusing to build Profiles, could collapse it (Riker 1962). Broad double-consensus coalitions are generally more robust than minimum-winning ones, which holds this low. The fragility is real nonetheless because the coalition was kept intact by channeling the Safe C++ faction out rather than in, preserving unity by subtraction. (low)

Consensus plus divergent commercial stakes produces holdouts who wait each other out (Simcoe 2012). The [17-year Concepts voyage](https://www.vinniefalco.com/p/the-17-year-voyage-c-concepts), the [two-decade Contracts quest](https://www.vinniefalco.com/p/the-two-decade-quest-c-contracts), the Safe-C++-versus-Profiles standoff, and the frozen interface fight all show delay tracking distributional conflict rather than pure technical difficulty. Some delay is expected of any consensus body and Concepts and Contracts did eventually ship, so long gestation can reflect care. This instance differs because the current safety standoff runs against an external regulatory clock the earlier episodes never faced. (medium)

The orphaned base compounds all of it. Under existential safety threat the organized defenders are the incumbent chair-holding firms and the Foundation, while the roughly 16 million beneficiaries are unorganized and voiceless (Mayhew 1974). The corporate constituency does supply real defenders, so the body is only partly orphaned, and an open threat could mobilize the base. Absent that, the defense of C++ is conducted by the parties whose interests the safety fight most divides. (low-medium)

No actor homes here alone; the cliff's principals are examined where their leverage concentrates, Google in the Frozen ABI, the closing implementer EDG in the Route-Around and Borrowed Ground, and the channeled-out Safe C++ faction in the Capture Spine. If EDG's wording expertise leaves before it is transferred and a pivotal employer or national-body bloc defects over Profiles during the C++29 cycle, then a concentrated bench and a brittle coalition fail together and the safety plan stalls at the worst moment. If the open-sourced front end, the editor pipeline, and the double-consensus redundancy hold, the body absorbs the losses. Through C++29 the outcome is roughly an even chance either way (low-medium).

No remediation path is identified. The evidence records no specific concern to route to an existing mechanism, and no standing process binds succession of embodied knowledge or hardens the safety coalition against a single defection. The absent mechanism is a deliberate transfer of authority and expertise ahead of the cliff, not after it. (low-medium)

## 14. Other Findings

Three surviving findings joined no compound and stand on their own.

Senior members document drift from a coherent language toward feature accretion, with C++26 the largest expansion since C++11 and Stroustrup warning that a claimed ["minimal viable product" is "not minimal, it's not viable"](https://www.theregister.com/software/2018/06/18/whats-all-the-c-plus-fuss-bjarne-stroustrup-warns-of-dangerous-future-plans-for-his-c/1333048), echoing his own [Remember the Vasa](https://open-std.org/JTC1/SC22/WG21/docs/papers/2018/p0977r0.pdf) and "no shared taste" objections (Grimes et al. 2019). The core mission of shipping the standard still holds, so the drift is partial and toward accretion rather than away from the language. (low)

Reputation-sensitive partners could distance over documented associations, among them the CppCon safety matter surfaced in [Patricia Aas's transparency account](https://patricia.no/2022/03/08/cppcon.html), the Tomazos expulsion, and conduct disputes that drove participants to seek a managed chat service after accounts were deactivated (Jonsson, Greve and Fujiwara-Greve 2009). Documented codes of conduct exist and no sponsor-withdrawal cascade is evidenced. (medium-high)

WG21 converges on ISO peer form through mandatory Directives and professional norms even as it remains an outlier in scale and in retaining the ISO model most language communities reject (DiMaggio and Powell 1983). Conforming to the Directives is mandatory and the source of the body's legitimacy. (low)
