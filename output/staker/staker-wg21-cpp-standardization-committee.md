# Staker: WG21 (The C++ Standardization Committee)

**Funding and governance converge at the top, sixteen million users sit outside the room, and volunteers carry the work between them.**

WG21 is an unincorporated ISO/IEC working group that maintains the C++ standard through volunteer experts, corporate meeting hosts, and an adjacent trade-association foundation.

July 2026, by Vinnie Falco

---

## 1. Executive Summary

WG21 holds sole ISO authority over C++, a language used by roughly 16.3 million developers. It carries that mandate as an unpaid working group with no budget, no staff, and no reference implementation, shipping one standard every three years. A body this small governs a constituency this large through no formal channel between them. That asymmetry frames every finding below.

The dominant dynamic is the foundation interlock. The same small group holds the committee's offices, sets its priorities, and governs the private foundation that funds the work. The overlap is documented, published, and survived the 2025 succession intact. Concentration like this usually stays hidden, and here it is disclosed in full. No evidence shows the overlap has been turned against the standard. The interlock is fused roles, not proven capture.

WG21 is a commonweal body: it gives its one product away to everyone. The stated beneficiary is the global C++ community. The benefit that steers the standard lands closer in, on the seated, employer-funded participants and the foundation-centered layer around them. The gap is structural rather than corrupt, because no channel carries unrepresented users into the room. Users keep the free public good. What they lack is a hand on the wheel. (medium-high)

Internally the trajectory is stable to improving. The committee's self-diagnosed coherence deficit persists, managed but not reversed, which is the stable part. The improving part is structural. The long single-convener era ended in a clean succession to a shared team. The foundation has begun absorbing volunteer-run infrastructure the standard depends on. Both moves cut dependence on any single person. The inner ring holds. Its apex is no longer one seat.

The gravest trajectory runs outward, and the report names it the accelerating exit. Three external forces now align: memory-safety regulation, well-resourced language rivals, and a commercial safety-gap economy. Together they shrink the domain where unmodified C++ is mandated. As switching costs fall, the users most able to leave are routing around the committee instead of petitioning it. Exit is the unrepresented majority's one lever, and it is the lever now accelerating. Every other dynamic admits a benign reading. The exit does not.

One caution frames the assessment. Much of what reads as engineered concentration fits equally as volunteer accretion, where roles collect on the few people willing to carry them. The evidence rarely separates design from accident. Where the structural and benign readings finish level, the assessment keeps both rather than choosing one by preference. The confidence markers carry that doubt, not the prose.

## 2. The Organization

WG21 is officially ISO/IEC JTC1/SC22/WG21, a working group formed in 1990-91 inside the ISO/IEC standards hierarchy rather than an independent legal entity ([The Committee - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/std/the-committee)). It holds no budget and employs no one. It produces a single deliverable: the C++ International Standard, ISO/IEC 14882. The standard is now in its 7th edition (2024), revised on a fixed three-year cadence ([Business Plan and Convener's Report N5019 - open-std.org](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/n5019.pdf)). Without money or legal personhood, WG21 takes its shape from the parties that route resources through it. The committee is a process, not an institution.

WG21's charter names one job: develop and maintain ISO/IEC 14882, with high portability "among different operating systems and across a wide variety of computers" as the stated aim ([C++ - INCITS](https://www.incits.org/committees/pl22.16)). The mission is narrow. It commits to portability and compatibility, not to adoption or safety, and leaves the charter silent on the two pressures now reshaping the domain.

Roughly 200 experts from about 25 national bodies attend three week-long meetings a year. Technical work moves through a three-stage pipeline: Study Groups incubate, EWG and LEWG design, CWG and LWG finalize wording ([Meetings and Participation - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/std/meetings-and-participation/); [SD-4: WG21 Practices and Procedures - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-4-wg21-practices-and-procedures)). The 2026 officer corps is convener Guy Davidson, vice-conveners Nina Ranns and Jeff Garland, and secretary Braden Ganetsky, with Herb Sutter as convener emeritus. Authority formally rests with the national bodies, but the convener declares consensus by judgment, not a counted threshold. That single discretion places unusual weight on who holds the chair.

Classified by who benefits (Blau and Scott 1962), WG21 proper reads as a commonweal body. Its output is a public-good specification consumed by a global user base that never votes on it. Its immediate beneficiaries are the national bodies and employers who staff it, which lends strong mutual-benefit characteristics and makes the single label genuinely mixed. The adjacent Standard C++ Foundation is unambiguous, a U.S. 501(c)(6) trade association organized for the business conditions of C++ developers ([About - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/about)). The overlap in personnel and money is examined in the foundation interlock. A public-good specification leans on a business league for its funding. (medium-high)

No specific concern triggered this assessment. The governing request added a process constraint rather than naming a dispute. With no triggering issue, no single organizational mechanism for handling one comes into view. The assessment therefore maps the whole stakeholder field rather than adjudicating one fight.

## 3. The Landscape

### Market position

WG21 has no rival for authority over C++, because it is the sole ISO body for the language. Competition runs one level down, where C++ contends with C, Rust, Go, and managed languages for systems and performance-critical work ([CISA Memory Safe Languages Roadmap Analysis - safeguard.sh](https://safeguard.sh/resources/blog/cisa-memory-safe-languages-roadmap)). A monopoly on the specification is not a monopoly on developer attention. Mindshare is contestable even when authority is not.

The TIOBE popularity index ranked C++ second in early 2025 (about 11 percent) but fourth by January 2026 (8.67 percent), as C and C# passed it ([TIOBE Index - web.archive.org](https://web.archive.org/web/20250411043213/https://www.tiobe.com/tiobe-index/); [C# wins TIOBE 2025 - infoworld.com](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4112993/c-wins-tiobe-programming-language-of-the-year-honors-for-2025.html)). The user base grew over the same window, with SlashData estimating about 16.3 million C++ developers in 2025, up roughly 72 percent over three years ([SlashData global developer population 2025 - slashdata.co](https://www.slashdata.co/post/global-developer-population-trends-2025-how-many-developers-are-there)). The ranking slip reflects faster growth elsewhere, not contraction. The language's competitive position now moves faster than its three-year release cycle. (medium)

Ecma's TC39 ships JavaScript annually, and Rust ships every six weeks with editions about every three years. The sibling ISO group WG14 (C) revises on an irregular multi-year cycle ([The TC39 Process - tc39.es](https://tc39.es/process-document/); [How Rust is Made and Nightly Rust - doc.rust-lang.org](https://doc.rust-lang.org/stable/book/appendix-07-nightly-rust.html); [JTC1/SC22/WG14 - open-std.org](https://www.open-std.org/JTC1/SC22/wg14/)). WG21's three-year cadence sits at the slow end of that range, though it is standard for ISO language work. The cadence fits the process, not the competition.

### Ecosystem dependencies

Independent implementers realize the standard, not WG21 - GCC, Clang/LLVM, Microsoft's MSVC, and EDG - and their conformance lags publication by years. C++20, C++23, and C++26 support remained partial across vendors into 2026 ([C++ compiler support - cppreference.com](https://cppreference.com/cpp/compiler_support); [Microsoft C/C++ language conformance - learn.microsoft.com](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/cpp/overview/visual-cpp-language-conformance?view=msvc-170)). The committee can adopt a feature and still wait years for users to reach it. Publication is the midpoint of delivery, not the end.

Two dependencies sit entirely outside the committee's authority. The parent ISO/IEC body owns the copyright and sells the finished standard, so WG21 publishes its drafts free but cannot release the final text ([The Standard - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/std/the-standard/)). The Standard C++ Foundation funds the meeting hosting and infrastructure the working group has no budget to cover ([Financial Assistance Policy - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/about/financial-assistance-policy)). Experts contribute the labor too, as volunteers, most sponsored by employers and none paid to produce the standard. A specification built on borrowed time and hosted money is only as stable as those contributions.

### Domain-specific vulnerabilities

Since 2022, a cascade of U.S. government guidance has labeled C and C++ "memory-unsafe" and urged migration to memory-safe languages. The record includes the NSA's 2022 memory-safety sheet, the multi-agency 2023 "Case for Memory Safe Roadmaps," the 2024 White House ONCD report, and a June 2025 NSA/CISA bulletin ([Memory Safe Languages CSI 2025 - media.defense.gov](https://media.defense.gov/2025/Jun/23/2003742198/-1/-1/0/CSI_MEMORY_SAFE_LANGUAGES_REDUCING_VULNERABILITIES_IN_MODERN_SOFTWARE_DEVELOPMENT.PDF); [The Case for Memory Safe Roadmaps 2023 - media.defense.gov](https://media.defense.gov/2023/Dec/06/2003352724/-1/-1/0/THE-CASE-FOR-MEMORY-SAFE-ROADMAPS-TLP-CLEAR.PDF)). The EU Cyber Resilience Act adds phased obligations through 2027 ([Memory-Unsafe Code Is a Liability - corrode.dev](https://corrode.dev/blog/memory-safety/)). WG21 has institutionalized a response in study group SG23 (Safety and Security), but the pressure originates with regulators and government buyers, where a specification body holds no vote. Whether that guidance turns into developer departure is examined in the accelerating exit. When the buyer of last resort is a procurement rule, technical merit stops being the only currency. (medium)

## 4. the volunteer load-bearing walls

WG21 produces a global-scale standard with no budget, no staff, and no reference implementation. Its continuity rests on individuals it does not pay and cannot direct. The language's own creator records the arrangement in his peer-reviewed retrospective: "The members are all volunteers - there is no paid secretariat" ([Thriving in a Crowded and Changing World: C++ 2006-2020](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl20main-p5-p-bfc9cd4--final.pdf)). Absence, not design, does the load-bearing work here.

The mechanism is subtraction. A paid secretariat would carry the continuity functions - editing, citation hosting, meeting logistics - that instead fall to whoever volunteers. Leadership concedes such a secretariat will never exist (Pfeffer and Salancik 1978). The void is therefore permanent, not transitional. Every unfilled function becomes a named person's unpaid burden, held by someone the organization cannot direct because it does not employ them. What reads as staffing is a standing chain of personal favors. (medium)

The concentration is documented at the center of the process. A small editor team applies the standard's text to the draft, led by a single Project Editor, Thomas Koeppe, the person "ultimately responsible for applying committee-approved changes". The LaTeX sources sit in a public repository that makes some knowledge recoverable ([cplusplus/draft](https://github.com/cplusplus/draft)). Even so, the working practice of applying hundreds of merged edits each cycle is tacit and rides on the editor (Polanyi 1966). Volunteer infrastructure gravitates to the most dedicated hands, which is ordinary. A repository preserves the text, not the hands. (medium)

The reading surface of the standard sits outside the committee entirely. ISO paywalls the final text, so the community reads the draft through infrastructure no seated member built or controls. One individual maintains the main HTML rendering, eel.is/c++draft, which is explicitly "not an ISO publication" ([Draft C++ Standard (eel.is/c++draft)](https://eel.is/c++draft/)). Historical revisions rely on the timsong-cpp mirror ([timsong-cpp/cppwp](https://github.com/timsong-cpp/cppwp)), paper citations resolve through the personally run redirector wg21.link ([wg21.link - WG21 redirect service](https://wg21.link/)), and code experimentation runs on Compiler Explorer ([compiler-explorer](https://github.com/mattgodbolt/compiler-explorer)). These maintainers hold a positional indispensability the register does not formally recognize. Concentration on committed volunteers is normal, and cppreference has since moved under Foundation hosting ([cppreference.com update announcement](https://isocpp.org/blog/2026/04/announcement-cppreference.com-update)), closing one gap. The rest remain one person deep. (medium-high)

Beyond individuals, the body cannot readily replace its suppliers. ISO is the sole publisher of the standard. The Standard C++ Foundation became the dominant meeting funder, "instrumental in keeping WG21 meetings going" by covering three of the first four post-pandemic meetings ([Standard C++ Foundation Annual Report FY2024](https://isocpp.org/blog/2024/10/standard-cpp-foundation-annual-report-for-fiscal-year-2024)). Reliance on ISO is inherent to an ISO working group, and shared leadership softens the Foundation tie, yet neither supplier has a standing substitute (Chopra and Sodhi 2004). A sole supplier need not act against a buyer to constrain it. The trend is a slow external degrade. (medium)

The output also passes through gates WG21 does not own. ISO publication, national-body sponsorship, Foundation-run web and repository infrastructure, and each implementer's decision to ship or withhold a feature are all discretionary chokepoints (Areeda 1990). The gates are rule-governed and outright denial is rare, which keeps the dependency tolerable rather than acute. An unclosed gate still decides who passes. (medium)

WG21 operates inside a platform it cannot alter. It cannot change the ISO/IEC Directives, cannot publish unilaterally, and does not keep its own output. ISO holds the copyright and sells the finished standard for about USD 277, while the committee gives its near-identical working drafts away free. The ISO relationship confers legitimacy and global reach, and cost-recovery pricing is stable rather than predatory, so the exposure stays latent (Rochet and Tirole 2003). A tenant improves property it will never own. (low-medium)

The beneficiaries are the readers. The broad end-user developer community consumes the free drafts and the community-run reading surfaces at no cost. The committee credits industry investment in C++ for the growth of its participation pool. The cost lands on the unpaid maintainers who carry continuity without title, budget, or backup. The distribution is structurally regressive: the least-recognized contributors absorb the most operational risk. (medium)

Power here runs on the absence of alternatives, not on authority. No maintainer commands anything, yet each could halt a function by simply stopping, the defining trait of a single point of failure. No coalition binds them - they are independent volunteers who largely do not coordinate. The one shared fault line is succession, since none of these roles carries a documented backup. The advantage sits with whoever cannot be replaced. (medium)

The absent full-time secretariat is a stakeholder defined by its vacancy. It has no office, roster entry, or name, because it does not exist and, by leadership's own account, never will. Staffed, its interest would be continuity - the administrative spine that funded standards bodies take for granted. What it wants is moot. What its absence produces is the entire dependency structure above it. A negative space can still bear load. (medium)

The unofficial standard-infrastructure maintainers are the people and services behind the standard's reading and citation layer - eel.is/c++draft, timsong-cpp, wg21.link, Compiler Explorer, and cppreference. Their formal role is none, since the committee neither seats nor pays nor tasks them. Their power base is expert (French and Raven 1959): they supply the scarce skill of turning a paywalled specification into a usable free surface. What they appear to want is the health of the ecosystem they serve, not control over it. The dependency has never been tested. Their trajectory improves slowly as the Foundation absorbs individual pieces. Indispensable and unseated is an unstable pairing. (medium-high)

ISO/IEC, operating through JTC1 and SC22, is the platform owner and the only actor in this dossier with formal dominance. Its role is publisher and rule-setter: it owns the copyright, prices the standard, and defines the Directives WG21 must follow. Its power base is legitimate, reinforced by reward and coercive control over publication. What it wants is a financially self-sustaining standards system, funded by the very sales that WG21's free drafts undercut. Its parent-body governance belongs to the inner ring. Here it is only the landlord. The owner is not the maintainer. (medium)

The trajectory is mixed but tilts toward fragility. Internal bus-factor risk is easing as the Foundation absorbs pieces of the infrastructure, while the external single-source and gatekeeper dependencies slowly degrade. If the load-bearing roles - project editor, eel.is, wg21.link, cppreference - and the meeting-host pipeline stay unfunded and unformalized, then a single departure or funding lapse can disrupt the standard's production or reading surface within one cycle. If instead the Foundation or ISO formalizes and funds them, the fragility eases, a shift already partly underway (medium horizon, medium confidence: the bus-factor is documented but no failure has yet occurred).

An existing mechanism partly covers the void: the Foundation's infrastructure funding and its financial-assistance policy, which recently brought cppreference under hosting. Adequacy is partial, since the policy rescues assets after the fact rather than securing them in advance. The specific absent mechanism is documented succession and funded, named maintainer positions for the load-bearing roles, feasible within the Foundation's current budget. The same Foundation money that eases the internal fragility also deepens the external single-source dependency, so mending one finding worsens another. Redundancy costs less than recovery. (medium)

Who fills the void this model leaves is the work of the foundation interlock.

## 5. the foundation interlock

WG21's leaders also fund and govern it. The same small group holds the committee's offices, sets its priorities through the Direction Group, and controls the Standard C++ Foundation that pays for the work. The overlap is documented and openly published. Its harm is contested, not demonstrated. No instrument separates the roles when they collide.

The interlock runs on a dependency the committee cannot avoid. WG21 has no paid secretariat, the void examined in the volunteer load-bearing walls. Meeting hosting, hardship travel, and proposal support therefore lean on Foundation money and on infrastructure the Foundation owns. Foundation financial-assistance approvals require a majority of serving WG21 officers. Money reaches the committee through people seated on both sides. The officer overlap is not a byproduct of the funding. It is the conduit the funding uses.

Begin with the board. The Standard C++ Foundation board is substantially the WG21 officer corps. The convener emeritus chairs it, a sitting vice-convener is its vice-president, and its treasurer and one director sit on the Direction Group. Because grant approval needs an officer majority, the same officers approve their own foundation's grants, a self-approval structure (Davis 1982) that disclosure records but cannot dissolve. Every role is named in public rosters, and no bylaw forbids the overlap. Disclosure is the only mitigation on offer, and disclosure is not separation. The conflict is structural, not alleged.

A stronger reading circulates: that the overlap operates as a coordinated patronage network run from one chair. That characterization rests on a single interested source, and this assessment does not adopt it. The documented facts need no such gloss. Officers approving their own foundation's grants is on the record. The record is enough.

The board also renews itself from within. The [3 November 2025 board minutes](https://isocpp.org/files/Meeting-Minutes/Standard-C++-Foundation-25-11-03-Board-Meeting-Minutes.pdf) show a self-perpetuating board. One person, Herb Sutter, holds both chair and chief-executive roles, with no recorded case of the directors overruling the executive. Self-perpetuating boards are the statutory baseline for a 501(c)(6) trade association (Tillotson and Tropman 2025), so the form alone is ordinary. What exceeds the baseline is the fusion of chair and CEO in one person with no documented check. Board capture here describes a structure, not an intent. (low-medium)

The Foundation casts no vote, and its influence still exceeds that formal zero. Through grants, travel, and hosting, through control of isocpp.org and CppCon, and through personnel shared with the deciding officers, it exercises institutional capture (Glaeser 2002), influence beyond formal standing. Influence proportional to funding is expected in a sponsor-dependent body, and adoption authority still rests with independently voting national bodies, so the vector is transparent and bounded. The losing side's own funder affirmed the one outcome open to test, the memory-safety direction, as sound on the merits. That forecloses any reading of a captured decision. The finding is an influence vector, not a captured outcome. (medium)

Priority-setting lives in a second body. The Direction Group is chartered and public, its output explicitly non-binding, and design groups are "strongly encouraged to give weight" to its opinions. Its current membership, listed in [P2000R5](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p2000r5.pdf), overlaps the Foundation board through Stroustrup and Wong, so funding and priority-setting share personnel as well as purpose. Chartered and non-binding is not the same as inert. An informal institution can steer without a vote (Helmke and Levitsky 2004), and the plenary here largely ratifies priorities shaped upstream. The word to resist is hidden. The group is public, and only its influence is informal. (low-medium)

The arrangement rewards the center and taxes the edge. Officers gain funded meetings, a community platform they control, and a priority process tuned to their own reading of the language. Corporate sponsors and funded participants gain a channel faster than volunteer time can match. The cost lands on participants who rely on the formal process and on the seatless user base examined in the unrepresented majority. Beneficiary and payer are both visible. They are simply not the same people.

Internally the interlock is a cohesive axis, not a loose alliance. Funding, priority-setting, and office bind through the same people, and that axis both originated and carried the winning safety direction. Its fault lines are few and specific. The convener's independence is untested, and the joints holding the structure are individuals rather than committees. Shared people make it cohesive and fragile at once.

The Standard C++ Foundation is a Washington 501(c)(6) trade association formed in 2012, reporting [FY2025 revenue of $2,102,990 and zero full-time employees](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/461356355). Its legal form is small and its reach is not. It funds meeting hosting, hardship travel, and proposal work, and it owns the community's main web presence and its flagship conference. Its stated purpose is to support the C++ developer community, and it presents itself as separate from the committee. The board overlap is why that separation holds only on paper. What it wants is a durable ecosystem and a continued central role. Both are within reach.

Herb Sutter convened WG21 from 2002 to 2025 and now carries the title of convener emeritus while chairing the Foundation as its chief executive. In 2024 he left Microsoft for [Citadel Securities](https://www.businessinsider.com/why-citadel-securities-hired-coding-expert-microsoft-herb-sutter-2024-11), and his reported Foundation compensation is $40,650. His power was never the office. It was expertise, name recognition, and two decades of relationships, none of which lapsed with the handover. He wants the Profiles safety direction to hold and his central role preserved. The office changed hands. The rest did not.

The officer corps turned over on schedule. Guy Davidson became convener on 1 January 2026, with Nina Ranns and Jeff Garland as vice-conveners and Braden Ganetsky as secretary. Davidson entered WG21 in 2015 and climbed to its highest office, a genuine upward path a closed core would not allow. His stated mandate is continuity, and continuity is exactly the unresolved question. Its independence from the arrangement it inherited is untested.

Nina Ranns concentrates roles that usually sit apart: Foundation vice-president, WG21 vice-convener, former committee secretary, and chair of SG22, the C and C++ liaison group. She is the clearest single thread tying the prior arrangement to the new leadership. Her expertise is concrete, including compiler work implementing C++26 contracts, so the roles rest on contribution, not sponsorship alone. Whether that concentration reads as efficiency or entrenchment turns on the convener question above. She is the joint that bears the load.

Bjarne Stroustrup appears here in one facet only: Foundation treasurer and Direction Group member. His fuller profile belongs to the later dossiers on language direction. The relevant fact here is narrow and heavy. The creator of C++ keeps a hand on the Foundation's money and a seat where priorities are set. Legitimacy and funding meet in one chair.

The trajectory is a formal handover around a stable core. The convenership transferred cleanly under ISO process, a more orderly succession (Weber 1978) than most volunteer bodies achieve. Yet per the [November 2025 Kona trip report](https://herbsutter.com/2025/11/10/trip-report-november-2025-iso-c-standards-meeting-kona-usa/), the departing convener kept the Foundation chair and its infrastructure, so authority moved into the office while influence stayed with the person who vacated it. The formal picture improved. The informal one did not. (low-medium)

The base widens while the core holds. Attendance runs near [200 and rising](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/n5031.pdf), with roughly 30 subgroups drawing in new experts, yet the leadership core rotates slowly (Lave and Wenger 1991). Newcomers still climb, and Davidson's rise from a 2015 entrant to convener is the standing proof. Slow rotation at the top is not a closed shop. It is a narrow ladder above a wide floor. (low-medium)

If the national bodies and the new convener leave the officer and Foundation overlap and the officer-approved grant structure unchanged, the informal Foundation-centered layer persists intact through the succession (short-to-medium horizon, medium confidence, because the structure already survived one handover unchanged). If grant approval is separated from serving WG21 officers, the interlock weakens (low near-term probability, low-medium confidence).

The existing mechanism is disclosure plus the Foundation's "not the committee" framing. It informs, and it does not constrain, because no independent enforcer exists and grants remain approved by serving WG21 officers. The specific absent mechanism is a recusal or independent-director rule on Foundation grant decisions, or a clean split of grant approval from the WG21 officer corps. Either fits inside the Foundation's current 501(c)(6) form and its existing board. The gap is not resources. The gap is will.

## 6. the inner ring

A stable inner group sets WG21's direction. It has done so through one convener for roughly 23 years, a by-invitation priority group, and appointed chairs. Against elected peers this is a genuine outlier. Against its ISO sibling it is ordinary. The concentration is visible in every published roster and forbidden by no rule. What the record lacks is any elective path into the group that steers the language. Direction here is inherited, not contested.

The mechanism is pre-shaping rather than decree. SD-4 describes the Direction Group as "a small by-invitation group of experienced participants who are asked to recommend priorities for WG21," and that recommendation frames the agenda the plenary later ratifies. The convener then determines whether consensus exists, and chairs decide which proposals reach a room at all. Each step is advisory or procedural on paper. Stacked together, they let a small group set what the committee weighs before any vote occurs. Agenda control precedes consensus.

Herb Sutter held the convenership from 2002 through the end of 2025, roughly 23 years apart from a one-year hiatus. Even the outgoing convener characterized such long tenure as unusual. The by-invitation Direction Group and the [convener-appointed chairs](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-3-study-group-organizational-information) complete the pattern of a durable inner group controlling agenda and succession under open participation - the iron law of oligarchy (Michels 1911). The deviation is measurable against peers. [WG14 caps its convener at a three-year ISO term](https://www.incits.org/news-events/news-coverage/isoiec-jtc-1sc-22-programming-languages-highlights), [TC39 elects its chair group to twelve-month terms](https://github.com/tc39/how-we-work/blob/main/management.md), and [Python re-elects its steering council annually](https://peps.python.org/pep-0013/). None runs a single leader for two decades. Scarce and unevenly held expertise explains the long tenure. It does not explain the absent contest for the seat.

Direction also concentrates at the point of decision. National bodies cast No votes, and open debate shows others forming independent judgments. Even so, the initiative to set priorities and to declare consensus rests with the convener and the by-invitation Direction Group (Dahl 1957). The [SG23 and EWG safety-profiles direction poll](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2023/p2816r0.pdf) shows the pattern working as chartered - a priority argued in the open, decided on merit, with dissent recorded. Concentration of initiative is not concentration of judgment. (low-medium)

Change needs assent from several actors at once, which makes the inner ring's settlements hard to disturb. A negative bloc of roughly one-quarter of national bodies can sink a revision even when two-thirds support it. The recent [committee-draft ballot](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/n5028.pdf) drew No votes from Ireland, Japan, and Korea. Implementers hold a second veto by declining to ship: [export templates](https://cplusplus.github.io/CWG/issues/820.html) was standardized, built by only one vendor, [recommended for removal in 2003](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2003/n1426.pdf), and later struck. Chairs hold a third through the schedule. These are veto players (Tsebelis 2002). The committee cannot easily replace the parties who hold them. Implementers, employers, and ISO each supply something WG21 has no substitute for, the source of their power (Emerson 1962). Multiple veto players are a deliberate stability feature of consensus standards, and here they also freeze whatever the inner group settles. (medium)

The parties the standard governs also help write it. Employees of the compiler and library vendors the standard binds chair the design and wording groups. The current roster lists NVIDIA, Microsoft, and Red Hat/IBM staff in senior chairs, and corporate sponsors fund the experts who attend. When the committee declined a large ABI break at Prague in 2020, the outcome protected the vast installed codebases those same firms maintain. Practitioner authorship is the ordinary and often the only competent way to write technical rules. It also lets the regulated shape the rules that bind them, which is regulatory capture in its mild structural form (Stigler 1971). Author and incumbent are not always separable here. (low-medium)

Priority-setting runs on channels outsiders cannot see. Decision-relevant discussion sits on membership-gated reflector lists and an invite-only wiki. That leaves the broad user base with only published outputs, an information asymmetry (Akerlof 1970) about what the committee is weighing. Self-correction is largely internal as well, since the committee's own officers govern the body that funds the work, a loop examined in the foundation interlock. The external check is real but narrow. ISO national-body ballots genuinely gate adoption, as the No votes above show, yet they never reach which proposals get prioritized (Ashby 1956). The loop mostly checks itself. (low-medium)

Access to the committee is brokered at chokepoints. In the United States, INCITS gates participation through national-body sponsorship. In late 2024 that lever removed a contributor after his sponsor withdrew over a dispute about a paper's title, a sequence documented in [his own first-hand account](https://tomazos.com/ub_question_incident.pdf). Chairs gate the proposal funnel by the same logic, deciding what gets heard. These positions are structural holes (Burt 1992), controlling the flow between otherwise disconnected parties. The removal was conduct-linked and these gatekeeping roles are required ISO functions, yet the same positions that keep order also decide who participates at all. Control of a doorway is control of a room. (low-medium)

The arrangement rewards the incumbents inside the ring. Long-tenured leaders, Direction Group members, and vendor-employed chairs keep the initiative to shape both the language and the schedule. The vendors gain a standard that protects their installed base and contains their implementation cost. Those who pay are the participants without a corporate sponsor and the far larger body of C++ developers who never enter the room. They receive the priorities the inner group sets and hold no elective route to change who decides. Benefit tracks position, not contribution. (medium)

Inside the ring the dependencies run in a chain. The convener and chairs hold formal authority, yet they depend on implementers to make features real and on employers to fund attendance. That dependence hands those parties influence without a vote. Coalitions gather around the largest employers, several of which hold more than one senior chair, and the fault line separates the sponsored core from the unsponsored periphery. The Direction Group is the broker, sitting between the convener's authority and the plenary's ratification and pre-shaping what passes between them. Brokerage, not command, is how the ring holds. (medium-high)

**WG21 Direction Group.** A by-invitation body that recommends the committee's priorities, currently Daveed Vandevoorde (2026 chair), Jeff Garland, Paul McKenney, Roger Orr, Bjarne Stroustrup, and Michael Wong. Its salience is definitive (Mitchell, Agle and Wood 1997). It carries expert and referent power rather than a formal vote. It is a proxy for the officer corps that appoints into it. It wants a coherent language and the standing to steer toward one, and it gains agenda influence at little risk, since membership is not contestable from outside. Its position holds steady even as the convener seat changes hands.

**WG21 subgroup chairs.** The operational layer that runs the three-stage pipeline: EWG under Erich Keane (NVIDIA), LEWG under Inbal Levi (Microsoft), CWG under Jens Maurer, LWG under Jonathan Wakely (Red Hat/IBM), and ARG under Vandevoorde. Their salience is dominant. Their authority is legitimate and their standing expert. They decide what gets scheduled and therefore what advances. Appointed by the convener and not elected, they answer upward rather than to a constituency.

**National Bodies.** The ISO members that hold the only binding votes, split into voting P-members and observing O-members. Their salience is definitive. Their power is legitimate and coercive, since a bloc of roughly one-quarter can block a revision. They want a standard that serves their domestic constituencies, and they wield the one check the inner ring cannot pre-shape. Their veto is real but blunt. It gates adoption, not the agenda.

**Major compiler and standard-library implementers.** The vendors whose toolchains decide what "C++" means in practice - Microsoft, the GCC and Clang ecosystems, and EDG. Their salience is definitive. Their power is expert and coercive. Their position is a gatekeeper's. The veto by non-implementation is homed here. The fuller profile, including the exit pressure they face, belongs to the accelerating exit. What matters for the inner ring is that they can nullify a decision without casting a vote.

**INCITS / INCITS-C++.** The United States Technical Advisory Group that administers American participation and the route to a vote. Its salience is dominant. Its power is legitimate and coercive. Its role is that of an intermediary between contributors and ISO. It holds the sponsorship lever described above. Its gate is administrative, and an administrative gate still opens and closes.

The visible seat is diffusing while the machinery holds. Shared convenership arrived in 2026, with Guy Davidson as convener alongside two vice-conveners, which spreads the ceremonial role. But the by-invitation Direction Group and the appointment of chairs are unchanged. If chair appointment and Direction Group selection stay non-elective, then agenda concentration in the inner ring persists regardless of who holds the convener seat (medium horizon, medium confidence - the machinery is structural rather than personal). If elective selection or term limits are adopted, the concentration diffuses.

The existing mechanism is ISO-standard convener appointment plus annual rotation of the Direction Group chair, which supplies rotation without contestability. The specific absent mechanism is elective selection of chairs or the Direction Group, or fixed term limits, of the kind TC39 and Python already run. Both are feasible within WG21's current governance form and membership size. Neither has been culturally welcome. Rotation moves the chair. Election would move the power.

## 7. the coherence deficit

WG21 diagnoses its own coherence problem in unusually stark terms and cannot own or reverse it. Its Direction papers call the loss of shared aims "possibly the most dangerous problem we face as a committee," a verdict leadership has restated across successive revisions without closing the gap it names. The drift is stable and self-aware. Where the cost lands is the committee's reputational and human capital, not the diagnosis that identifies it.

The mechanism is a loop the committee cannot exit from inside. Self-diagnosed drift appears first as effort diffusing across an expanding proposal surface. That drift resists correction because the coherence failure dissolves into consensus polling, where no single party owns the aggregate. The fixed release deadline then admits partly finished features, and each increment of accretion draws down the credibility the standard trades on. Cause and cost move in opposite directions: the drift is stable while the capital it consumes is degrading. (medium-high)

The clearest evidence is the committee's own language. The WG21 Direction Group was chartered because C++ was "in danger of losing coherency due to proposals based on differing and sometimes mutually contradictory design philosophies" ([P0939R2](https://open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2019/p0939r2.pdf)), and a later revision states plainly, "We have no shared aims, no shared taste" ([P2000R3](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2022/p2000r3.pdf)). A member paper reaches the same conclusion: "A body comprised of hundreds of individuals cannot function without a unifying mission" ([C++ Should Be C++](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2023/p3023r1.html)). Much of the expansion tracks genuine industry demand, and the same leadership actively counters it. Yet mission drift (Grimes et al. 2019) that outlives its own correction machinery is the finding. An alarm raised by the people best placed to act on it, and left ringing, is stronger evidence than the same alarm raised from outside.

Effort has thinned as the surface has grown. The committee now runs nearly 30 subgroups and circulates mailings exceeding 100 papers, and by 2018 the convener already recorded, "We used to have less papers than people, but that is no longer the case" ([P1338R1](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2019/p1338r1.pdf)). Per-group backlogs of roughly 100 papers ration the scarcest input in the system, which is senior review attention rather than proposal supply. Expansion that tracks real growth in the C++ user base is expected, but the diffusion of attention away from the coherence mission is the agency cost (Jensen and Meckling 1976) the committee's own papers describe. (low-medium)

The three-year release cadence sets a target, and targets invite gaming. Features can ship "80% ready" to make a release train, a risk the Direction Group's own immediate-direction paper flags. Completing three finished proposals beats reaching the deadline with six left unfinished ([P5000R1](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p5000r1.pdf)). The cadence was a deliberate and successful reform that replaced the 13-year gap between C++98 and C++11. This cycle deferred the profiles-enforcement attribute rather than shipping it raw. When the deadline becomes the measure of progress it stops measuring readiness, which is Goodhart's law (Goodhart 1984) operating on a calendar. (low-medium)

No individual is accountable for the coherence of the whole. Stroustrup's own history of the process calls it "design by a confederation of committees" and records that he finds it "amazing that anything constructive ever emerges". Consequential outcomes such as incoherence and feature bloat resolve through consensus polling that distributes each decision until responsibility for the aggregate belongs to no one. Diffusing consensus is the deliberate design of a standards body and a guard against single-point capture, but a guard against capture is also a guard against ownership. The outcome is an accountability sink (Davies 2024). The structure that stops any one actor from seizing the language also stops any one actor from steering it back toward coherence. (medium)

Formal authority and operating reality have drifted apart. On paper, plenary consensus and sovereign national-body votes govern, and national bodies do cast real No votes. In practice, priorities are set through Direction-Group guidance and through national sponsorship that can be pro forma. The formal structure still governs final adoption, so it is not ceremony. But the distance between who formally decides and who effectively sets the agenda is a decoupling (Meyer and Rowan 1977). It leaves the coherence mission owned by an advisory layer and enforced by no one. (low-medium)

Surface metrics hold steady while two forms of capital erode beneath them. Attendance sits near 200 and paper volume is up. But C++ slid on the TIOBE index from second in early 2025 to fourth by early 2026 as C and C# climbed past it. That slide is a proxy for the memory-safety credibility now under government pressure. Human capital erodes alongside it. Niall Douglas, a long-time library contributor, announced a move to the sibling C committee with the assessment that "WG21 broke a whole generation of us." The accelerating exit reads that kind of departure as a pattern rather than an isolated case. Participation is growing, and a hardened standard library lands in C++26, which cuts against a pure-decline reading. But rising headcount paired with falling mindshare and senior burnout is the signature of capital consumption (Mises 1949). The gains from added features accrue to their authors and sponsors. The volunteer base and the end-user C++ developer community bear the drawdown. (medium)

Internal to the dynamic, three actors hold the coherence brief and none holds the lever. The Direction Group, the by-invitation senior panel also central to the inner ring, carries the explicit charge to defend design coherence through the P0939, P2000, and P5000 papers. But its authority is advisory and its recommendations are non-binding. The WG21 convener and officer corps own the machinery that could enforce coherence, the release cadence and the consensus calls. Yet they run the process rather than direct its content. The fault line separates the coherence defenders from the feature adders, and it runs through the same rooms, because many senior members occupy both roles at once.

Bjarne Stroustrup holds the sharpest version of the contradiction. As the language's creator he carries a personal authority no title confers, and he has spent it on the coherence problem. His "Remember the Vasa" paper warns the committee is "on a path to disaster through enthusiasm and design-by-committee" ([Remember the Vasa](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2018/p0977r0.pdf)), and in public he called the accumulating proposals "insanity to the point of endangering the future of C++." The committee hears him and proceeds anyway, and his warnings have not slowed feature volume. That locates the coherence deficit in the structure rather than in any missing voice. A founder who cannot brake the body he founded is the plainest measure of how diffuse the accountability has become.

The trajectory follows paper volume and subgroup count, both still rising, against a prioritization gate that stays advisory. If the surface keeps expanding without a binding intake or prioritization gate, the self-diagnosed drift continues and reputational capital keeps eroding under safety pressure. If P5000-style discipline is made binding, the drift slows. The cost side is already degrading, which is why the first branch is the more likely one over the medium term. (medium-high)

The correction machinery already exists and is inadequate by its own design. The Direction Group plus P5000 prioritization is the standing mechanism, but both are advisory, so the drift they name persists. The absent piece is enforcement: a binding intake or prioritization gate, or a hard per-subgroup paper budget. Either is feasible within the committee's current governance without new ISO authority. The diagnosis is in hand. The binding remedy is not.

## 8. the unrepresented majority

The C++ user base holds no governance seat and no removable proxy. About 16.3 million developers bear the consequences of standard decisions, yet their interests reach the committee only through parties that answer to someone else. A member direction paper, C++ Should Be C++, presses the internal case that the standard underserves the "average programmer." The gap is the defining structural weakness in the committee's stakeholder landscape, a plain representation gap (Young 2000). No standards body can seat an atomized global user base directly, and employers, national bodies, and implementers represent users indirectly. The indirection is the whole problem, because those proxies represent their own interests first. A constituency this size with no direct channel is not a rounding error. It is the structure working as built. (medium-high)

The mechanism is representation by proxies the represented cannot remove. Three substitutes stand in for the base, each facing a different direction. National bodies and employers carry user interests as a byproduct of pursuing their own. The Direction Group claims the community's voice by appointment. Independent educators shape user sentiment from outside the committee entirely. The base does not appoint these proxies, does not instruct them, and cannot recall them. A proxy that cannot be fired answers to its own interests and wears the label of the principal's. One instrument remains that no proxy controls: exit.

The proxies assert the mandate in their own words. The Direction Group, a small by-invitation advisory body, states in its [Direction for ISO C++](https://open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2018/p0939r0.pdf) papers that it speaks for "the interests of the larger C++ community." The claim is self-verifying, because the group makes it about itself. Appointed expert bodies are a legitimate, common governance form whose advice binds no one. Even so, the community cannot authorize, instruct, or unseat the group that claims its voice, representation without authorization (Pitkin 1967). Legitimacy asserted downward from appointment is not legitimacy granted upward from the represented. The proxy is real. Its accountability runs nowhere near the base. (medium)

Participants decide the standard, and the base absorbs the result. The people in the room are the agents. The diffuse user base is the principal (Eisenhardt 1989). Monitoring runs one way and weakly, since the base cannot watch most decisions and holds no sanction short of departure. Employer-funded participants carry a standing incentive to weight their sponsors' interests. Technical standards genuinely require expert agents to decide for a lay base, and exit remains a slow, blunt monitor. The gap is designed in, not willed. (medium)

The funding model runs on cross-subsidy (Faulhaber 1975). Employers pay for participant time, corporate hosts absorb meeting costs that [SD-5](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-5-meeting-information) says fall almost always on "corporate sponsors," and the Standard C++ Foundation funds hosting, travel, and grants. The mass user base pays nothing and consumes the free working drafts. Cross-subsidy is the deliberate, openly documented arrangement that makes an unfunded global base possible at all. Its distributive signature is concentrated benefits and diffuse costs (Wilson 1980). The parties who fund the work are the parties who sit, and the base that consumes the output has no seat to fund. Whoever pays for the room tends to arrange the furniture. (medium)

Some captured value runs past any strict contribution measure - rent appropriation (Coff 1999). Two instruments show it: ABI-stability policy that shields large sponsor codebases, and the isocpp.org and CppCon platform the Foundation controls. Upstream, ISO owns the copyright and sells the finished text, which [ISO/IEC 14882](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/iso/isoiec148822020) lists in the low hundreds of dollars. The working group gives its drafts away for free. Those who fund the work do reasonably earn proportional influence, so the reading is contingent rather than damning. The ABI advantage is already eroding as lock-in weakens and the domain that demands it shrinks. Capture here is real but not durable. (low-medium)

The ledger is asymmetric in a specific way. Funded, seated interests receive monetization, feature influence, and a durable platform. The base receives a free, portable, backward-compatible language and no say over where it goes. That transfer is not trivial, since a public good delivered at zero price to millions of developers is a real subsidy flowing the other way. What the base cannot obtain at any price is a mechanism to contest the terms. Benefit without voice is the trade this dynamic makes. (medium)

Inside the dynamic, only one side is organized. The funded bloc is seated, coordinated, and interlocked with the Standard C++ Foundation and the officer corps - the wiring that the foundation interlock and the inner ring trace. The base is atomized by construction: millions of individuals with no membership body, no delegate, and no treasury. Between them sit the two proxies that claim the base's voice directly, pointing opposite ways. The Direction Group speaks down from authority, and the educators speak out to the crowd. No broker carries the base's interest upward under a mandate. The fault line separates those who fund and sit from those who use and watch. One side is a bloc. The other is a crowd.

The end-user C++ developer community is the dependent stakeholder at the center of this dynamic: a legitimate, urgent claim paired with almost no power. It runs to roughly 16.3 million developers across game engines, embedded systems, finance, automotive, and defense. Its formal governance role is none. Its power base is referent and diffuse, since the language matters only because people use it, and it acts only through slow collective adoption or abandonment. It wants a stable, performant, backward-compatible language and free access to the specification. It loses the most when the standard drifts from working practice, and it holds no lever to correct that drift except leaving. Rarely is a constituency this large also this powerless. (medium)

Independent educators are the informal proxy that fills the void, and they answer to no one either. [C++ Weekly](https://articles.emptycrate.com/training.html) from Jason Turner, [The Cherno](https://thecherno.com/), [Kate Gregory](http://gregcons.com/Training.aspx), [Meeting C++](https://www.meetingcpp.com/), and the r/cpp forum shape how the base reads committee decisions. Their formal role is none, and their power base is referent, built on audience trust rather than any seat. They want a healthy language to teach and an audience that needs teaching, which leaves them a mild interest in the language staying complex enough to require explanation. Popular educators surround every language and mostly just teach, so this is informal reach and not a claimed mandate. When the base forms a view on a feature, it often forms it here. The widest-reaching proxy answers to no one. (medium)

The largest users shape the standard mostly by staying out of it. Very large C++ shops consume the portability guarantee while sending few or no participants and keeping their own house rules. Epic Games is the clearest case. Its Unreal Engine carries on the order of 16 million lines of C++ and mandates a recent standard, yet keeps its own coding standard and avoids much of the standard library. EA's Frostbite and Amazon's AWS are weaker instances of the same posture. Their power base is latent reward power, held in reserve, because participation at their scale would command attention they choose not to spend. Free-riding on a public good is ordinary, and this non-participation is inferred from absence rather than stated in any record. Absence, read closely, is itself a position. (medium)

The corporate employer-sponsor bloc is the dominant stakeholder on the funding side, and its benefit-capture and subsidy face lives here. It combines the purse, the seat, and the expertise - it pays for participation, seats accredited experts through national bodies, and supplies deep technical command. It wants its large C++ investments protected and its costs reduced, a rational and openly stated aim. It gains monetization-adjacent influence, ABI protection, and a recruiting-and-visibility platform. It stands to lose that edge as the safety debate wears down ABI lock-in. Its fuller profile spans the foundation interlock and the inner ring, so only the distributive face sits here. The bloc funds the commons and sets its terms. (medium)

The void is stable, and the exit lever is already firing. Awareness of the arrangement is not spreading evenly - it concentrates among the exit-capable, the large and well-resourced users able to act on it. Rising awareness therefore converts into departure rather than internal pressure, because the people who see the gap most clearly are the people who can most easily leave. Awareness that concentrates among the exit-capable is the exclusion side of the committee's central tension. The response side, where partial exit hardens into strategy, is the accelerating exit, taken up next. If no structured user channel is created, the base stays represented only by proxies it cannot remove and voices dissent by leaving (medium horizon, medium confidence, because the void is stable and exit is already visible). If a structured feedback mechanism emerges, voice partially substitutes for exit. Awareness here does not build pressure. It books an exit.

The existing remedy is the proxy chain itself - national bodies and employers nominally carry user interests. Its adequacy is weak by construction, because those proxies answer to their own members and mandates, not to the base. The specific absent mechanism is a structured, non-voting user-feedback channel. It would be an open comment process on working drafts with a required committee response, kept separate from the ballot and from the proxy. Peer standards and language bodies run analogs, so the design is feasible inside the committee's current form, budget, and membership. It would not hand the base a vote. It would hand the base a documented answer. Not a vote, but a record.

## 9. the accelerating exit

One dynamic in this analysis resists the two-sided reading applied to every other. The committee holds sole authority over C++ and still depends on outside implementers, funders, ISO, and national bodies it cannot direct. Meanwhile three external forces - regulators, well-resourced rivals, and a commercial safety-gap - now push in one direction at once. Every finding gathered here degrades, and their convergence is what ordinary hub interdependence cannot account for. The parties best able to leave are the ones already leaving. The exit is the report's one uncontested trajectory.

The subject here is the trajectory, not the safety design the committee chose. WG21 prioritized Stroustrup's Profiles over the Alliance-funded [Safe C++](https://safecpp.org/P3390R0.html) on the technical merits, an outcome the losing side's own funder has since affirmed. Nothing in this dossier reads that choice as an error. Regulation, rival languages, and falling switching costs bear on C++ regardless of which safety model won. The external pressure is indifferent to which design prevailed.

The mechanism runs as a chain, each link lowering the cost of the next. Government guidance erodes the mandated-safe domain where C++ once sat unchallenged, which raises the value of memory-safe rivals. As interop tooling matures the switching cost that kept dependents captive falls, and routing around the standard becomes feasible for more of them. Capable participants who judge internal voice ineffective leave for other venues, the person-level rehearsal of what institutions do when they build alternatives. A commercial safety-gap economy sits over the chain, earning from C++ staying unsafe and reinforcing both the regulatory and the rival pressure. The chain is the response side of the tension opened in the unrepresented majority, where a seatless user base holds voice but no vote. (medium-high)

WG21 is the sole authority over C++ and, like any standards hub, sits inside a division of labor it does not command. Occupying an interdependent position is the normal role of a standards body, and the dependencies are the ordinary split between specification and implementation. What the baseline does not explain is the deliberate construction of routes around the position. Google's [Carbon](https://github.com/carbon-language/carbon-lang/blob/trunk/README.md) exists as a C++ successor, the [Rust Foundation](https://rustfoundation.org/) hosts a Google-funded C++ interop initiative, and both name unresponsive C++ evolution as the reason. Mindshare follows the same slope, with C++ falling from second to fourth on the TIOBE index across 2025 as C and C# passed it. Its dependents are building the exits.

C++'s standing in regulated and government software rests on government tolerance, and that tolerance is narrowing (Vernon 1971). The [ONCD report](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Final-ONCD-Technical-Report.pdf) names C and C++ as memory-unsafe and steers new code toward safe languages. The CISA, NSA, and FBI roadmap guidance asks manufacturers to publish transition plans, and the EU [Cyber Resilience Act](https://www.craact.eu/article/13) phases binding obligations through 2027. None of this is a ban, guidance still permits safe-subset C++ through MISRA and Profiles, and the vast non-government installed base is untouched. It is demand erosion, not a kill switch. (medium-high)

Switching costs remain enormous and are falling at the same time (Klemperer 1987). Existing codebases, the stable ABI, certified toolchains, and a trained workforce are genuine accumulated investment, which is why migration off C++ runs slow rather than fast. The countervailing fact is tooling built to erase those very costs, including Rust-C++ interop, Carbon's bidirectional interop, and DARPA's [TRACTOR](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/translating-all-c-to-rust) program to automate C-to-Rust translation. Lock-in bought time. Others now subsidize the price of leaving. (medium-high)

When voice proves ineffective, the capable use exit (Hirschman 1970). Niall Douglas left WG21 for the C committee, writing that the standards process "chews you up if you try to change anything which matters." Sean Baxter withdrew after Safe C++ lost the priority argument and declined an explicit invitation to continue. Formal voice is real, since anyone may bring a paper and cast a vote, and each departure is one person's choice. The ones leaving tried hardest to change it from inside. (medium-high)

Bargaining power inside the arrangement tracks who has somewhere else to go (Fisher and Ury 1981). Implementers and corporate sponsors hold strong alternatives, from declining to ship a feature to funding a rival language. Locked-in users hold weak ones and can be taken for granted. Varied alternatives are ordinary, and strong ones can discipline a committee toward responsiveness rather than away from it. The advantage here is asymmetric, and it favors the exit-capable. (medium)

The arrangement rests on a minimal winning coalition with identifiable break points (Riker 1962). A one-quarter national-body bloc, or the withdrawal of a major implementer or the Standard C++ Foundation, would each be enough to disturb it. Broad supermajority rules exist precisely to make such defections costly, which keeps the break points latent rather than active. The fault lines are drawn, and so far unpulled. (medium)

A commercial ecosystem now earns revenue from C++ staying memory-unsafe, which gives it an adverse interest in the committee's safety work succeeding. Two clusters compose it. C-to-Rust migration vendors, including Immunant's [c2rust](https://github.com/immunant/c2rust), Tweede golf, Ferrous Systems, and ISRG's [Prossimo](https://www.memorysafety.org/initiative/sudo-su/), sell the transition away from the language. Hardware memory-safety commercializers, including Arm's Morello and MTE, Codasip, SCI Semiconductor, lowRISC, and the [CHERI Alliance](https://cheri-alliance.org/discover-cheri/), sell a way around it. The benign reading is strong. These actors address a real security problem, they overlap the rival and government pressure already counted, and their success does not require WG21 to fail. Its identity is positional rather than named. The incentive runs one way. (low-medium)

The beneficiaries are legible. Rivals capture systems workloads on safety grounds, the government coalition gets the safer ecosystem it asked for, and the safety-gap economy books the transition as revenue. The payers are the dependents with the fewest alternatives, the seatless end-user base and the regulated sectors that must clear compliance gates while their toolchain's safety story stays unproven. Benefit flows to the mobile. Cost settles on the captive. (medium-high)

**The government memory-safety coalition** ranks as a dangerous actor. Its power is coercive and legitimate, spanning ONCD, CISA, NSA, FBI, NIST, DARPA, and Congress in the United States and the EU behind the Cyber Resilience Act. It wants memory-safety risk out of critical software and needs no committee seat to pursue it, because it works on demand rather than supply. Its guidance does not bind the installed base, which caps its near-term force. Its direction has not reversed once in the record.

**Rust and Carbon** are the dangerous alternative to staying on C++. Their power is expert and referent. The Rust Foundation, backed by AWS, Google, Microsoft, Huawei, and Mozilla, stewards the language that government guidance names most often. Carbon remains Google's more experimental successor bet. Both want the systems workloads C++ holds, and both lower the cost of taking them. The benign counterweight is scale, since the C++ base is still roughly three times Rust's, so substitution is a slope and not a cliff. Rivals need not win to make leaving cheaper. (medium-high)

**The safety-critical compliance regime** is a dangerous actor. Its power is coercive and expert, running through MISRA, AUTOSAR, and ISO 26262, which static-analysis vendors and automotive OEMs enforce. It gates which C++ features are usable in regulated domains, which makes it the body that decides whether Profiles actually clears the bar. It answers to no committee vote. The gate is where the safety design meets the market. (medium)

**The C++ Alliance** is a dangerous actor whose salience receded after it wound down the Safe C++ effort. Its power is reward and expert, exercised through funding of full-time participants and tooling, and it resourced Safe C++ to a complete and evaluable proposal. Its role in this dynamic reads as clarifying rather than adversarial, because a finished alternative is what let the committee choose on the merits at all. Its own principal has since stated that Profiles won the argument, aligning the funder with an outcome that ran against its own investment. The funder underwrote the choice it went on to lose. (medium-high)

**The safety-gap economy and the named departers** were profiled with their findings above and need no restatement here. The safety-gap economy is the only exit force that profits whether users migrate or merely stay exposed. The departers are evidence rather than agents, marking where the most determined contributors judged the return on voice had gone negative. (low-medium)

Every finding in this dossier points the same way, which is why the trajectory is the report's clearest. The exit is already underway among the exit-capable and the regulated, and it accelerates as switching costs fall. If enforceable, implementer-backed Profiles safety does not ship on a regulator-aligned timeline, before the EU obligations bite through 2027, then migration to Rust, Carbon, and hardware memory-safety accelerates among exactly the sectors C++ can least afford to lose. If Profiles ship and satisfy regulators, the erosion slows. Horizon medium, confidence medium-high, because the directional evidence is uniformly degrading.

The existing mechanism is real, comprising SG23, Profiles, and a hardened standard library shipping in C++26. Its adequacy is contested, because Profiles are unproven at scale and enforcement is deferred to C++29, past the regulatory horizon that matters. The specific absent mechanism is a credible, implementer-backed safety deliverable on a regulator-aligned timeline. WG21 cannot build that alone, because shipping it depends on the same implementers and ISO processes the committee does not control. The lever sits largely outside its own hands. (medium)

The unrepresented majority named a representation void the committee has not closed. The cost of leaving it open is what this dossier measures, and that cost is climbing. The void stays open. The exit gets cheaper.

## 10. Stakeholder Register

The register scores each actor's salience and names the power bases behind it. Salience classes follow Mitchell, Agle and Wood, and power bases follow French and Raven. Both are structural positions, not verdicts on conduct. Dark stakeholders hold power by position rather than by name and are marked as such. In this register, salience and formal standing part ways more often than they align.

Two actors carry no home dossier and are profiled here.

**Boost and the Boost Foundation.** Boost is a peer-reviewed open-source library collection begun in 1998 by committee participants and grown past 160 libraries. A Delaware nonprofit, the Boost Foundation, was formed in 2020 to hold its assets. Its stated aim is to establish existing practice and supply reference implementations suitable for standardization, and the record backs the claim. Ten libraries entered TR1, and shared_ptr, weak_ptr, regular expressions, and type traits passed into the standard. Its power is expert and referent, exercised not through a bloc vote but through the committee members who also author its libraries. In 2024 the community moved Boost's fiscal sponsorship to the C++ Alliance. The move followed a governance dispute with the Boost Foundation over control of the assets, including the boost.org domain. The Alliance casts the transfer as a resourcing upgrade carrying explicit community exit rights. The Boost Foundation casts it as the loss of community-controlled stewardship. Both accounts are partisan, and neither is settled here. The contest decided who administers the incubator, not whether it still feeds the standard. Boost now sits inside a rival funder's orbit, its pipeline intact. Ownership moved, not function. (medium)

**Academic and research contributors.** They span universities and the US Department of Energy national laboratories. Bjarne Stroustrup publishes from Columbia, and Carnegie Mellon's Software Engineering Institute maintains the CERT C++ secure-coding guidance. But the concentrated weight sits in the DOE labs, among them Sandia, Argonne, and Oak Ridge. Their base is expert and referent, built on deep domain expertise and reference implementations such as Kokkos. Long-horizon federal funding, from the DOE Office of Science to the NNSA and the Exascale Computing Project, pays for sustained standardization work that volunteer participants cannot match. Delivery shows it. The lab group carried std::linalg, a BLAS-based linear-algebra interface, from a 2019 proposal into C++26. Argonne's hold on the US technical advisory group chair adds procedural access to that technical weight. The register rates the tier dependent, yet the lab sub-bloc behaves closer to dominant, and the distance between the two is the tell. What the group wants is a standard that serves high-performance and safety-critical computing, plus the trained researchers who are themselves the domain's scarce resource. The lab bloc alone both votes and staffs the future. (medium-high)
