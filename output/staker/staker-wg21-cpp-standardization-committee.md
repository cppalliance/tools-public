# Staker: WG21, the ISO C++ Standardization Committee

**A member-run C++ standards body where corporate funders and a founding cohort hold structural power the ecosystem's millions of users cannot reach.**

WG21 is the ISO/IEC working group that authors and maintains the C++ standard (ISO/IEC 14882), run as a mutual-benefit association funded by corporate sponsors and served by an overlapping support foundation.

July 2026, by Vinnie Falco

---

## 1. Executive Summary

**Key Judgments**

1. WG21's governing rulebook was authored by the office it empowers and was never ratified by the membership, an arrangement that lets a stable inner circle set the agenda, appoint every subordinate leader, and choose its own successors. (high)
2. The committee's money, its rule-writing chairs, and a self-electing board are fused in a single circle of dual-role officers, converting the resources of formally non-voting corporate funders into effective control that the voting membership never granted (medium-high).
3. WG21 seals the reasoning behind its technical decisions, and that seal - paired with the total absence of any outcome audit - both manufactures the appearance of consensus and lets conflicted experts act, unobserved, where the developers they serve cannot see (medium).
4. The firms that fund the committee's operation and hold its scarce chair positions capture disproportionate feature value and set the working agenda, converting subsidy and gatekeeping into shipped standard features that serve their own products (medium).
5. The millions who bear the standard's costs hold no seat in the body that writes it, and the proxies who speak in their name can be neither instructed nor recalled (medium-high).
6. The C++ standards committee runs on borrowed infrastructure it neither owns nor can quickly replace: an ISO platform that sets its rules and sells its output, a public corpus and the reference renderings practitioners actually read that each rest on a single volunteer, and undocumented operational knowledge lodged in a few individuals, with no successor or failover surfaced at any of these points (medium-high).
7. WG21 has entered a hardening safety-regulation window it cannot control without a shipped memory-safety story, and that gap is eroding the reputation and performance-based authority its standing rests on faster than either can be rebuilt (medium).
8. Because WG21 sets no floor on implementation and cannot compel adoption, a significant part of the real, portable definition of C++ is fixed outside the normative text - by converging compiler implementers, by the single build tool that determines whether a new feature is usable at all, and by the external vendors that supply the safety discipline the committee declines to specify (high).
9. A vast, silent installed base freezes the C++ standard's evolution through ABI lock-in, and the stakeholders who hold real alternatives take the exit while the captive majority, denied both a vote and an affordable migration, cannot (medium-high).
10. WG21's institutional knowledge is concentrated in an aging founding cohort at the same moment its C++29 safety coalition sits one defection from collapse, leaving both the committee's expertise and its safety agenda exposed to the departure or refusal of a handful of irreplaceable members. (low-medium)

WG21 is the ISO/IEC JTC1/SC22 working group that authors the C++ standard, the single international standard for a language with an installed base on the order of sixteen million developers, together with the industries and end users whose systems run their code. Participation reaches the committee only through national bodies, and the committee's money and public infrastructure are held by a dedicated 501(c)(6), the Standard C++ Foundation. It is at once the monopoly author of a widely relied-upon standard and a small, sponsor-dependent body.

The dominant dynamic is the Capture Spine. A self-electing Foundation board that also constitutes WG21's leadership converts the money and chair-holdings of formally non-voting corporate funders into working control over the standard's direction, so that a single overlapping circle supplies the funding, holds and staffs the rule-writing chairs, and fixes the technical agenda. The conversion is over-determined: it holds whether or not any participant intends it, so the absence of provable intent describes the structure rather than exonerating it. Most of the other dynamics either feed this spine or are held in place by the same instruments that shield it from correction. (medium-high)

The stated beneficiary and the actual prime beneficiary diverge. The standard is presented as a vendor-neutral commonweal good serving the broad developer base and the public exposed to C++ software. In practice steering, staffing, and money accrue to the chair-holding and funding firms and the incumbent leadership, while the diffuse base that is the nominal beneficiary bears the diffuse cost and must pay ISO to read the text that binds it. On the prime-beneficiary test (Blau and Scott 1962), WG21 is a mutual-benefit association for its funders and incumbents behind a commonweal front. The structural reason for the gap is that those who decide are seated through funding and appointment, while those the standard is written for enter only through proxies they did not choose and cannot recall. (medium-high)

The trajectory is degrading at the edges while the center holds. The funder layer is widening as new sponsors underwrite meeting attendance, high-value contributors are exiting, and the memory-safety story remains unshipped as the regulatory clock runs down, with CISA roadmap pressure and EU Cyber Resilience Act obligations beginning in September 2026. What keeps the center stable is the same apparatus that makes the capture hard to see and hard to correct: a self-authored, never-ratified rulebook that concentrates appointment and consensus authority in a stable inner circle, and a seal on subgroup reasoning that renders the money-to-control conversion unobservable. The degradation therefore lands on funding breadth, contributor depth, and safety delivery rather than on the incumbency itself. (medium)

## 2. The Organization

WG21 is a member-run ISO working group with no premises, staff, or budget of its own. It authors the C++ standard through volunteer experts and leans on corporate sponsors and an overlapping foundation for money and infrastructure. Formally ISO/IEC JTC1/SC22/WG21, it was formed in 1990-91 from accredited experts of the member nations of ISO/IEC JTC1/SC22 with an interest in C++ ([The Committee - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/std/the-committee)). The project record shows the United States was asked to submit a C++ New Work Item in 1989, the project was approved in April 1991, and the group held its first meeting on 18-19 June 1991 in joint session with the US committee J16, with early participation from sixteen countries ([C++ Projects - open-std.org](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/projects)). ISO published the first C++ International Standard on 1 September 1998 as ISO/IEC 14882:1998 (same source). The group sits under SC22, created in 1985 for programming-language standardization, whose international secretariat ANSI holds in the United States ([ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 22 - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO/IEC_JTC_1/SC_22)).

**Its stated scope** is "Development and maintenance of ISO/IEC Standards, Technical Specifications, and Technical Reports related to the programming language C++" ([Business Plan and Convener's Report N5019 - open-std.org](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/n5019.pdf)). The parent subcommittee's stated aim is to improve portability of applications, programmer productivity and mobility, and application compatibility over time (Wikipedia SC22 source above).

WG21 runs as a three-stage pipeline of subgroups, each chaired under the convener's authority (isocpp The Committee, above). Stage 1 Study Groups incubate proposals until they mature ([SD-3 Study Group Organizational Information - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-3-study-group-organizational-information)). Stage 2 design subgroups Evolution (EWG) and Library Evolution (LEWG) own feature design and ship-vehicle selection. Stage 3 specification subgroups Core (CWG) and Library (LWG) own the normative wording ([SD-4 WG21 Practices and Procedures - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-4-wg21-practices-and-procedures)). A closing plenary decides, by consensus as determined by the convener, whether to adopt each recommended change (same SD-4). The 2025 business plan reports three hybrid meetings a year, experts from around 25 national bodies, nearly 30 active domain-specific subgroups, and a three-year revision cadence for ISO/IEC 14882 (N5019 above). Membership routes through national bodies, or through the US body INCITS, each "sovereign" over its own representation. Members are individual experts while national bodies cast the votes ([Meetings and Participation - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/std/meetings-and-participation/), [FGA: The gen on the BSI C++ Committee - jdebp.uk](https://jdebp.uk/FGA/cxxpanel.html)). A volunteer public mirror reports 20-plus nations and hundreds of papers, spanning C++98 through the in-progress C++26 working draft ([wg21.org](https://wg21.org/)).

**Governance concentrates in the convener,** who determines consensus, chairs the group, sets the schedule, appoints Study Groups, and answers upward to SC22, JTC1, and ITTF (isocpp The Committee, above). The current officers are convener Guy Davidson, vice-conveners Nina Ranns and Jeff Garland, and secretary Braden Ganetsky (same source). This follows a documented transition: Herb Sutter chaired WG21 across 2002-2008 and 2009-2025 and served as secretary 1998-2002, presiding over every ISO C++ standard except C++98 ([Standard C++ FAQ WG21 - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/wiki/faq/wg21)). On his term's expiry he became convener emeritus while remaining chair and CEO of the Standard C++ Foundation, and ISO installed Davidson with a three-person convenership team of Davidson, Garland, and Ranns ([A new convenor team and secretary - herbsutter.com](https://herbsutter.com/2025/11/)), confirmed in committee minutes ([WG21 2025-10 Kona Admin telecon minutes N5029 - open-std.org](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/n5029.pdf)). The selection followed SC22 Resolution 14-04, which assigns a vacant convenership to the candidate whose national body holds the fewest SC22 convenorships - the UK held none, the US several ([The Shape of Code, convenor - shape-of-code.com](https://shape-of-code.com/tag/convenor/)). The Direction Group, a small by-invitation body of experienced participants - Howard Hinnant, Roger Orr, Bjarne Stroustrup, Daveed Vandevoorde, and Michael Wong per SD-4 (above) - shapes priorities, and the group has adopted ISO guidelines barring AI-generated papers while allowing AI as a copy-editing aid (N5029 above).

**Money reaches the committee indirectly.** WG21 levies no fees on the international group. Meetings should have national-body sponsors such as ANSI, BSI, or DIN, but that can be pro forma, and "the financial burden is almost always assumed by corporate sponsors". Hosting a meeting costs $25,000 to $40,000, attendees pay their own travel, and ISO rules bar a mandatory facilities charge ([SD-5 Meeting Information - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-5-meeting-information)). Costs instead flow through national bodies and a support foundation. INCITS, the US body, funds itself through annual participation fees ([Membership Info - incits.org](https://www.incits.org/participation/membership-info)) - $2,703 per technical committee or study group in 2026, less for small and academic members ([Fees for Participation on INCITS Technical Committees - incits.org](https://www.incits.org/dotAsset/2f813690-f5b9-455d-8a94-5afb6435609f.pdf)), and $11,544 to $47,840 for Executive Board membership ([Fees for Participation on the INCITS Executive Board 2026 - incits.org](https://www.incits.org/dotAsset/c9027f72-5ff0-4686-a8f3-24c85417e630.pdf)). The UK body's C++ committee, by contrast, is "voluntary and gratis" (jdebp.uk above). The Standard C++ Foundation supplies meeting-hosting funds, travel assistance, and proposal grants approved by subgroup chairs ([About - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/about)).

**Two nonprofits sit beside the committee.** The Standard C++ Foundation, incorporated in Washington on 9 October 2012 and announced that November, is a 501(c)(6) trade association "organized exclusively for the improvement of business conditions for C++ software developers" ([November CTP - devblogs.microsoft.com](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/cppblog/november-ctp-isocpp-org-standard-c-foundations-announced-today/); [Standard Cpp Foundation - Nonprofit Explorer, ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/461356355)). Its fifteen founding members included Microsoft, Google, Intel, HP, IBM, Bloomberg, and DRW Trading Group, and its founding directors came from Google, Intel, Boost, Texas A&M, Microsoft, and IBM (devblogs source). It operates isocpp.org, the isocpp and cplusplus repositories, and CppCon, funded by tiered non-voting sponsorships - Gold $10,000, Silver $5,000, and Bronze $1,000 a year ([Membership - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/about/membership)). Its leadership overlaps heavily with WG21: Herb Sutter is president and chairman and was convener through 2025; Bjarne Stroustrup is treasurer and a Direction Group member; Nina Ranns is vice-president and a WG21 vice-convener; Michael Wong is a director and Direction Group member (isocpp About, above). The second entity is The C++ Alliance, a California 501(c)(3) public charity formed in 2017 with IRS recognition in 2019, founded and led by Vinnie Falco as president and executive director ([Bylaws - cppalliance.org](https://cppalliance.org/pdf/Bylaws.pdf); [Fiscal Sponsorship Proposal - cppalliance.org](https://cppalliance.org/pdf/Fiscal-Sponsorship-Proposal.pdf)). Its charter expressly includes "supporting the work of the ISO Standard Committee by improving the core language and standard library," it employs staff engineers who publish open-source software, and it funds the tooling behind the public WG21 mailing mirror (wg21.org above). The Alliance frames itself as overlapping the Foundation in mission but distinct in tax status - a 501(c)(3) taking public donations against the Foundation's 501(c)(6) member-dues model ([The C++ Alliance - cppalliance.org](https://cppalliance.org/)).

**By financial scale** the Foundation is small but growing: Form 990 filings show revenue rising from $59,100 in FY2013 to $2,102,990 in FY2025, against $1,570,703 in expenses and $936,950 in net assets that year, with total liabilities of zero throughout and all revenue classified as contributions (ProPublica above). Across every filed year, president Herb Sutter is the only officer recorded with any compensation - $40,650 in FY2025, a high of $132,375 in FY2017. The output itself is priced per unit: ISO/IEC 14882:2024, the 2,104-page seventh edition, sells for CHF 227 as a single-user electronic copy ([ISO/IEC 14882:2024 - webstore.iec.ch](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/102625)), with equivalent prices at national resellers.

**On the Blau-Scott prime-beneficiary test,** WG21 classifies best as a mutual-benefit association: its members - national bodies and their accredited corporate and individual expert delegates - both shape and consume the standard, and membership routes through bodies each sovereign over its own representation. The published standard carries commonweal characteristics, since ISO sells it to the public, but the governing apparatus is member-driven, and the legal form of its principal support entity corroborates the reading: the Standard C++ Foundation is a 501(c)(6) business league, the classic mutual-benefit tax category (ProPublica above).

### Scope

- **The decision this assessment serves.** It maps WG21's stakeholders - who holds structural power, who bears the cost, and where dependency and incentive concentrate - to inform how participants, national bodies, funders, implementers, and outside observers engage with or seek to reform the committee's governance.
- **Key assumptions.** The diagnosis rests on the sourced organizational and domain facts reproduced here: the three-stage subgroup structure and officer roster as recorded through the 2025-2026 convenership transition, the funding and leadership overlap between WG21 and the Standard C++ Foundation, and the continuing government-and-industry push toward memory-safe languages.
- **Out of scope.** This run does not assess the technical merit of C++ or of any specific proposal, and it renders no normative judgment on whether the committee or its governance ought to exist. It characterizes the stakeholder structure as documented.

## 3. The Landscape

WG21 occupies a dominant but pressured position: it is by far the largest and most active language group in its ISO subcommittee, yet it depends on outside implementers it does not employ and faces a competitive and regulatory shift toward memory safety it does not control.

### Market position

WG21 authors and maintains ISO/IEC 14882, the C++ standard, inside JTC1 under SC22 - the "portability subcommittee" for programming languages, their environments, and system-software interfaces ([ISO/IEC JTC1/SC22 - open-std.org](https://www.open-std.org/JTC1/sc22/)). It is one of only eight active SC22 working groups. Its structural peers are WG14 (C), WG5 (Fortran), WG9 (Ada), WG4 (COBOL), WG17 (Prolog), WG23 (Programming Language Vulnerabilities), and WG24 (Linux), while sixteen former groups (Pascal, ALGOL, BASIC, POSIX, and others) have been disbanded (open-std SC22 record above; Wikipedia SC22 above). Among these peers WG21 is by far the largest and most active by attendance and output. Measured against language-stewardship bodies outside ISO, C++ governance is distinctively heavyweight: Python runs a five-person elected Steering Council and the PEP process ([PEP 13 Python governance - GitHub](https://github.com/python/peps/blob/main/peps/pep-0013.rst)), and Rust a Leadership Council with topic subteams and the RFC process ([RFC 1068 Rust governance - GitHub](https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs/blob/HEAD/text/1068-rust-governance.md)). C++ alone among major languages is governed inside a formal international standards body with national-body voting and ISO balloting.

**By developer population C++ is large and growing.** In its core niche of systems programming its principal competitor is Rust, the one production-ready memory-safe language operating without a garbage collector at C and C++'s level, now adopted for performance-critical components by Google, Microsoft, Amazon, and the Linux and Windows kernels ([Memory-Unsafe Code Is a Liability - corrode.dev](https://corrode.dev/blog/memory-safety/)). C++ counts about 16.3 million developers in Q1 2025, up from roughly 9.5 million in Q1 2022, a 72 percent gain, ranking fourth behind JavaScript, Java, and Python and ahead of C (about 9.1 million) and Rust (about 5.1 million) ([Sutter's BeCPP keynote: C++ added more developers - wro.cpp](https://wrocpp.github.io/posts/cpp-fastest-growing-2025/); [Bjarne Stroustrup on C++ developer numbers - LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bjarnestroustrup_there-are-472-million-developers-in-the-activity-7400914240313917440-TH7T)). C++ and Rust are the two fastest-growing languages by percentage over 2022-2025 ([December 2025 - Sutter's Mill](https://herbsutter.com/2025/12/)), set against a broader developer population of roughly 47 million in 2025, forecast to reach about 58.7 million by 2029 ([Worldwide Developer Population Growth Forecast 2025-2029 - IDC](https://my.idc.com/getdoc.jsp?containerId=US53789825)). Survey penetration is consistent: 23.5 percent of Stack Overflow's 2025 respondents use C++ ([Technology 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey - survey.stackoverflow.co](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology/)); TIOBE ranked C++ third at 9.12 percent in July 2026, behind Python and C ([TIOBE Index - TIOBE](https://www.tiobe.com/tiobe-index/)); and JetBrains' 2024 survey found 27 percent of developers had used C++ in the prior year ([State of Developer Ecosystem 2024 - JetBrains](https://www.jetbrains.com/lp/devecosystem-2024/)).

**Output cadence and workload are both heavy and rising.** WG21 ships a new International Standard on a three-year cadence - C++98, 03, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, with C++26 in working draft and C++29 planned - and parallelizes experimental work as Technical Specifications (wg21.org above; N5019 above). Average meeting attendance rose from about 67 experts across 2004-2016 to 252 in February 2020, and the March 2026 Croydon hybrid meeting drew 204 attendees from 24 national bodies ([Survival rate of WG21 meeting attendance - The Shape of Code](https://shape-of-code.com/2020/12/13/survival-rate-of-wg21-meeting-attendance/); [Business Plan and Convener's Report N4960 - open-std.org](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2023/n4960.pdf); [WG21 2026-03 Croydon Hybrid Meeting Minutes - open-std.org](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/n5040.pdf)). Subgroups grew from 16 in 2012 to nearly 30 in 2025, with roughly 30 telecons a month between plenaries, and in the single Croydon week EWG processed 70 agenda items while LWG ran 38 polls (N5040 above). Retention is uneven: of 374 attendees recorded across 2004-2016, 47 percent appeared only once, and the estimated half-life of attendance was 9-10 years (Shape of Code above).

### Ecosystem dependencies

WG21's authority and output rest on layers it does not control. Procedural authority runs upward through SC22, JTC1, and ISO/IEC, and the group revises IS 14882 on ISO's ballot cadence (N5019 above). Below it sit more than 20 participating national bodies that send accredited experts and cast ballots - 24 were represented at Croydon in March 2026 - each sovereign in appointing its representatives, with the US body INCITS open to any individual (SD-4 above; Meetings and Participation above; N5040 above). The standard's practical value depends on three implementations shipping it, each pairing a compiler with its own standard library: GCC with libstdc++, Clang/LLVM with libc++, and Microsoft's MSVC with the Microsoft STL, and conformance to C++20/23/26 remains partial across all three ([cpp compiler support vendors - cppreference.com](https://cppreference.com/cpp/compiler_support/vendors); [Clang C++ Programming Language Status - clang.llvm.org](https://clang.llvm.org/cxx_status); [microsoft/STL - GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/STL)). Financially the committee leans on the Standard C++ Foundation, which funds meeting hosting, travel assistance, and proposal grants and operates isocpp.org and CppCon, legally separate from WG21 but overlapping in leadership ([Financial assistance policy for ISO C++ meetings - isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/blog/2015/05/financial-assistance-policy)).

### Domain-specific vulnerabilities

The dominant pressure is the government-and-industry push toward memory-safe languages. NSA (2022, 2023) and CISA (2023, "The Case for Memory Safe Roadmaps") classify C and C++ as memory-unsafe and urge migration. The 2024 White House ONCD report "Back to the Building Blocks" reinforced this, and CISA's "Product Security Bad Practices" lists new memory-unsafe code for critical infrastructure as a bad practice, expecting software manufacturers to publish memory-safety roadmaps by the end of 2025 ([CSI Memory Safe Languages Reducing Vulnerabilities - media.defense.gov](https://media.defense.gov/2025/Jun/23/2003742198/-1/-1/0/CSI_MEMORY_SAFE_LANGUAGES_REDUCING_VULNERABILITIES_IN_MODERN_SOFTWARE_DEVELOPMENT.PDF); [CSI Software Memory Safety v1.1 - media.defense.gov](https://media.defense.gov/2023/Apr/27/2003210083/-1/-1/0/CSI_SOFTWARE_MEMORY_SAFETY_V1.1.PDF); [The Case for Memory Safe Roadmaps - ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov/CSA/2023/231206.pdf)). This pressure directly elevates Rust as a competitor and drives C++'s own safety agenda, reshaping the conditions in which WG21 sets priorities against a running regulatory clock (corrode.dev above).

## 4. The Self-Ratifying Core: a self-authored, unratified rulebook lets a stable inner circle appoint itself and its successors

WG21's governing rulebook was authored by the office it empowers and was never ratified by the membership, an arrangement that lets a stable inner circle set the agenda, appoint every subordinate leader, and choose its own successors. (high) The finding is not one defect but a closed loop: unpoliced entry at the base coexists with a monopoly on appointments at the top, that monopoly seeds a by-invitation body which pre-settles priorities before any formal vote, and multi-decade tenures plus founder centrality hold the structure stable even through a change of personnel. The loop holds under challenge because its anchor - the concentration of consensus-calling and appointment power in a single office defined by a self-written, unratified instrument - survives the most adversarial reading. The benign account explains generic concentration but has no answer for a governance document that confers the office's own powers without a plenary mandate.

**The mechanism.** Authority runs top-down through one office and is insulated from the bottom. A single convener determines when consensus exists, appoints every subgroup chair and study group, and sets the schedule, all under a standing document that office wrote itself. Because the same office controls both the calling of consensus and the appointment of everyone who shapes proposals, the membership's formal openness never reaches the decision layer: entry is easy and powerless, appointments are closed and decisive. The by-invitation advisory group that sits above the subgroups converts this appointment monopoly into agenda control, pre-settling the priority list that chairs then schedule to, so that plenary polls tend to ratify choices already made in an informal channel.

The structural anchor is documented and uncontested in its particulars. The convener is the single decision center (Dahl 1957) - determining consensus, appointing all chairs and study groups, and setting the schedule - and the chairs [serve at the convener's pleasure](https://lists.isocpp.org/all/2026/03/0477.php). The powers themselves are defined in SD-4, the practices-and-procedures document, which per the record was [authored by the convener's own office](https://lists.isocpp.org/admin/2017/12/0430.php) and is [unilaterally maintained](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2026/06/27122.php) rather than ratified by plenary vote. The office's appointment and consensus powers are set out on the committee's own pages. Concentrated single-caller decision-making is the ISO peer-class baseline, which holds this to a finding rather than an anomaly - the specific, unmatched deviation is the self-authored, never-ratified instrument that grants the office its authority. The prior convener's role as that rulebook's author is profiled in the Capture Spine.

Openness at the base is real and, by the committee's own record, unpoliceable (Ostrom 1990). Participation runs through national bodies. The US route is pay-to-join for any organization on a published fee schedule, while some other national bodies charge nothing at all. National bodies are sovereign over whom they send, and the committee was found to have [essentially zero power to limit participation](https://patricia.no/2023/01/09/open_letter_guest_post.html) even where it wished to exclude a specific individual, with access widening further as the Alliance [funded guest attendance](https://cppalliance.org/company/2026/03/27/WG21-Meeting-Support-Statement.html) grew. The result is a boundary a motivated party can pack from outside while the appointment core stays closed within - openness where it is powerless. Broad participation is a genuine stated value of the peer class, so the finding is the porousness itself rather than the low cost of entry. The edge the benign reading covers least well is the documented inability to exclude, which is a direct product of the same national-body sovereignty the benign reading praises. (medium)

The channel that turns appointment control into agenda control is the by-invitation advisory group [announced in 2017](https://lists.isocpp.org/admin/2017/02/0192.php) as explicitly "not a decision group" (Helmke and Levitsky 2004). Its formal structure and papers are a matter of record - it holds a standing plenary slot and issues the priority direction ([P2000](https://open-std.org/JTC1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2022/p2000r4.pdf), and [P5000](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p5000r1.pdf), which designated the safety Profiles work its top near-term tier) - and the record characterizes the operating pattern as pre-settlement through informal channels followed by ratification by formal poll. That characterization is analytical rather than documentary, and the group formally only recommends and speaks solely when unanimous, so the adverse reading of a closed body pre-deciding priorities is carried at reduced confidence against a benign reading of a senior advisory body of the kind normal for the peer class. The geometry - a closed core reached through appointments - holds. The pre-settlement valence is the softenable part. (low-medium)

Stability comes from tenure and founder centrality. A small inner group - the standing advisory body plus the leadership that overlaps the Standard C++ Foundation board - controls information, agenda, and succession (Michels 1911), with tenures measured in decades: the convener's office was held for about 23 years and the evolution subgroup chaired for 24. The language's creator remains central, authoring active safety profiles and co-signing the group's safety agenda, and the long-tenured principal showed the identity fusion typical of founder-run bodies (Block and Rosenberg 2002). Both legs are contested and read down: participation inequality, in which a small active minority accumulates proportional influence, is the expected baseline for volunteer and member organizations, and a real, planned handover of the convenership did occur. The weight of the evidence is that the concentration is structurally real but its adverse framing is not decisive - the succession transferred the powers intact rather than dissolving them, a bifurcation of the office developed in the Hollowing Bench. (low-medium)

The core is hard to move from inside or out. Altering the status quo requires assent from the convener, from each relevant subgroup chair in sequence, and from the national-body ballot bloc under double-consensus thresholds, with the implementers' conformance lag operating as a further, de facto veto (Tsebelis 2002). Multiple veto players are the deliberate stability design of ISO standardization, so the baseline is expected and the finding stands at the structural level: the same design that guarantees stability also guarantees that a self-perpetuating core cannot be dislodged by ordinary majorities. Beneath this sits a dependence the committee cannot escape (Emerson 1962) - the standard is worthless without a handful of uncompellable compiler front-ends it cannot direct - and that leg is degrading, as the announced wind-down of one key front-end supplier thins an already small implementer base. The coupling from that dependence into rule-writing influence is real but reads down from capture to unavoidable expertise concentration under the benign account. The rule-writing leg of that coupling is developed in the Capture Spine. (medium-high)

**Who benefits.** The arrangement's beneficiaries are the people inside the loop. Agenda and succession control accrue to the inner leadership and the advisory group, and derivatively to the employer firms whose staff hold those seats, since a nominally advisory group and an operational design gate can share a single corporate interest. Those who pay are the parties with formal standing but no reach into the core: the general membership, whose openness stops at the decision layer, and the broad developer public spoken for by proxies it cannot recall, whose exclusion is treated in Governance Without the Governed. The stated beneficiary of a standards body is the user community at large. The structural reason for the gap is that the instrument defining who decides was never submitted to those it governs.

**The internal power map.** The internal relations are dense and mutually reinforcing. The convener sits at the center of a patronage network, appointing all chairs and study groups, and cooperates closely with the vice-convener and with the advisory group whose priority papers set the office's standing agenda. The chairs in turn are under standing pressure to schedule to that priority list. The participation gate is held one layer out, at the national body, and is entangled with leadership: the same figure who is the national committee's secretary is also a Foundation officer, so the lever that admits or removes participants sits close to the core it protects. The principal fault line is not internal rivalry but external erosion in the implementer dependence, with a latent one in the safety coalition's fragility, which the core's present stability masks.

The **convener (Guy Davidson)** is the single most decisive internal office and classifies as a definitive stakeholder (Mitchell, Agle and Wood 1997): his power base is the legitimate authority of a formal ISO office joined to the reward power of appointment (French and Raven 1959), and he determines consensus, names every chair and study group, and sets the schedule. Stated as a neutral administrator investing in profiles and seeking reproducible floating point, he in practice backs the Profiles line and carries a numerics agenda, placing him with the incumbent safety line rather than as a neutral check. His autonomous power is nonetheless below the office's face value: his standing agenda is set by the advisory group's priority papers, the rulebook that defines his powers is maintained by his predecessor, and he was seated as the compromise nominee holding the fewest convenorships under an SC22 resolution. He wields a maximal office along rails laid by others, and his part in the C++29 safety settlement makes that office time-critical now.

The **Direction Group** (Vandevoorde, Stroustrup, Orr, Wong, Garland, McKenney) is a definitive stakeholder whose legitimacy is expressly contested. Its power base is the expert and referent authority of founder-level figures plus a standing plenary slot and a priority list chairs treat as authoritative. It pre-settled the safety Profiles work as the top near-term tier. On paper it is advisory, "similar to a Board of Directors," recommending priorities only, but it is the de facto priority-setter chairs schedule to, and it is now seeking a plenary endorsement vote for its direction paper, which would begin to close the gap between its stated and actual roles by formalizing the influence. It is charter-less, with no published selection criteria or operating procedures, and its influence is amplified by employer conduits, since one firm employs both a Direction Group principal and a wording-group chair. Its trajectory is stable and presently activist, defending the C++29 safety timeline through a formal call to action.

**INCITS**, the ANSI-accredited US national body, is a definitive stakeholder whose formal role of casting one national vote understates its real lever: control of who may participate, exercised through member sponsorship. Its power base is legitimate authority joined to a coercive gate, since withdrawal of a sponsoring member's backing is de facto expulsion, as when a sponsoring delegation [withdrew sponsorship and removed a participant's access](https://www.tomazos.com/ub_question_incident.pdf). The open, one-vote-per-organization framing coexists with that coercive gate in practice, and the gate is entangled with committee leadership - the expulsion in the record was executed from inside the body by a Foundation-linked delegation, and the national committee's own secretary is a Foundation officer. Its trajectory is stable, with the sponsorship lever episodic and engaged mainly at ballots.

**Trajectory.** Across its parts the core is stable and, on one leg, eroding. The appointment monopoly, the closed advisory channel, the oligarchic concentration, founder centrality, the porous base, and the veto-player lock all read as stable through 2025-2026, even as top offices turned over: the convenership recently changed hands, a handover recorded in the Kona administrative minutes. The single degrading element is the external implementer dependence, thinned by a supplier wind-down. Contested items held on the merits during the period: work shipped over sustained objection while the safety Profiles work was blocked from the current standard and an ABI break was again declined, so the multi-veto status quo held where it was tested.

Through the C++29 cycle the self-ratifying core is very likely to persist in substantially its present form. The recent handover diffused the central office across two officeholders but transferred its powers intact, and founder-figure centrality remains undiminished in the active safety agenda. The only leg under real strain is the external implementer dependence. (medium)

**Remediation.** The existing external check - oversight by the parent ISO bodies and the double-consensus balloting of national bodies - reaches the committee's technical output but not the internal instrument that defines who decides, which has no ratification gate at all. The specific absent mechanism is a plenary ratification, and periodic re-ratification, of the practices-and-procedures document, coupled with a separation of the consensus-calling office from the appointment power it now also holds. Both are adoptable within the committee's current governance form and at no budgetary cost, since each is a standing-document change confirmable by the same plenary poll the body already uses, so the gap is one of will rather than capacity. No such ratification mechanism presently exists.

## 5. The Capture Spine: corporate funders convert money, chairs, and a self-electing board into control the members never voted.

The committee's money, its rule-writing chairs, and a self-electing board are fused in a single circle of dual-role officers, converting the resources of formally non-voting corporate funders into effective control that the voting membership never granted (medium-high). The couplings are structural and individually documented: a permanent Foundation holds the money and infrastructure, a self-perpetuating board drawn from WG21's own leadership governs that Foundation, and the funders who supply the money also staff and chair the groups that write the rules. A benign reading is available at each link - aligned stewardship by the few people competent to do the work - but that reading reinterprets motive without severing any coupling, and the concentration of money and direction in one small group obtains whether or not anyone designed it.

The loop runs in one direction and closes on itself. A permanent Foundation holds the committee's money and public infrastructure. A self-perpetuating board drawn from WG21 leadership governs that Foundation. That board is the vehicle by which the non-voting funders' money becomes effective direction. The funders who gain that direction are in large part the compiler-vendor implementers who staff and chair the rule-writing groups. Those same dual-role officers then set the technical agenda they fund. Money and direction therefore terminate in one small, overlapping group, which is what makes the structure a spine rather than a set of separate arrangements.

A permanent administrative and funding layer sits distinct from the volunteer technical mission (Pournelle 1979). The Standard C++ Foundation reported $2.1 million in FY2025 revenue and a single compensated officer, hosts isocpp.org, cppreference, and the committee wiki, and runs its own annual conference, as its [own annual reporting](https://isocpp.org/blog/2024/10/standard-cpp-foundation-annual-report-for-fiscal-year-2024) documents. Some self-maintaining administrative layer is expected of any body at this scale and frees experts for technical work, so the Foundation's bare existence is weak evidence of capture. The diagnostic weight comes from who governs it (low-medium).

That governance is where the structure sharpens. The Foundation was chartered as a 501(c)(6) with non-voting sponsors, and control rests solely with a self-electing board whose composition, [recorded in the board's own minutes](https://isocpp.org/files/Meeting-Minutes/Standard-C++-Foundation-24-11-20-Board-Meeting-Minutes.pdf), mirrors WG21 leadership, so the overseer and the overseen coincide (Tillotson and Tropman 2025). The sponsors who fund the body are, by its membership structure, non-voting. Self-perpetuating selection is the statutory baseline for the 501(c)(6) form, and overlap with the technical body it serves is natural given the shared mission. The finding is the totality of the overlap; the corporate form itself is unremarkable (medium-high).

Formally non-voting funders nonetheless supply essentially all the money and most of the labor and hold the chairs - NVIDIA's stack of subgroup chairs, the finance-sector bloc, and the Foundation's founding sponsors - which converts funding and relationships into influence beyond any formal role (Glaeser 2002). The chair-holding and funding dominance are documented. The inference that they amount to effective control is grounded in that chair-holding rather than assumed. The benign reading - that influence tracking contributed money and labor is expected in any sponsor-dependent volunteer body, and that non-voting status was deliberately designed to keep money out of formal governance - is genuine, and it is why this link sits at medium rather than higher (medium).

The funders who gain that influence are, in large part, the compiler-vendor implementers who staff and chair the wording groups that specify what their own employers must build - the regulated parties writing the rules (Stigler 1971). The committee's own roster shows implementer-employed chairs across the wording groups, and the process rules themselves, SD-4, were authored by the convener personally and remain amendable at will - the self-authored, unratified rulebook developed in the Self-Ratifying Core is the instrument this capture runs through. Practitioner authorship of technical rules is the ordinary mechanism for competent oversight in a specialized field, so the reduced finding here is the fusion of that authorship with the funding apparatus rather than the authorship itself (medium).

These roles are not held at arm's length. The same individuals who control the Foundation's money - among them Sutter, Ranns, Stroustrup, Levi, and Wong - simultaneously hold WG21 leadership positions that set technical direction, and the financial assistance the Foundation dispenses is gated by the very chairs who benefit from it (Davis 1982). This is the loop's closure: one circle controls the money and sets the direction. Below a certain size a specialized field will always see its few qualified people wear several hats, and coordinated collusion is unproven. The documented fact is the structural conflict, and the two readings - necessity versus capture - diverge only on interpretation, with no observation yet distinguishing them (medium-high).

The apparatus also forms an organized, pre-coordinated bloc that out-influences larger but fragmented national participation (Büthe and Mattli 2011). The US delegation, routed through INCITS and holding most convenerships, together with the Direction Group and Foundation speaking early with "one voice," shapes outcomes beyond formal voting weight. The bloc's structural weight is factual. The claim that it speaks with a single coordinated voice is partly an inference and is held at low confidence. Every mature standards body shows some coordination edge for its organized participants, and early coordinated input is as consistent with diligence as with capture (low-medium).

**The beneficiaries are** the funders and the officers who bridge them to the rules: corporate sponsors convert money into direction without casting a vote, and the officers who sit on both sides accrue influence no single title reflects. The firms that fund and staff the committee also shape which features advance, a dynamic detailed in the Chairholders' Dividend. The cost falls on the voting members and national bodies whose formal weight is diluted by the coordinated bloc, and on the broad developer community that holds no seat, the concern carried by Governance Without the Governed (medium).

**Power inside the spine** runs along a few clear lines. The committee depends on the Foundation for money and public infrastructure, a dependency with no ready substitute. The tightest coalition is the Direction Group and the Foundation, which share board members and present a single early voice. The funder-implementer chairs form a second, overlapping bloc. Nina Ranns is the broker, the connective tissue joining WG21 leadership, the US national body, and the Foundation, while the sharpest fault line separates the Foundation from the C++ Alliance, which funds an independent tooling and community base and presses a reform campaign against the fused money-and-governance model (low-medium).

Five actors carry the dynamic, ordered by stakeholder salience.

**Herb Sutter** is the clearest case in the register of real power exceeding formal role. He authored SD-4 and maintains it, chairs the Foundation as its only compensated officer, and after 23 years as convener ceded the chair to a successor and [left Microsoft in late 2024](https://www.devclass.com/development/2024/11/12/iso-c-chair-herb-sutter-leaves-microsoft-declares-forthcoming-c-26-most-impactful-release-since-c11/1625565). The move reads less as relinquishment than repositioning: he gave up the neutral chair that barred technical advocacy while keeping the money-and-infrastructure chair and an active proposal seat, so he can now argue technically - his [Profiles paper](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/p3081r2.pdf) is the live C++ safety settlement - while the Foundation he chairs funds the meetings. His [Citadel Securities technical-fellow appointment](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/herb-sutter-citadel-securities) aligns him with the rising finance bloc. He stands to lose little, because his power base does not depend on the office he vacated (medium-high).

**Nina Ranns** occupies a broker position her titles understate. Her power concentrates across three centers - the convenership team, the US national body where she has served as secretary since 2018, and the Foundation vice-presidency - giving her control of minutes, procedure, and institutional memory. Presented as a neutral process officer who explains voting procedure, she in fact holds process and informational control, and her paid standing depends on those roles, a positional interest beneath the neutral-procedure presentation. Control of meeting mechanics is quiet but decisive power that no single title captures (medium).

**The Standard C++ Foundation** is, on paper, a neutral support entity legally separate from WG21 with non-voting sponsors. In practice its reward power - discretionary control of hosting, travel, and grants, plus ownership of the isocpp.org and CppCon infrastructure - sits with a five-person board that doubles as WG21 leadership, and the funds it dispenses are gated by WG21 officers, hosting on the convener's request and travel and grants on the chairs'. This is the inverse of the deliberate separation of money from governance that Rust maintains. Coordination among the overlapping officers is not proven and should be read as structural conflict-of-interest and the capacity to coordinate, not demonstrated collusion. Even so, the actual power the Foundation holds over WG21 far exceeds its separate-support-entity framing (medium-high).

**The C++ Alliance**, led by Vinnie Falco, is the principal challenger to the spine and carries a contested rather than illegitimate standing: its register classification reflects disputed legitimacy inside WG21, not misalignment. It holds near-zero formal committee office but real influence through independent funding, the wg21.org tooling behind the public mailing mirror, the largest C++ Slack, and reform essays that set outside framing. Its account - that it funded [Safe C++](https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/09/16/safe-c-proposal-all-but-abandoned-in-favor-of-profiles/925237) as a genuine experiment, [accepted the negative result](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4065702/safe-c-proposal-for-memory-safety-flames-out.html), endorsed Profiles, and issued a March 2026 statement disclaiming any vote-direction - is consistent with the record, though self-reported and partisan. Its mission and tax status and bylaws distinguish it from the Foundation, but its funding scale and runway are an open gap, so there is no basis to assert it can outlast the Foundation (low-medium).

**Microsoft** is a Foundation founding sponsor and ships MSVC and the STL. It employs the LEWG chair, Levi, and the author of the Profiles framework paper, Dos Reis, and historically held the convenership through Sutter, a lever that lapsed when he left in 2024. Its influence now roughly tracks its formal presence. What remains partly hidden is direction-shaping through the Profiles framework authorship - agenda influence beyond any single chair seat - layered on its founding-sponsor standing (medium).

**The trajectory is** tightening rather than loosening. The funding layer widened through 2026 as the C++ Alliance began underwriting meeting attendance and hosting, layering fresh funder relationships onto the persistent NVIDIA and finance-bloc chairs, even as that newest funder explicitly disclaimed vote-direction. The core dual roles held steady, Sutter keeping the Foundation chair after ceding the convenership, and the self-perpetuating board did not change form. The funder layer is very likely to keep widening through the C++29 cycle, deepening rather than diffusing the concentration of money and direction in the dual-role officer class. Whether that widening converts into tighter effective control rather than broader but still-benign sponsorship is the contested step (medium).

**No tailored remediation** was scoped for this dynamic, because no specific concern was posed as an analytical trigger. The structurally absent mechanism the evidence points to is nonetheless plain: there is no firewall between the layer that holds the money and the layer that governs the standard, the two being held by one self-electing board. Instituting such a separation would require de-coupling the Foundation board from WG21 leadership, a change within the committee's power to adopt but one the current overlapping structure is not organized to produce (low-medium).

## 6. The Sealed Room: sealing the reasoning manufactures consensus and lets conflicted experts act where those they serve cannot see

WG21 seals the reasoning behind its technical decisions, and that seal - paired with the total absence of any outcome audit - both manufactures the appearance of consensus and lets conflicted experts act, unobserved, where the developers they serve cannot see (medium). The seal and the audit vacuum are documented facts. What the confidence tag qualifies is their reading - manufactured consensus and an unmonitored agency gap on one side, ordinary confidential deliberation by aligned experts on the other. The record favors the first on structure and leaves the second available on interpretation.

At the center sits SD-4, the committee's practices document, which bars public quoting of subgroup discussion, wikis, and reflector content, so that only papers and poll tallies reach the outside while the reasoning that produced a decision stays sealed (SD-4 practices and procedures). Information control thereby becomes decision control (Akerlof 1970): an observer sees what was decided and the tally, but not why, who dissented, or what was traded. Information asymmetry from deep specialization is expected, and confidentiality can encourage franker debate - but that benign reading cannot explain why WG21 alone seals reasoning while its SC22 sibling committees publish minutes, a deviation from directly comparable peers with no benign analog. That deviation anchors the finding.

The audit vacuum compounds the seal. The convener who runs consensus also determines it, and in more than thirty years the committee has published no retrospective or outcome audit of any standardized feature, and no ISO appeal has ever been filed (Ashby 1956). Because the reasoning is sealed, the already-absent self-audit has nothing to examine. The two findings reinforce each other. What auditing exists is entirely external - the community-run [Are We Modules Yet](https://arewemodulesyet.org/) tracker and academic feature-adoption studies - none of it inside the committee. That volunteer bodies below a resource threshold lack audit capacity does not fit a committee backed by a funded foundation, and a three-year ballot is not an outcome audit. The total thirty-year absence is what the benign reading cannot cover.

With reasoning invisible, recorded dissent that would puncture an illusion of agreement never enters the public record. The governing Direction Group speaks, by its own account, only when unanimous, and Safe C++ was set aside for Profiles with "no comparative evaluation on record" (Safe C++ all but abandoned in favor of Profiles) (Janis 1972). The benign reading is genuine - convergence from shared technical exposure, including members updating after more of it, is the expected baseline - and the non-unanimous Contracts ballot (114-12-3) shows dissent reaching the record when a formal vote forces it. Weighted against the sealed norm, the evidence supports a manufactured-unanimity reading for sealed subgroup work while conceding that formal ballots can still surface disagreement (low-medium).

Beneath the formal procedure runs an informal one. The double-consensus ballot is the public face of direction the Direction Group pre-settles privately. Implementation-experience operates as an unwritten gate rather than a rule, and SD-4's stated procedure coexists with its actual unilateral maintenance (Meyer and Rowan 1977). The same discretion sits at identifiable chokepoints (Burt 1992): subgroup chairs decide what is forwarded, INCITS sits between individuals and WG21, and the convener sits between subgroups and plenary. Some gap between policy and practice exists in every institution, and single points of contact and chair triage are ordinary responses to paper volume. The finding is not that the gap exists but that the seal makes it undetectable (medium).

Absent any outcome audit, the fixed three-year release cadence becomes the surviving success metric, a target increasingly decoupled from quality: P3443 documents schedule prioritized over engineering, with 56% of binding-poll papers published under a week before the vote ([P3443R0, a reflection on the 2024 process](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2024/p3443r0.pdf)) (Goodhart 1984). The cadence held - C++26 reached feature-complete and produced its DIS on schedule ([C++26 is done, March 2026 trip report](https://herbsutter.com/2026/03/29/c26-is-done-trip-report-march-2026-iso-c-standards-meeting-london-croydon-uk/)) and C++29 began at once - which is the concern: an intact schedule riding over thinning review. Degraded conditions are meanwhile normalized, each cohort inheriting thin review and strain (over 60% report they often feel exhausted, 46% call the pace unsustainable) as the natural baseline (Pauly 1995). A benign reading survives - cadence as coordinating discipline, workload as the cost of genuine growth - so the tilt toward decoupling is real but unsettled (low-medium).

The gate on all of this is taste. A bounded expert in-group's validity criteria - "good design," "taste" - decide which proposals are admissible, and that gate diverges from users' needs (Haas 1992). The Direction Group concedes it has "no shared taste" even as taste keeps gating proposals (P2000R4, Direction for ISO C++), and authors report work "shut down on stylistic grounds" ([To Bind and Loose a Reference](https://thephd.dev/to-bind-and-loose-a-reference-optional); [a 2018 ISO meeting report](https://vittorioromeo.com/index/blog/mar18_iso_meeting_report.html)) while safety goes underserved. Domain expertise legitimately governs a technical body and "taste" can mean hard-won judgment - but the benign reading needs that judgment to be coherent, and a self-admitted absence of shared taste, applied at the chair chokepoints as a forwarding criterion, is a self-refutation it cannot absorb (medium-high).

**The agency gap.** The committee's experts and leadership decide. The roughly sixteen million developers and the national-body constituencies bear the consequences (Eisenhardt 1989). The seal is the mechanism that lets an agent act "largely unobserved" - with reasoning invisible and no audit to reconstruct it, an expert can pursue an employer's interest without the principals detecting the divergence. The conflicted interest itself is anatomized in the Capture Spine, where the firms that fund the committee also staff the chairs that specify what they build. The seal is what hides that conflict from the people it affects. The observed result, attention migrating toward employer-strategic features while stated wants such as safety and reflection lag, is tracked in Safety on the Clock. This coupling closes the loop, because the seal is what makes the unobserved agency possible. The benign reading is not trivial - delegation to expert agents is the point of a standards body, and the national-body ballot is a monitoring backstop - so the agency-gap claim is held below the seal that enables it (medium).

The distribution of gain and loss follows the seal. Those who benefit act inside it - leadership whose consensus cannot be publicly contradicted, and the conflicted experts whose interest the seal keeps hidden - while those who pay are the principals, who inherit decisions they cannot see reasoned and whose only channel to the convener is weak and indirect: adoption signals and surveys, read at a remove, with no vote (medium).

A reconstruction economy grows in the space the seal leaves. Because only papers and tallies are public, an outside layer does the committee's remembering for it: independent trip reports and commentary channels, academic programming-language researchers who supply the only recurring outcome evaluation, and long-form technical critics who document the ABI and library-quality consequences the committee will not self-audit ([a representative 2026 modules retrospective](https://mropert.github.io/2026/04/13/modules_in_2026/)). These roles are positional, filled by whoever attends and writes, and exist only because the reasoning is sealed - a mutual dependence in which the committee leans on them for its public narrative while they depend on the seal persisting (medium).

**Meeting C++ / Jens Weller** is the most developed instance of that layer: an independent platform (the entity Meetingcpp GmbH) built and run since 2012 by one person - a weekly blog roll, daily social feeds, a video channel, a Patreon, and the annual [Meeting C++](https://meetingcpp.com/) conference, operated [as a standing platform](https://meetingcpp.com/meetingcpp/) that every C++ constituency already reads. Its power base is referent and expert, not formal authority. On salience it is dormant - soft narrative reach, no governance lever, no time-critical claim - and the fitting posture is to keep it informed (Mendelow 1991). It profits commercially and reputationally from the transparency the seal withholds, an adverse interest worth naming: the niche depends on the committee not closing the gap. The platform is aligned, with a long and consistent track record, and improving - a [2026 hybrid conference announced](https://isocpp.org/blog/2026/03/announcing-meeting-cpp-2026), six-figure social reach, a fourteenth-anniversary milestone - its one structural fragility being that a channel the whole ecosystem reads rests on a single operator (medium).

**Adobe STLab / Sean Parent** occupies a sharper version of the same outside position. Parent, a senior principal scientist in Adobe's Software Technology Lab and by his own statement not an active committee member, publishes external evaluations, including [Memory safety and C++ successors](https://developer.adobe.com/cpp/whitepapers/cpp-successor-update/), a feasibility audit of memory-safe alternatives to C++, the kind of outcome audit WG21 never produces internally. His power base is expert and referent, with explicitly no legitimate standing inside the committee. On thin, single-artifact evidence his salience reads as dangerous - a high-visibility credible outsider, backed by Adobe's weight, who can place successor and safety-feasibility questions on the practitioner agenda directly, bypassing the committee. The stake is at once professional and organizational: keeping Adobe's large C++ codebases viable while steering the language's safety trajectory. The read rests on a single artifact and is held accordingly (low).

Every anchor held through 2025-2026. SD-4 sealing persists and the reconstruction ecosystem remains the only public window. No internal retrospective emerged, leaving the audit function external. The cadence held through the C++26 DIS and rolled straight into C++29. Expert taste again gated the outcome, the implemented Safe C++ borrow-checker set aside for design-judgment Profiles because the Rust safety model is unpopular in the room. The audit vacuum is very likely to persist across the C++29 cycle: nothing has audited the committee in thirty years and nothing emerged now, and only external pressure, such as the regulatory clock tracked in Safety on the Clock, could plausibly force a change (medium-high).

No corrective mechanism is on record, and this run identified no user-specified concern that would trigger one. The finding is the absence itself. The missing instrument is twofold - an independent, periodic outcome audit of shipped features, and disclosure of subgroup reasoning to the level the SC22 sibling committees already meet by publishing minutes. Neither exceeds a body of WG21's form or means: the funding anatomized in the Capture Spine puts it well above the volunteer-scarcity threshold the benign readings invoke, and both measures are governance choices a membership committee can make without new money. That none has been adopted in thirty years, and no appeal ever filed, is itself the strongest evidence that the seal is load-bearing rather than incidental (medium).

## 7. The Chairholders' Dividend: the firms that fund and staff the committee capture the features and decide what gets worked on

The firms that fund the committee's operation and hold its scarce chair positions capture disproportionate feature value and set the working agenda, converting subsidy and gatekeeping into shipped standard features that serve their own products (medium). The same small set of employers pays for the delegations, seats the chairs that decide what advances, and emerges with its priorities standardized: NVIDIA with asynchronous execution, Bloomberg with Contracts, the finance firms with low-latency primitives. The pattern is documented. What is contested is its reading. On the adverse account the co-location of money, chairs, and captured features is extraction. On the benign account it is expertise-based leadership, in which the firms that invest the most expert labor naturally shape the areas they work, so contribution and capture are correlated rather than causal. The evidence supports the structural claim strongly and the causal-capture claim only partially. The same money-and-chairs conversion the Capture Spine traces at the board level operates here at the level of individual features.

**The capture mechanism runs** through three linked moves. First, acquiring and holding chairs is itself the contested activity: effort targets steering design toward employer products, gatekeeping through chair scheduling and forwarding, and the national-body fee-and-sponsorship gate, an allocation of effort to capturing share rather than creating value (Tullock 1967). Second, a held chair is a sequential chokepoint - a feature dies if a chair does not schedule or forward it, and the chair chooses the ship vehicle and owns the normative wording - so the chair converts into a captured feature for the chair-holder's employer (Coff 1999). Third, the firms paying for the apparatus are the same firms holding the chairs, so funding the machine and steering it are one act rather than two. The benign reading survives at each step, since some positioning effort is expected in any resource-limited body and the bulk of committee effort still produces the standard, so the mechanism is coherent but interpretively contested (low-medium).

**The subsidy is the strongest link** in the chain. The apparatus runs on cross-subsidy: employers pay delegate salaries and travel, corporate hosts absorb the meeting cost, the Foundation covers travel need and hosting, and national fees fund domestic processing (Faulhaber 1975). By the committee's own meeting information, a single host absorbs a $25,000-40,000 cost and corporate sponsors underwrite the remainder. Cross-subsidy is the expected, intentional funding model for voluntary standards work, so the fact alone is baseline. Its force here comes from the identity between the firms that fund and the firms that steer (medium-high).

**Benefit capture is the observable output.** Firms holding scarce chair positions capture value disproportionate to raw contribution. NVIDIA landed [the std::execution proposal](https://www.open-std.org/JTC1/SC22/WG21/docs/papers/2023/p2300r7.html) in C++26 with [its own bundled reference implementation](https://github.com/NVIDIA/stdexec), a sender model [promoted as portable asynchrony](https://www.hpcwire.com/2022/12/05/new-c-sender-library-enables-portable-asynchrony/). Bloomberg drove [Contracts](https://open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/p2900r14.pdf) through sustained multi-year authorship and [argued against a separate Contracts technical specification](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2024/p3276r0.pdf) in favor of direct inclusion. The finance firms standardize their low-latency primitives. Both marquee features shipped when C++26 was finalized in March 2026, [over open disagreement about their value](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/03/31/contracts-are-in-c26-despite-disagreement-over-their-value/5229065), realizing the captured value for the chair-holding firms (medium). The benign reading, that the firms investing the most labor shape the areas they work, is genuine and correlated with the facts, but the concentration of chairs in the firms capturing the features favors the capture account.

**The costs are diffuse.** Chair-holding firms and the Foundation board capture the concentrated benefit of steering the standard while roughly sixteen million unorganized developers bear the dispersed cost and pay to read the published ISO standard they helped shape (Wilson 1980). Concentrated benefit against diffuse cost describes most institutions and is baseline, and the broad base free-rides on unofficial free renderings of the paywalled text. The finding is the asymmetry itself, which implies no malfeasance, and it is where Governance Without the Governed begins, since the population bearing the cost holds no seat to vote on it (medium).

**Selective incentives skew the staffing.** The non-excludable good, free drafts, is sustained by volunteer labor motivated by private, excludable side-benefits, chiefly steering design toward an employer product and reputation signaling (Olson 1965). Work with no private payoff is chronically unstaffed: the library wording group is described as "saturated," review capacity is flat, and the 2024 process reflection records incubator groups skipped to staff the core C++26 pipeline. Selective incentives sustaining a volunteer body is the peer-class baseline, and here private motive and public good largely coincide. The visible cost is the crowding-out of unglamorous work, the same starvation of unfunded effort that the Safety on the Clock dynamic tracks, where safety deliverables wait while employer features ship (medium).

**Reinforcing the structure, prestige tracks position.** Deference and celebration flow to the convener, direction group, chairs, and marquee conference figures, while the small editorial team that produces the normative text is comparatively uncelebrated, and members report recognition tracking "style" over research contribution (Bourdieu 1984). Hierarchy allocating prestige to senior positions is the peer-class baseline, and senior figures are frequently major contributors, so this is the weakest strand. It matters as the reward structure that makes chair-seeking rational (low).

**The beneficiaries and payers** divide on clean lines. The beneficiaries are the chair-holding employers - NVIDIA, IBM through Red Hat, Bloomberg, and the rising finance bloc - together with the funders whose money funds the delegations. Those who pay are the unorganized developer base, which receives features it did not prioritize and cannot vote on, and the unglamorous editorial and review work that no employer sponsors. The revolving door reinforces the pattern: personnel move among the firms that fund, implement, and populate the committee. Herb Sutter moved from Microsoft to Citadel Securities while chairing the Foundation, in a role the firm reportedly created for him. Vandevoorde moved from EDG to NVIDIA. Wong moved from IBM onward. Future-employment prospects across the funding and implementing firms are themselves a selective incentive (Kalmenovitz et al. 2023). The mobility is factual and the influence channel unobserved, so the structural condition holds while the corrupting interpretation does not gain confidence (low-medium).

**Internal to the dynamic,** power runs through the chairs and the money that seats them. The evolution and wording subgroup chairs are the load-bearing brokers, the funding firms are their patrons, and the Foundation is the disbursing intermediary whose travel and hosting support is gated by chair request. The principal fault line is single-employer concentration in nominally independent seats, a risk the committee's own two-representatives-per-company rule for ABI review implicitly concedes. A live conflict between Microsoft and Bloomberg over the Contracts design shows the bloc is not monolithic. The power bases at work are predominantly reward and legitimate - the capacity to fund and the delegated authority of a chair - rather than coercive (medium).

The evolution and wording subgroup chairs sit at the center as a definitive stakeholder set: four sequential chokepoints across evolution, library evolution, core wording, and library wording, with authority formally delegated by the convener. Their stated posture is neutral process run by consensus. The actual pattern is heavy single-employer concentration, with evolution under an NVIDIA chair, library evolution under a Microsoft chair, and library wording under a Red Hat/IBM chair, compounded by role fusion where the library-evolution chair also holds a Foundation office. The editors also sit in the pen-holding loop that applies approved changes, influence over the final text beyond any room vote. What they hold is the binding throughput constraint through which all future safety work must pass (medium).

NVIDIA holds the heaviest single-employer chair concentration, spanning evolution, two incubator groups, the direction-group chair, ABI review, and a national-body seat, alongside the bundled reference implementation of the execution feature it standardized. Its stated aim is portable heterogeneous computing for the whole ecosystem. The actual effect is that intake, design, direction, ABI review, and US voting are steered at once in the areas serving its accelerator strategy. The coordinated-influence potential of that stack exceeds any single chair's formal remit, and the committee's own concentration cap for ABI review names the risk obliquely. With execution already landed, its urgency is moderate and its agenda ongoing (medium).

IBM, through Red Hat, employs the library-wording chair and libstdc++ maintainer plus a core-wording vice-chair and an ABI-review vice-chair, giving it control of one of the three implementations and a heavy hand in the standardese. Its interest is aligned and structural: an implementer's stake in ABI stability and implementable wording. The decisive lever is quiet, an implementer's effective veto over unimplementable wording applied at the last drafting stage rather than in a floor vote, and the roster's IBM label understates that the real influence runs through the GCC implementation (medium-high).

Bloomberg is dominant within its domain rather than committee-wide, through authorship saturation of the Contracts facility, the allocator work behind the C++17 polymorphic-memory model, and a library-wording vice-chair seat, backed by [platinum conference sponsorship](https://cppcon.org/sponsors/) and [its public account of investing in C++ at scale](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/stories/bloomberg-advancing-c-scale/). It calls Contracts "the single most powerful tool" for correctness and safety and reports investing in it "for many years." The alignment is consistent, though the win is contested after adoption. The hidden lever is feature-capture-by-authorship, in which sustained employer resourcing of one paper's author list converts into de facto ownership of a language feature (medium).

The finance-sector member firms - Citadel Securities, DRW, PDT Partners, Jump Trading, and Hudson River Trading - are a dominant and rising bloc. Their engineers hold the performance-adjacent chairs, a Foundation founder sits among them, and Herb Sutter is now a Citadel Technical Fellow whose firm publicizes both [a sea change in its C++ posture](https://www.citadelsecurities.com/careers/career-perspectives/sea-change-in-c-why-opportunities-abound/) and [its early adoption of C++26](https://builtin.com/articles/engineering-future-inside-citadel-securities-early-adoption-c26). Their stated aim is a better language for low-latency, real-time, and embedded use, and chair capture in the performance subgroups advances that aim. On the calibrating point, their role in core execution is adoption and requirements rather than authorship. The bloc is becoming the load-bearing constituency for high-performance C++ as big-tech participation retreats, and the Citadel-Sutter-Foundation nexus is the channel through which a member bloc reaches the top of governance (medium).

**Surfacing as a dark actor,** think-cell is the clearest hidden-register case in the dossier: a Berlin charting-software company of roughly 150 to 200 employees that has [funded the German national body's delegation since 2013](https://www.heise.de/news/think-cell-sichert-deutsche-Beteiligung-an-C-Standardisierung-1811068.html), after that body moved to dissolve its working group for lack of contributions, and [describes itself as the funder of the delegation](https://www.think-cell.com/en/career/events/2025-02-10). Its representative [chairs the ranges study group](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/n5016.pdf), the exact library facility its own product depends on. Its power base is reward power coupled with the legitimate and expert power of the chair seat, making it a definitive stakeholder to manage closely. The civic-support framing understates a directed standardization interest: by funding a national body a private firm effectively owns that body's committee participation and a subgroup chair, a lever its visible conference sponsorship does not capture (medium).

**The trajectory is degrading.** The captured features moved from proposal to shipped standard in C++26, the cross-subsidy is broadening as new attendance-funding programs come online, and finance firms keep absorbing committee talent. Conditional on the chair concentration that produced the C++26 captures remaining intact through the C++29 cycle, the next tranche of major library features is very likely to track the same employers' product agendas. That forecast rests on a well-documented co-location of funding and chairs but a contested causal mechanism (medium).

No remediation path is available. The evidence file identifies no specific concern and no chartered counter-mechanism for feature capture, and the committee operates no rotation limit, no employer-concentration cap outside ABI review, and no conflict-of-interest bar that would separate funding from chair-holding from feature authorship. The absence of any such instrument within the current budget, volunteer governance form, and membership scale is itself the finding (medium).

## 8. Governance Without the Governed: the millions who bear the cost hold no seat and are spoken for by proxies they cannot recall.

The millions who bear the standard's costs hold no seat in the body that writes it, and the proxies who speak in their name can be neither instructed nor recalled (medium-high). The affected population is not a marginal constituency but the whole reason the standard exists - working developers, the industries built on their code, and the end users whose devices run it - and none of them enters WG21 except through an intermediary it did not choose. The finding is not that the base is unrepresented in the abstract. It is that the representation on offer is structurally uninstructable. That precision matters because the benign reading is real and partly correct: appointed representation through national bodies is a legitimate, standard ISO design, so what the record supports is a vacuum of accountability rather than one of access.

The vacuum operates through three linked moves: the affected parties hold no seat, they are spoken for by proxies who can be neither instructed nor recalled, and responsibility for what those proxies decide dissolves into process. Each move is individually defensible as ordinary ISO procedure. Their compound is what removes the governed from their own governance. (medium-high)

**No seat for the affected.** The population the standard binds is absent from the table that writes it (Young 2000): the developer base cited defensively at roughly sixteen million, the latency-sensitive SG14 contingent that supplies input without a vote, downstream regulated industries, and the end users of C++ software. The benign counter is genuine and weighs against the strong reading: no standards body seats every affected party, and anyone may join a national body to gain a voice. What it does not cure is that a voice is available only by opting into the proxy structure, never by any direct standing of the affected as such. (medium-high)

**Proxies without a leash.** The intermediaries who do hold seats speak for groups that can neither instruct nor remove them (Pitkin 1967): national bodies cast votes for their countries' users, who have no mechanism to recall a delegate, and the Direction Group speaks "for the direction of C++" with no constituency that ever selected it. The appointed-expert model is a legitimate ISO design in which delegates serve their technical judgment, and national bodies do genuinely represent - which is why this finding survives only at reduced confidence. The edge the benign reading covers least well is the Direction Group, which asserts a mandate over the language's direction while answering to no selecting body. (medium-high)

**Nowhere for responsibility to land.** When a decision taken in the base's name goes wrong, tracing it to a responsible party yields only a tally (Davies 2024): consensus is a convener straw-poll, the reasoning behind it is sealed, and the Direction Group speaks only unanimously, so no member owns the position. The honest concession is that some diffusion of responsibility is inherent to committee governance above a threshold of complexity and need not be engineered blame-avoidance. The evidence supports the structural fact of the accountability sink more firmly than any claim of intent. (medium)

The distribution is asymmetric by construction: those with seats set direction, and those without absorb the results. The beneficiaries are the funders and chairholders who occupy the room - the same actors the Capture Spine shows converting money into control the members never voted, and the Chairholders' Dividend shows steering what gets worked on. The payers are the seatless base and, at the far end of the chain, the public exposed to memory-unsafe software, who bear the security consequences of decisions they cannot see, and the future cohorts who inherit the accumulated safety debt. The asymmetry has a small, telling marker: the base must pay ISO even to read the text that binds it. (medium-high)

The dynamic's most striking internal feature is an external check that exists on paper and never fires. National bodies can block a Draft International Standard, yet that lever has gone unexercised on governance for three decades - the one venue of authority outside WG21's internal consensus is the dog that has never barked, which is why internal governance meets no external discipline before the ballot stage. The space it leaves is now being filled from an unexpected direction, as safety regulators harden into an involuntary proxy for the base's security interest - the same regulatory pressure the Safety on the Clock dynamic is running against. (medium-high)

National Bodies as ballot-casters (BSI, DIN, AFNOR, and others) are the dominant stakeholder of this dynamic: their power rests on legitimate authority - formal delegated ISO voting - backed by a latent coercive lever, since a bloc of negatives can force revision or block a standard once a [ballot requires two-thirds in favour and no more than one-quarter against](https://www.iso.org/sites/ConsumersStandards/voting_iso.html). Their legitimacy is formal and high, but their urgency is low, the lever episodic and, on governance, historically dormant. The result is an inverse case - high formal power paired with near-zero exercised influence - aligned with the base's interest in principle yet behaving as a procedural ratifier rather than a governor. (medium-high)

The broad C++ developer community, on the order of sixteen million and including the latency-sensitive contingent, is a dependent stakeholder: a high normative claim - the standard exists for it - with no formal power and no seat. Its only lever is aggregate adoption, slow and uncoordinated and impossible to wield as a bloc. Staying on old standards or exiting to other languages is the only vote it casts. The committee reads it only indirectly, through instruments such as the [Stack Overflow developer survey](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology), and it is in turn spoken for by the Foundation, study-group chairs, and vendor delegates - proxies with their own agendas - so its influence is not merely diffuse but ventriloquized. (medium-high)

JetBrains is a dark stakeholder here: absent from every governance channel, yet materially shaping how the voiceless base is measured. The commercial developer-tools vendor runs an annual survey and a dedicated [State of Developer Ecosystem 2025](https://blog.jetbrains.com/research/2025/10/state-of-developer-ecosystem-2025/) report, alongside a [State of C 2025](https://lp.jetbrains.com/the-state-of-c-2025) instrument, aggregating base sentiment independent of the Foundation-run survey. Its power is expert - data and tooling credibility - with no legitimate standing in WG21, and its salience is dormant, a keep-informed actor whose well-resourced instrument can surface what the base wants faster and more broadly than committee channels. The benign reading, that this is IDE-marketing telemetry serving product strategy, co-survives. The influence is real but soft, an agenda input the committee neither controls nor can suppress. (medium)

learncpp.com occupies a parallel position at the education layer. Run since 2007 by a single author, Alex, it is a [free modern-C++ tutorial path](https://www.learncpp.com/) that by [its own account](https://www.learncpp.com/about/) carries no signups or upsells. Its power is referent and expert - a trusted single-author authority - with no standing in standardization, placing it as a discretionary, keep-informed stakeholder. It captures the base that will not pay ISO for the normative text, and in doing so quietly shapes the mental model of incoming developers - a marginal influence on governance but a long-run one on the base's baseline understanding, concentrated in one person. (medium-high)

Two stakeholders sit at the vanishing point of representation, defined by position rather than name. The end users and the public whose devices and critical infrastructure run C++ bear the security cost of memory-unsafety - the very harm driving regulator pressure - yet hold no voice in, and largely no awareness of, the governance that produces it. Behind them stand the future and unborn developer cohorts, who will inherit the frozen ABI layouts, the safety debt, and the complexity accretion while holding literally zero representation. Both are maximally affected and wholly unrepresented, and the survival of the end-user case anchors the finding - the ultimate cost-bearer sits entirely outside every channel - even as the proxy framing softens. (medium-high)

On direction the picture is static where it is legible: the representation gap is stable, the uninstructable-proxy structure is stable, and the accountability sink shows no discoverable movement either way. The one moving part is external - regulators hardening into an involuntary proxy the base never appointed. Over the next standards cycle the affected base very likely remains seatless (medium-high): no mechanism to seat it has been proposed, the national-body ballot that could force the issue stays dormant on governance, and every pressure toward representation originates outside the committee rather than within it.

This run engaged no analytical trigger of this class, so no remediation was scoped to it. Judged on its own terms, the existing mechanism - appointed national-body representation plus open study-group participation - is adequate as ISO procedure and is the accepted channel by which the affected may enter, but it is structurally incapable of granting the base an instructable, recallable voice, because entry is conditioned on joining the proxy rather than on any direct standing of the governed. Within WG21's current form, budget, and membership model no mechanism for direct, accountable representation of the affected base exists, and none is proposed. (medium-high)

## 9. Borrowed Ground: the committee runs on a platform, a corpus, and volunteers it neither owns nor can replace

The C++ standards committee runs on borrowed infrastructure it neither owns nor can quickly replace: an ISO platform that sets its rules and sells its output, a public corpus and the reference renderings practitioners actually read that each rest on a single volunteer, and undocumented operational knowledge lodged in a few individuals, with no successor or failover surfaced at any of these points (medium-high).

The dependency runs in three stacked layers. At the base sits the ISO/IEC JTC1 platform, which supplies the committee's legal existence, its balloting machinery, and its participation rules, and which owns the copyright to the product. Above it sits the document corpus and the read-path: the papers, the working draft, and the HTML renderings through which the world actually reads the standard, each hosted or generated by one volunteer or one academic server. At the top sits the tacit operational knowledge - toolchain, editorial, reflector, and logistics - that keeps the machine running and is held in a handful of heads. Each layer is a single point of dependency the committee does not control, and a lapse at any one removes a capability the body cannot quickly reconstitute (medium-high).

WG21 operates inside the ISO/IEC JTC1 platform (Rochet and Tirole 2003), which fixes the Directives, balloting, editing review, copyright, and participation gates set out in the [ISO/IEC JTC1 Procedures](https://isocpp.org/std/iso-iec-jtc1-procedures), can change process unilaterally ("ISO is encouraging us to use white paper process"), and profits from the output by selling it: [ISO/IEC 14882:2024](https://www.iso.org/standard/83626.html) is copyrighted and sold, while [The Standard](https://isocpp.org/std/the-standard) makes only the drafts free. The same machinery is a chokepoint the body does not own (Areeda 1990): ISO holds the copyright and bars a free authoritative text, and National Body accreditation gates who may even enter the room, since a guest can be barred on NB objection. These are deliberate choices that secure international legitimacy and vendor-neutrality, and the terms have been stable and predictable. The platform relationship shifted only at the edge in 2026, when the March meeting was "no longer a joint INCITS meeting." The dependency is nonetheless real: the rule-setter is external and the output is paywalled (medium).

Concrete single points of dependency exist below the platform (Chopra and Sodhi 2004): ISO is the sole publication path, a single DTU-hosted server holds the [document repository](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/standards), and the Standard C++ Foundation is both the standing financial backstop and now the single host of isocpp.org, cppreference, and the wiki. The C++ Alliance's wg21.org adds a browsing layer but explicitly does not rehost the papers, so no failover surfaced. Relying on one academic host or one foundation is efficient for a small body and reflects scarce willing providers, but efficiency and dependency are the same fact seen from two sides: the single points persist and no redundancy has been surfaced (medium).

Undocumented operational knowledge concentrates in very few hands (Polanyi 1966): the LaTeX toolchain was single-handedly maintained by Pete Becker through C++11, the project-editor team is tiny, the reflector officer role is one person (Vollmann), and meeting logistics are one person (Maurer), roles catalogued in SD-4. Single-maintainer infrastructure is typical of volunteer projects and reflects trust and continuity at smaller scale, but the concentration is what turns each point into a single point of failure, and this layer is degrading rather than stable: [Edison Design Group](https://www.edg.com/company/background)'s 2026 wind-down, with John Spicer [stepping back](https://herbsutter.com/2025/11/10/trip-report-november-2025-iso-c-standards-meeting-kona-usa/), is a concrete realization of single-holder risk, only partly mitigated by the planned open-sourcing of its front-end (medium-high).

ISO benefits directly from the arrangement, collecting the revenue from selling the standard and lending the legitimacy that is the point of operating within it, while the single volunteers gain little beyond reputation and provide their service for free. The wider ecosystem benefits too, for as long as everything keeps working. The cost is borne latently by the committee and by the millions of developers downstream of it: because no point has yet failed, the risk stays invisible, and the honest reading is "has not failed yet" rather than "will fail." The exposure is structural, not a forecast of imminent collapse (medium).

Power here sits in indispensability rather than in its exercise. The platform holds high formal power and low day-to-day influence over substance: the NB/DIS ballot, the only external recourse ISO hosts, has never been used against a WG21 action in more than thirty years, so its authority is genuine but dormant. Procedural authority runs upward, since SC22 confirms the convener and once enforced its selection rule (Resolution 14-04) against a US attempt to change it mid-selection. The corpus and read-path chokepoints hold power of a different kind: none of the volunteers exercises leverage and all are aligned, so the risk is continuity and availability rather than capture. The one fault line is a quiet consolidation, as the read-path migrates toward the same apex that runs governance while the Foundation absorbs cppreference's hosting and administration (medium-high).

**ISO/IEC JTC1/SC22** is the dominant stakeholder of this dynamic, becoming definitive at ballot boundaries. Its power base is legitimate and positional: it owns work item 14882, runs the ballots, and confirms the convener, and WG21 cannot publish without it. It presents as a neutral, rules-based steward and has behaved consistently as one, enforcing its rule even against a large national body, and it delegates heavily and rarely intervenes in substance. It is best read as a latent structural gate whose formal power is real but dormant, a posture stable through 2026 (medium-high).

**cppreference.com (Nate Kohl)** is a definitive read-path stakeholder in the process of being absorbed into the register. Its power is expert and referent: it is the default free reference practitioners consult instead of the paywalled text, run with volunteer editors, and Kohl is a Google engineer. The Mendelow reading is Keep Satisfied. The [2026 announcement](https://isocpp.org/blog/2026/04/announcement-cppreference.com-update) documents the Foundation taking over its hosting and administration, and that absorption is the live trajectory. The material point is not misalignment - the site is aligned and useful - but concentration: real influence over how C++ is understood is migrating from a solo operator to the same board that runs governance, a quiet consolidation of the read-path under the apex (medium).

**Tim Song (timsong-cpp / cppwp)** is a definitive in-domain stakeholder and the single actor who is at once an infrastructure gatekeeper and a governance insider. His power is expert plus referent and legitimate: he maintains the [widely cited HTML renderings](https://github.com/timsong-cpp/cppwp) of the working paper and historical standards that the ecosystem relies on, and he is an active LWG member authoring library wording (P2160, P2255, P2259, P2281). The Mendelow reading is Manage Closely. The point is concentration rather than misalignment: he holds unusual leverage over how the library text is both written and read, effectively a register-class actor surfaced through the read-path, and a near-solo dependency that held through 2026 (medium).

**The open-std.org host (DTU / DKUUG / Keld Simonsen)** is a dependent structural chokepoint. Each page of the [site carrying the public paper corpus](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/) states that it is sponsored by DTU with HTML design by Keld Simonsen, with [DKUUG historically providing the service](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/impit/open/j1n4536/j1n4536.htm). Its power is latent, combining legitimate power by SC22 delegation with de facto gatekeeper power as the single infrastructure maintainer, and it is aligned, offering free hosting with no agenda beyond dissemination. The Mendelow reading is Keep Satisfied. The stake is the dependency itself: no redundancy is surfaced through 2026, so an availability failure here would disrupt the whole ecosystem's access to the corpus (medium).

**eel.is/c++draft (Eelis van der Weegen)** is the sole maintainer of [cxxdraft-htmlgen](https://github.com/Eelis/cxxdraft-htmlgen) and host of the [HTML rendering of the working draft](https://eel.is/c++draft/) that most practitioners actually read, the de facto free front-end to the paywalled normative text. His power is expert and referent, with no formal authority, so the attribute-level classification is dangerous but the function is a single-maintainer dependency, not coercion. The concern is again indispensability: a critical read-path concentrated in one person's tool and personal domain, which the committee neither controls nor backstops (medium).

The platform and corpus dependencies are stable through 2026, since ISO's paywall, copyright, and single publication path all held and the single open-std host and the reference renderings persisted, while the tacit-knowledge layer is degrading as the founding single-holders begin to step back. Over the next two to three years, as that cohort ages out with EDG's 2026 wind-down as the first concrete instance, a disruptive loss of a single-point capability before a documented successor exists is likely (medium). This platform dependency is only one of two external levers the committee cannot manage. The second, a government safety-regulation lever it likewise does not control, stacks on top of it and is developed in Safety on the Clock (medium).

The record shows no remediation for this exposure. The committee has surfaced no documented succession plan for its single-point operational roles, no mirrored or redundant hosting for the corpus and the read-path, and no backup-maintainer arrangement, measures all adoptable within a volunteer body's budget and governance form, such as written runbooks, a mirrored corpus, and named deputies, yet none is in place (medium).

## 10. Safety on the Clock: attention flows to employer features while the safety story goes unshipped and the regulatory deadline runs

WG21 has entered a hardening safety-regulation window it cannot control without a shipped memory-safety story, and that gap is eroding the reputation and performance-based authority its standing rests on faster than either can be rebuilt (medium).

**Attention migrates** toward employer-strategic features - GPU asynchrony for NVIDIA, Contracts for Bloomberg, low-latency work for the finance-sector member firms - while the diffuse user base's two stated top wants, safety and reflection, wait their turn (Jensen and Meckling 1976). The reading that this migration deliberately starves safety is the softest link in the chain: employer features are genuinely useful, and a volunteer body works on what its participants are paid to care about, so the alignment gap is held low. One want, reflection, reached C++26 while the other, safety, slipped, leaving that gap partly closed and partly widened. (low)

The unshipped safety story is itself the capital being consumed (Mises 1949). Safe C++ was all but abandoned in favor of Profiles, and the core Profiles were themselves deferred to C++29 via a whitepaper. The March 2026 trip report records reflection shipping while safety did not, and a 2024 process reflection found 56 percent of polled papers had under a week of incubation. A cohort coasting on accumulated standing and deferring maintenance for present throughput is the finding. The benign reading, that steering a mature standard through disruption naturally strains reserves, survives alongside it. (low-medium)

**The external lever** is the hardest evidence here (Vernon 1971): the domain depends on government tolerance. The [CISA memory-safe roadmap campaign](https://www.cisa.gov/case-memory-safe-roadmaps), joined by [NSA and ONCD guidance](https://www.trust-in-soft.com/resources/blogs/memory-safety-is-key-the-shift-in-u.s.-cyber-standards) and [DARPA funding of C-to-Rust translation](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/translating-all-c-to-rust), frames C and C++ as a liability, and the [EU Cyber Resilience Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act) can render non-compliant products unsaleable in the EU while imposing manufacturer liability. The [CRA's phased deadlines](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/eu-cyber-resilience-act-requirements-products-software.html) - reporting from 11 September 2026, main obligations December 2027 - and [CISA guidance pushing roadmaps by January 2026](https://www.kdab.com/software-technologies/rust/memory-safety-roadmap-for-secure-programming/) set a clock the committee cannot reset. That adoption is voluntary and the pressure targets the memory-unsafe language class broadly rather than the committee's governance is the concession that holds this finding one tier down. (medium-high)

The consequence is legitimacy erosion (Suchman 1995). The committee's authority is pragmatic and cognitive, authoritative because widely adopted, and it is coasting on accumulated standing while slow safety delivery and the regulator challenge erode the current performance basis. ISO-process legitimacy is structural and holds. The pragmatic basis built on delivery is what is thinning. (low-medium)

**Beneficiaries and costs** divide cleanly: the features that advance serve the firms that staff and fund the work - NVIDIA, Bloomberg, and the finance-sector member firms - while the cost of the unshipped safety story falls on the broad C++ developer community, which did not set the priority order and is the most exposed to the regulatory lever. National-security and consumer-protection interests benefit from the external pressure, but whether Profiles satisfy them is unproven: no sourced signal shows regulators crediting Profiles over the memory-safe-by-default languages they name. The safety proposal set aside had been funded through The C++ Alliance, the funder dependency the Capture Spine develops, and the preference for Profiles over Safe C++ was settled at the closed review gate the Sealed Room traces. (medium)

Power relations turn on brokers, because the regulators hold power over the domain with no legitimacy inside it and their pressure can enter only through intermediaries. The Convener (Guy Davidson) and the Direction Group (Vandevoorde, Stroustrup, Orr, Wong, Garland, McKenney) translate the threat into the C++29 safety axis. Stroustrup's [note to the committee](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/p3651r0.pdf) names it a "credible threat" and the group's [safety strategy](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2025/p3874r0.pdf) sets the plan. The lever's force comes from effective alignment with the Rust Project / Rust Foundation, whose [displacement at the margin](https://blog.jetbrains.com/rust/2025/12/16/rust-vs-cpp-comparison-for-2026/) is what regulators can point to. The fault line runs between the exposed base and its brokers: the users most exposed to the lever are the least able to leave, the exitless base the Frozen ABI examines. (medium)

US and EU government safety regulators (NSA, CISA/FBI, White House ONCD, NIST/DARPA, EU CRA) are the dangerous stakeholders in this dynamic: high power, high urgency, no internal legitimacy. Their power is statutory and market-access based, backed by procurement levers and DARPA research funding. They want the memory-unsafe vulnerability class eliminated, and an escalating trail from NSA to CISA to ONCD to the CRA to DARPA aligns them in effect with memory-safe-by-default languages. Holding no formal position inside WG21, they are nonetheless the single largest force reshaping its agenda, which is why safety became the C++29 headline axis. What they stand to gain is a compliant ecosystem, and they lose little by continuing to press. (medium-high)

**The trajectory is one-directional** - the alignment gap is stable while capital consumption, the external lever, and legitimacy all erode, a strongly degrading dynamic. If the committee reaches the September 2026 CRA reporting deadline with safety shipping only as a C++29 whitepaper rather than a normative, implemented feature, then over that horizon regulators crediting C++ as memory-safe is unlikely and continued erosion of the pragmatic-authority basis is very likely. Confidence in this forecast is medium. That erosion then surfaces as the fragile voluntary compliance the Route-Around traces, where implementers and vendors drift toward a de-facto standard of their own.

No existing mechanism addresses this dynamic, and the analytical trigger for this run identified no specific concern in this class. The committee has no standing instrument that binds a normative safety deliverable to an external deadline. The absent mechanism is such a schedule commitment, which a volunteer, consensus body running on multi-year release trains is structurally ill-equipped to adopt within its current governance form. On present evidence none exists. (medium)

## 11. The Route-Around: where the committee under-delivers, implementers and vendors build the de-facto standard themselves

Because WG21 sets no floor on implementation and cannot compel adoption, a significant part of the real, portable definition of C++ is fixed outside the normative text - by converging compiler implementers, by the single build tool that determines whether a new feature is usable at all, and by the external vendors that supply the safety discipline the committee declines to specify (high). The committee authors the standard, but the standard is only one input to what actually ships. Where its output goes unimplemented, under-specified, or slow, other parties occupy the vacated function and set behavior the committee must then accommodate. The result is less the committee's displacement than the formation of a rival de facto authority beside it, strongest precisely where the committee's control is weakest - implementation and safety.

The dynamic runs through two coupled mechanisms. The first is an implementation vacuum: WG21 has no requirement of implementation experience before a feature is adopted ([P2274R0](https://wg21.link/p2274r0)), so features can enter the standard unshipped and their real behavior is then settled by implementer convergence and incompatible binary module formats rather than by the text. The second is route-around pressure: where the committee under-delivers - most visibly on safety, and on features that ship on paper but not in compilers - external vendors, consortia, rival languages, and build tooling supply what practitioners actually use, becoming the effective ship vehicle. The two reinforce one another, because the absent implementation floor is what leaves the function open for others to occupy.

The implementation vacuum is the best-supported element. Beyond the absent requirement already noted, Profiles advanced without an implementation, unified executors advanced without a prototype and were then retired (P2300R7), and C++20 modules remain only partially implemented years after standardization. The one public audit of the feature's real-world status, Are We Modules Yet ([modules-report](https://github.com/royjacobson/modules-report)), showed roughly 108 of 2,587 tracked projects modularized, a picture practitioner accounts confirm (Can we finally use C++ Modules in 2026?). A prototyping norm and Technical Specification vehicles exist but are optional. The finding is that this available screening check is not required, and that it was bypassed for features that then had to be withdrawn (Russell 2006).

The committee behaves as steward of "the" C++ even as adoption erodes beneath that posture (Brunsson and Jacobsson 2000). Implementers diverge, regulators press memory-safe languages, Rust displaces C++ at the margin, and Google has routed new systems work to Carbon ([carbon-lang](https://github.com/carbon-language/carbon-lang)) rather than to the committee. The contrary reading carries weight: every voluntary standard depends on buy-in, and WG21's ISO-backed adoption still holds the center of the installed base, so some erosion is ordinary external pressure rather than governance failure. On balance the evidence favors genuine erosion, since the movement is directional and sourced to named defections, but the benign reading keeps it short of decisive (medium-high).

Two weaker findings frame the same routability. WG21 is a net consumer of resources it does not own - ISO for publication, national bodies for votes, implementers for realization, the Standard C++ Foundation for money, volunteers for labor - so what it controls could in principle be routed around, by Carbon, by Rust, or by divergent compiler extensions (Pfeffer and Salancik 1978). Sponsors also route among venues to minimize concessions: the Technical-Specification-versus-International-Standard ship-vehicle fight, de facto compiler behavior versus the text, and rival forums, while ISO promotes a lighter white-paper process and the body imposes no implementation floor (Lerner and Tirole 2006). Both findings are genuinely contested - resource interdependence is the designed condition of a vendor-neutral standards body, and weighing ship vehicles is legitimate process design - so each is held low, and Carbon in particular remains experimental rather than a live replacement ([Carbon in 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/carbon-2026-latest-news-delays-real-reasons-behind-slow-alheraki-xwf8f)) (low-medium).

The beneficiaries are the parties that occupy the vacated function. Compiler implementers gain a de facto veto over what the standard can mean in practice. The dominant build-tool vendor gains gatekeeping power over feature usability. The safety-tooling market - static-analysis vendors, the MISRA coding standard, hardware-safety efforts - monetizes the gap the language leaves open. Rival languages capture the users and firms the committee cannot hold. Those who pay are the broad C++ developer community, who inherit fragmented portable behavior and must buy external discipline to write safe C++, and the committee itself, whose authority thins each time the real decision is made elsewhere. The distribution follows structure, not intent: no actor need defect for the de facto standard to form. It forms wherever the committee declines to require implementation or to ship safety (medium).

The controlling relationship is between the committee's leadership and its implementers. The binding real-world veto rests with the implementers - a feature is inert until they ship it, and their feedback gates ship-vehicle decisions in practice, beyond their formal seats. That leverage is concentrating rather than diffusing, with EDG winding down and Intel's compiler folded into Clang (Kona trip report, November 2025), so the veto consolidates into fewer hands even as the Direction Group and subgroup chairs depend on implementer feedback to steer. The sharpest fault line runs through the funders: the Platinum members of the Rust Foundation include the same firms that underwrite WG21's base - Microsoft, Google, AWS, and Meta - so the committee's own sponsors are hedging into the competitor that most sharply judges its governance. The exit those firms and users take is the one traced in the Frozen ABI, where the mobile few leave and the captive base cannot.

**Major compiler implementers** (GCC/libstdc++, Clang/LLVM/libc++, MSVC/STL, EDG) are the definitive stakeholders of this dynamic: high power, high legitimacy, moderate urgency. Their power base is coercive in the technical sense - sole authority over whether a voted feature actually ships, so chronic conformance lag operates as an effective veto - reinforced by position, since their leads hold the LWG chair, a Direction Group and Evolution seat, and vendor-representative seats. Their stated aim is high-conformance implementation. Their actual behavior is to pace or withhold it selectively (MSVC withheld the /std:c++23 flag until complete ([MSVC release cadence](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/cppblog/new-release-cadence-and-support-lifecycle-for-msvc-build-tools/)); modules lagged for years), which is structural leverage rather than misalignment. Consolidation is raising their salience, with a near-term spike from EDG's wind-down.

**CMake / Kitware** is the other definitive actor, powerful by tooling control rather than committee standing (power 3, legitimacy 2, urgency 2). It is the de facto build system the ecosystem relies on to consume standardized features - modules are usable in practice only through its P1689 scanning ([import CMake](https://www.kitware.com/import-cmake-c20-modules/)) - so a language feature's real-world usability is gated by one vendor's roadmap ([History of CMake](https://cmake.org/history/)). It participates in SG15 and brought P1689 into committee process, but its leverage is external: adoption of a shipped feature can stall on a single tool. Direction stable - it remains the dominant build system and modules adoption remains gated on it.

**Rust Project / Rust Foundation** is a dangerous, competitive stakeholder (power 2, legitimacy 1 inside WG21, urgency 2): it holds no committee authority but competes by substitution, drawing users and offering the governance and safety model against which C++ is measured (Rust vs C++ for 2026). Its stated position - memory safety by default with incremental C++ interop - is borne out by open governance ([RFC 3392](https://rust-lang.github.io/rfcs/3392-leadership-council.html)) that commentators contrast with WG21, framing the discontinuation of Safe C++ as a governance failure relative to Rust. Its influence is indirect but strong: it is both the benchmark that judges the committee's governance and the destination for defecting users, and, through the funder overlap above, a hedge held by WG21's own sponsors. Direction improving over 2025-2026, though roughly 5.1M Rust developers against C++'s 16.3M means substitution plays out over years.

The remaining route-around actors belong here as well:

- **Zig / Zig Software Foundation** - dangerous, competitive (2/1/2): a C-family systems language with a drop-in C/C++ compiler and runtime safety checks, positioned for teams the slow C++ safety story leaves exposed. The drop-in compiler is a quiet capture mechanism taking users at the margin. Improving - Zig reached the [TIOBE top tier](https://www.birjob.com/blog/zig-hit-tiobe-top-30) and targets a stability-locking [1.0 for mid-to-late 2026](https://ziggit.dev/t/zig-roadmap-2026/10750).
- **Commercial static-analysis / SAST vendors** (Coverity/Black Duck, Klocwork/Perforce, PVS-Studio, Parasoft, SonarSource) - dangerous, adverse-flagged (2/1/2): they monetize the memory-safety and undefined-behavior gap the committee leaves open, selling detection plus MISRA/AUTOSAR/CERT conformance, with the top five holding roughly 52% of a [growing market](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/static-application-security-testing-market). The adverse interest is direct - a language safe by construction shrinks the market - so they exert soft pressure to keep safety a tooling problem rather than a language one. Improving.
- **MISRA** - dangerous (2/1/2): owns the de facto safety-critical C++ coding standard (MISRA C++:2023, 179 rules), effectively mandatory in its domain and supplying the safe subset the language omits ([MISRA C++:2023 compliance](https://www.sonarsource.com/blog/misra-c-plus-plus-compliance-early-access/)). Its value depends on the language continuing to need an external safe subset. Improving - usage rose to roughly 61% of automotive teams.
- **CHERI hardware-safety consortium** (Arm Morello, Cambridge, SRI, lowRISC, Linaro) - dangerous (2/1/2): builds hardware-enforced memory safety for largely unmodified C/C++, an alternative safety path the committee does not control that can reframe the debate around hardware ([two paths to memory safety](https://ednutting.com/2025/10/05/cheri-vs-oma.html), [CHERI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capability_Hardware_Enhanced_RISC_Instructions)). Register members Microsoft and Google both participate, which is the hidden channel. Stable - Arm ended Morello with no product roadmap while CHERI-RISC-V advanced.
- **AUTOSAR** - dormant (2/1/1): authored the automotive C++14 baseline that fed MISRA C++:2023 ([MISRA, AUTOSAR, and CERT](https://gsasindia.com/blog/coding-standards-misra-autosar)). Its C++ role is now largely subsumed, persisting by legacy embedding inside the current MISRA baseline. Stable.
- **Stack Overflow** - dormant (2/1/1): the default first-stop C++ reference and a large annual developer survey, whose free corpus both measures the base and substitutes for the paywalled normative text - a substitute role that depends on ISO keeping that text paywalled. Stable - the 2025 survey again measured C++ usage independent of the committee.
- **arewemodulesyet.org / Roy Jacobson** - demanding (1/1/2): the independent audit of whether modules actually shipped, quietly setting the factual baseline against which committee optimism is judged (linked above). Stable.

Trajectory across the dynamic points toward a firmer rival authority: the implementation vacuum is structural and stable, while the erosion around it is intensifying, with Rust still displacing at the margin and Google still routing new work to Carbon, so the de facto standard consolidates faster than the committee closes the gap. That consolidation completes the pattern - the real, portable behavior of C++ is implementer-set, and the safety discipline the committee leaves unshipped (Safety on the Clock) is supplied by these vendors. Over a three-to-four-year horizon, absent an implementation floor or a shipped safety story, it is likely that the share of practical C++ behavior fixed outside WG21 grows - through implementer convergence, tooling gates, and external safe subsets - rather than shrinks (medium-high).

The remediation is a specific absent mechanism, not an inadequate existing one. No analytical trigger is engaged here and the committee runs no standing process for this class of concern. The gap it could close within its current governance, budget, and membership is an implementation floor - a requirement of demonstrated implementation experience, or a mandatory prototype or Technical Specification stage, before a feature enters the International Standard. This is a process rule rather than a resource commitment, so it is adoptable without new funding or structural change, and the features that advanced only to be withdrawn are the case for it. No existing mechanism performs this function today, because the prototyping norm and TS vehicles that could are optional and routinely bypassed.

## 12. The Frozen ABI: a silent installed base locks the language in place while the mobile few take the exit the captive cannot

A vast, silent installed base freezes the C++ standard's evolution through ABI lock-in, and the stakeholders who hold real alternatives take the exit while the captive majority, denied both a vote and an affordable migration, cannot (medium-high). This is where the lock-in-to-exit loop closes: switching costs make incompatible improvement declinable, the resulting freeze leaves dissatisfied users only a voice they structurally lack, and, denied that voice, the mobile few depart while the base stays and subsidizes the status quo. Two coupled mechanisms carry the dynamic - a coalition of sunk-cost holders that renders any ABI break unwinnable, and an exit channel that draws off the contributors the body can least afford to lose.

**The mechanism is coupling.** A mature corpus of deployed C++ generates switching costs that hold users in place (Klemperer 1987), and those same costs convert a technical decision into a political one: an incompatible layout change, however superior, asks today's users to pay so that future users capture the gain, and so it is declined (Farrell and Saloner 1986). The freeze then meets representation. The captive base has no vote, so its only channel is voice. Where voice is unavailable, the parties who can leave do, and those who cannot stay and subsidize the status quo (Hirschman 1970). Those who leave are not a random sample - they are the high-value, mobile contributors, and their departure lowers the quality of what remains for everyone who stays. The contested core of the loop is the claim that denied voice is what drives the exit. Read benignly, the departures are ordinary career mobility unrelated to voice, and that reading survives on the current record. The structural lock-in beneath the loop, by contrast, is not in dispute (medium).

**The freeze holds.** Its clearest instance is the committee's refusal of Google's [ABI - Now or Never](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2020/p1863r1.pdf), which would have broken binary compatibility to correct acknowledged-suboptimal standard-library layouts. The change was declined because early adopters would bear the transition cost while later users captured the gain, freezing the layouts in place. The refusal was reaffirmed across 2025-2026 even as a [CppCon 2025 talk](https://cppcon2025.sched.com/event/27bOC/could-we-handle-an-abi-break-today) reopened whether a break would still be as painful, and vendors enforce the same freeze in product - Microsoft's toolchain preserves binary compatibility back to Visual Studio 2015. Caution about breaking a massive installed base is rational and stability is a genuine user benefit, so the freeze is a defensible value choice rather than a mere coordination failure. It is nonetheless a lock, and it constrains the standard's own evolution (medium-high).

**The base is exitless. The mobile are not.** The roughly sixteen million C++ users have neither real voice, since they hold no vote, nor easy exit, since lock-in holds them in place, and routing their input through national bodies and experts is the deliberate ISO representation design rather than an oversight. The contributors who could not change the body through voice have instead left: Niall Douglas redirected effort toward WG14, the authors writing as ThePhD and Izzy Muerte disengaged publicly - documented respectively in [a committee trip-report account of the aftermath](https://thephd.dev/san-diego-2018-c++-committee-trip-report) and [On "Safe" C++](https://izzys.casa/2024/11/on-safe-cxx/) - Baxter stepped back once Profiles prevailed over his Safe C++ proposal, per reporting that the effort was all but abandoned, and Tomazos was removed. The exit concentrates among high-value contributors, degrading the pool for those who remain: Google ramped its participation down after the lost ABI vote, and EDG's confirmed wind-down - its principal, Spicer, having signaled the close, noted in the November 2025 Kona trip report - removes a long-tenured source of expertise. Some attrition is ordinary churn in a thirty-five-year volunteer body and headline participation has grown, but the losses are weighted toward those hardest to replace (medium-high).

Alternatives are unevenly held, and the unevenness decides who can credibly leave (Fisher and Ury 1981). Google holds a strong next-best option in Carbon and its endorsement of Rust, high-stake finance and GPU firms hold moderate ones, and the captive base holds weak options and is taken for granted. The asymmetry held through 2026: Carbon's 0.1 is not expected before late 2026, so Google's near-term alternative is Rust while the base's alternatives remain weak. Differing alternatives across an ecosystem is ordinary, and a strong-option firm exiting can be read as healthy market behavior rather than a committee pathology, which is why the pathological reading is held only tentatively (low-medium).

Whoever ships binaries gains from the freeze, and whoever cannot move bears its cost. Its beneficiaries are the parties whose economics depend on stable binary interfaces - the broad binary-distribution ecosystem, and named vendors such as Debian and Oracle - while the cost falls on the captive users, who subsidize a frozen suboptimal layout, and on future users, who inherit it. The structural reason for the gap is that the payers hold no vote while the beneficiaries form a silent aggregate the committee never counted yet treats as decisive: the collective sunk cost of the installed base is what made the ABI break unwinnable, and that aggregate is the sturdiest element of the whole dynamic, holding regardless of how any single member is read. The internal fault line runs between Google, which sought the break, and IBM, which defended stability. Google lost and disengaged. The Direction Group, for its part, feels Google's weight not through votes but through coercion-by-defection, as Carbon and the Rust endorsement raise the cost of ignoring it (medium-high).

Google is a definitive but disengaging stakeholder. Its power is structural and largely invisible: it owns Clang/LLVM, the front end the wider ecosystem - Carbon and IBM's Open XL included - builds on, it employed the standard's editors, Smith and Koppe, and it can fund an entire competing language. Its legitimacy is moderate and declining, having once held the editor's pen and subgroup gavels before its engineers ramped down, and its committee urgency is low even as Carbon and Rust apply standing pressure from outside. Its posture is consistent exit: it pushed faster evolution and ABI breaks, then, having concluded that indefinite ABI stability did not serve it, walked rather than kept fighting, and now builds Carbon and endorses Rust. Its formal footprint is shrinking while its real leverage - toolchain ownership plus coercion-by-defection - exceeds its remaining seats, and that exit increasingly takes the form of the Route-Around, building the alternative rather than repairing the committee's own (medium-high).

Two named dark actors anchor that sunk-cost aggregate, both dormant and both wielding a veto-by-consequence in place of a vote. Debian, a volunteer distribution and load-bearing consumer of the frozen ABI, treats an ABI break as a rare and expensive event: its [GCC-5 libstdc++ transition](https://wiki.debian.org/GCC5), among its largest ever, was absorbed by carrying a [dual C++11 ABI](https://developers.redhat.com/blog/2015/02/05/gcc5-and-the-c11-abi) rather than a clean break, and its documented transition pain is precisely the evidence invoked when a break is called unwinnable. Oracle, whose Developer Studio compiler economics depend on stable binary distribution, lends enterprise-vendor legitimacy to the same position, publicly framing an unstable ABI as "a nightmare for library and middleware vendors" in [its case for a stable C++ ABI](https://www.oracle.com/technical-resources/articles/it-infrastructure/stable-cplusplus-abi.html) while its [g++-compatible mode](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/E77782_01/html/E77792/gqfds.html) tracks the libstdc++ ABI. Neither holds a seat, and both stay quiescent until a break is threatened. The wider binary-distribution ecosystem behind them - major distributions and closed-source library vendors - is the decisive silent constituency, positional rather than named (medium).

The net trajectory declines: the freeze itself is stable, reaffirmed by the committee and enforced by vendors, but the exit channel is worsening as high-value contributors continue to leave, a drain that carries directly into the Hollowing Bench, where the thinning of the founding cohort is assessed in its own right. Through 2028, a further net loss of high-value, mobile expertise is likely, as EDG's wind-down and Google's sustained ramp-down outpace replacement and thin the long-tenured pool for those who remain. Confidence in that forecast is (medium-high).

This dynamic has no remediation on offer, and the assessment identifies none. The single lever that would unfreeze the standard, an ABI break, was weighed and declined, and its cost - forfeiting the stability that is itself a genuine user benefit - is real rather than notional. Lock-in is a structural property of a mature language's installed base, not a mechanism the committee can adopt or retire within its budget, governance form, or membership, so the honest finding is that no proportionate fix exists, only a standing trade-off the committee has chosen to hold (medium-high).

## 13. The Hollowing Bench: knowledge concentrates in an aging founding cohort while the safety coalition sits one defection from collapse

WG21's institutional knowledge is concentrated in an aging founding cohort at the same moment its C++29 safety coalition sits one defection from collapse, leaving both the committee's expertise and its safety agenda exposed to the departure or refusal of a handful of irreplaceable members. (low-medium) The two exposures are not independent: the same long-tenured principals who hold the irreplaceable knowledge are the pivotal actors whose exit or veto the safety plan cannot survive, so concentration and fragility reinforce each other rather than merely coinciding.

**The mechanism** runs through three coupled dynamics. Expertise sits with a founding and early cohort that admits successors slowly, so each high-value departure removes a holder the pipeline has not replaced. That thinning bench overlaps the C++29 safety coalition - Direction Group unanimity, the convener, the chair-holding employer firms, and national-body consensus - whose design hands several members an effective veto. And the committee's consensus procedure, layered over divergent commercial stakes, converts hard questions into wars of attrition that leave live standoffs unresolved on the books, so each open standoff is a latent defection. Knowledge that cannot be replaced, a coalition that cannot lose a member, and disputes that never fully close therefore compound into a single fragility. (low-medium)

The evidence for concentration is direct. Leadership clusters in a long-tenured founding and early cohort - Stroustrup from the language's creation, Herb Sutter, Michael Wong, and Daveed Vandevoorde from the 2000s - documented in [Stroustrup's history of C++ 2006-2020](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl20main-p5-p-bfc9cd4--final.pdf) (Rao and Argote 2006). The near-term cliff is concrete: the wind-down of Edison Design Group (EDG), with John Spicer stepping back, removes front-end expertise the ecosystem's tooling has long leaned on, recorded in the November 2025 Kona trip report and against Edison Design Group's company background. Replenishment is slow: the inner circle admits newcomers by invitation and at the convener's pleasure, and turnover is churny - a survival analysis of WG21 meeting attendance finds 47 percent of attendees appear once against a nine-to-ten-year half-life (Lave and Wenger 1991) - even as fresh guests keep arriving, with two dozen first-timers among roughly two hundred attendees logged in the March 2026 Croydon meeting minutes. Succession compounds the concentration: a real, telegraphed handover seated Guy Davidson as convener in January 2026 with Nina Ranns as vice-convener, yet Sutter retained the Foundation chair, the money, the infrastructure, and an author seat, so authority bifurcated rather than transferred, per the January 2026 convener handover announcement (Weber 1978). (low-medium)

Against this thinning bench the safety coalition is a minimum the committee cannot easily rebuild. The C++29 safety plan rests on Direction Group unanimity plus the convener plus chair-holding employers plus national-body consensus, and a single major defection - a large national-body bloc, or implementers declining to build Profiles - could collapse it (Riker 1962). Broad double-consensus coalitions are sturdier than minimum-winning ones, which holds this at the low end. The unorganized side has no comparable leverage: under existential memory-safety pressure the organized defenders are the incumbent chair-holding firms and the Standard C++ Foundation, while the some sixteen million developers who would benefit are unorganized and voiceless (Mayhew 1974) - though the corporate constituency does supply real defenders, so the body is only partly orphaned. (low)

The standoffs that keep the coalition brittle are visible in the record. Consensus governance over divergent stakes produced the seventeen-year Concepts voyage and the two-decade Contracts quest, documented in [the seventeen-year voyage of C++ Concepts](https://www.vinniefalco.com/p/the-17-year-voyage-c-concepts) and [the two-decade quest for C++ Contracts](https://www.vinniefalco.com/p/the-two-decade-quest-c-contracts) (Simcoe 2012), with delay tracking distributional conflict more than pure technical difficulty. The Safe-C++-versus-Profiles dispute settled for the core safety Profiles proposal for C++26 by channeling the Safe C++ program - carried by a member profiled in The Capture Spine - out rather than in, per the shift from Safe C++ to Profiles, while the frozen-ABI fight rolls forward. Some of this delay is the ordinary cost of a consensus body and reflects genuine care, which tempers the reading of every standoff as a strategic holdout. (medium)

**Who gains** from the arrangement are the incumbents: the long-tenured inner circle and the chair-holding firms retain agenda control and hold strong outside options, so concentration and coalition design protect their position even as the bench thins. Who pays are the parties with no seat and no exit - the broad developer base that inherits whatever the committee ships or fails to ship, and the safety agenda itself, which is the first casualty if a pivotal member walks. A lost founding-cohort expert also falls hardest on those who cannot fund a private replacement for the vanished knowledge. (low-medium)

The internal power map is a small set of pivots over a large dependent base. The convener brokers between the Direction Group and the subgroup chairs. The chair-holding employers and the Foundation supply the organized muscle. The fault lines run through the members whose single move ends the game. Two of those pivots are developed elsewhere: Google, the mobile firm whose strong-alternatives exit option the coalition cannot absorb, is profiled in The Frozen ABI, and the implementers whose cooperation Profiles requires - including the closing front-end vendor whose tacit knowledge is at issue - are profiled in The Route-Around and Borrowed Ground. National-body friction is already live: [a national-body paper contesting Contracts after adoption](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2026/p4043r0.html) shows Bloomberg and the national bodies in open, if still weak, conflict over shipped work - the kind of seam a larger bloc could widen into the defection the plan cannot survive. (low)

**The near-term trajectory** deteriorates: succession has already formalized into a split rather than a transfer, EDG's closure has materialized the founding-cohort cliff (softened only by a promised open-sourcing of the front end), and the pipeline and coalition are stable only in the sense that neither has yet broken. As EDG's expertise disperses and Spicer steps back over the C++29 cycle, a further high-value departure from the founding cohort is likely to strip an irreplaceable holder before replenishment catches up. Because those same principals anchor the safety coalition, such a loss would land directly on the plan. (low-medium)

No mechanism is dedicated to either exposure. Knowledge transfer is happening only through ordinary scaffolding - emeritus roles, editor succession, and the promised open-sourcing of the closing vendor's front end - which mitigates the concentration at the margin but is not a designed succession or replenishment program, and nothing in the committee's current structure insures the safety coalition against a single pivotal defection. Within its volunteer, consensus-bound form the committee could formalize deputy and successor tracks for the inner roles and widen standing invitation to the Direction Group, but no such mechanism exists today. (low-medium)

## 14. Other Findings

**Mission drift.** Senior members document organizational drift (Grimes et al. 2019) away from coherent design toward feature accretion and fashion, voiced in the watchword [Remember the Vasa!](https://open-std.org/JTC1/SC22/WG21/docs/papers/2018/p0977r0.pdf) and the admission in Direction for ISO C++ that the committee has "no shared taste." Both readings survive: some adaptation across more than three decades is expected and the core task of shipping the standard still holds, yet the recent trajectory is degrading, C++26 being the largest expansion since C++11 and Stroustrup objecting in [C++ Should Be C++](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2023/p3023r1.html) that the release is neither minimal nor viable (low).

**Reputational contagion.** Reputation-sensitive partners could distance themselves over documented associations (Jonsson, Greve and Fujiwara-Greve 2009): the CppCon "Individual X" sex-offender scandal recounted in [Patricia's personal statement](https://patricia.no/2022/03/08/cppcon.html), the Tomazos expulsion, and conduct disputes acute enough that some figures deactivated accounts, as noted in [a call for a WG21 managed chat service](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/2022/p2263r1.html). The countervailing reading holds - such disputes are endemic to large volunteer communities, documented conduct processes exist, and no sponsor-withdrawal cascade is evidenced - which bounds the exposure (medium-high).

**Institutional isomorphism.** WG21 converges on standard ISO committee form (DiMaggio and Powell 1983) through coercive pressure, the mandatory ISO/IEC JTC1 procedures, and normative professional pressure, even while remaining a deliberate outlier in scale that retains an ISO model many language peers reject. The pattern is largely benign, since conformance to the Directives is mandatory and is itself the source of the body's legitimacy, and it is stable as of 2026, the end of the joint INCITS meeting and independent Alliance-hosted meetings marking only slight divergence from prior US and INCITS coupling (low).
